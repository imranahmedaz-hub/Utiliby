What this free online email spam word checker does

Every day mailbox providers block or hide billions of unwanted messages, and sometimes they catch good email by mistake. Marketers, small businesses and anyone who writes bulk or cold email want to know whether their message looks like spam before it goes out. This free online email spam word checker reads your subject line and message and gives you a spam risk score from 0 to 100, a list of spam trigger words and phrases found in your text, and a set of structure checks for the things filters notice: capital letters, exclamation marks, too many links, link shorteners, links that disguise their real destination, mostly-image layouts, hidden text and a missing unsubscribe line.

Your text is highlighted by type of trigger, and a What to say instead tab gives calmer, more honest wording. You can paste plain text or full HTML, set the sensitivity and add your own words to flag. Nothing leaves your browser.

How spam filters actually decide

A spam filter is not a list of banned words. It is a scoring system that adds up many signals. The biggest signals are about the sender: is the domain authenticated with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, does the sending address have a good history, do recipients open and reply, and how many mark the message as spam. Next come technical signals such as the sending server and the links. Content comes last: wording, layout and the balance of text and images. A trusted sender can use words that would sink an unknown one. This is why the tool is best used as writing advice, together with proper authentication from the DMARC and SPF Record Generator and a clean list from the Email Validator and List Cleaner.

Context matters. A bank that really needs a customer to verify an account has to say so, and a real sale really can end today. The aim is not to avoid every word, but to avoid hype, pressure and vagueness, and to say true things plainly.

Structure and formatting signals

  • Capital letters: a high share of capitals looks like shouting.
  • Punctuation: several exclamation marks, or runs of !!! and ???, are penalised.
  • Links: too many links, link shorteners and bare IP addresses all lower trust.
  • Mismatched links: text that shows one address while the link goes to another looks like phishing.
  • Images: an email that is one big picture hides its wording from filters and readers who block images.
  • Hidden text: tiny or invisible keyword text is a well-known trick and is punished.
  • Unsubscribe: marketing messages without an opt-out are riskier and often illegal.
  • Attachments: executable files are blocked almost everywhere, and bulk attachments lower trust.

How to write email that avoids the spam folder

  • Be honest and specific. Say what you are offering and for whom.
  • Write to one person. Use a real sender name and, where you can, the reader's name.
  • Use plain link text. Read the guide beats Click here.
  • Keep it balanced. Real text, a few images, alt text on each.
  • Add an opt-out and your postal address.
  • Test your subject line. The Email Subject Line Generator checks length and wording for you.
  • Build clean HTML. The HTML Email Template Builder produces lightweight, table-based code.

If your emails already land in spam

Send a test message to a Gmail and an Outlook mailbox you control, and open the full headers in the Email Header Analyzer to see whether SPF, DKIM and DMARC passed and aligned. Check that your list contains only people who agreed to hear from you, remove bounces and long-term non-openers, and keep your complaint rate very low. Warm up new domains and send volume gradually. If you collect addresses from your own documents, extract and then validate them with the Email Extractor and the validator, and only email people who have a genuine relationship with you.

Honesty and the law

Avoiding spam filters is not about tricking them. It is about sending mail people want and presenting it honestly. In the United States, CAN-SPAM forbids deceptive headers and subject lines, and requires a postal address and a working opt-out. The EU and UK require a legal basis, usually consent, for marketing to individuals. Using this tool to disguise unwanted or deceptive mail is not its purpose and would not work against modern filters anyway.

Quick glossary

  • Trigger word: a word or phrase that appears much more often in spam than in normal mail.
  • Spam trap: an address that exists only to catch senders with poor list habits.
  • Sender reputation: a provider's record of how recipients treat your mail.
  • Complaint rate: the share of recipients who mark your mail as spam.
  • Bounce: a message that could not be delivered.
  • Double opt-in: asking new subscribers to confirm by clicking a link.
  • Warm-up: raising send volume slowly on a new domain or address.
  • Phishing: deceptive mail that tries to steal sign-in or payment details.

Accuracy, limits and responsible use

The phrase list is a general-purpose sample, not a copy of any provider's rules, and providers do not publish theirs. The score is a guide to wording and structure and cannot predict placement. Always test with real inboxes, and send only permission-based email.

Frequently asked questions

How do I use this free email spam word checker?

Paste your subject line and email body, and read the risk score, the list of trigger words, the structure checks and the safer wording ideas. Edit your text and the score updates at once.

Is this spam checker really free?

Yes. It is a free online tool with no sign-up and no limit. Your text stays in your browser.

What are spam trigger words?

They are words and phrases such as free money, act now, risk-free and you have won that appear far more often in spam than in normal mail. Filters use them as one signal among many.

Do spam words still matter?

Less than they used to. Modern filters weigh sender reputation, authentication, engagement and complaints more heavily. But hype wording can still add to a score and puts readers off, so it is worth removing.

Will my email go to spam if it contains the word free?

Not by itself. A single word rarely decides it. The tool gives common words a small weight and strong scam phrases a larger one, and adds up the total.

What is a good spam score?

In this tool, 0 to 14 is low risk, 15 to 34 is moderate, 35 to 59 is high and 60 or more is very high. The scale is a guide for writing, not a measure used by mail providers.

Does the tool check my domain or IP reputation?

No. It checks only the text. For authentication, use the DMARC and SPF Record Generator, and for message paths use the Email Header Analyzer.

What does the structure check look at?

The share of capital letters, exclamation marks, number of links, link shorteners, links that go somewhere other than they say, mostly-image emails, missing unsubscribe wording and similar signals.

Why are capital letters a problem?

All caps looks like shouting and is common in spam. Filters and readers both react badly to it. Use sentence case and let the content do the work.

Are link shorteners bad for email?

They are often used to hide where a link goes, so many filters distrust them. Use your full, branded domain in email links.

What is the image-to-text ratio?

Emails that are mostly pictures with little text can look like attempts to hide wording from filters. Aim for real text with supporting images, and add alt text.

Can I paste HTML?

Yes. The tool detects HTML, reads the visible text and also checks links, images and hidden text in the code.

Does an unsubscribe link help deliverability?

Yes. It is required by law for marketing email in many places and it gives unhappy readers a way out that is not the spam button, which protects your reputation.

What should I write instead of click here?

Use link text that says where the link goes, such as Read the full guide. It is better for readers, for accessibility and for filters.

Can I add my own words to flag?

Yes. Type extra words or phrases separated by commas in the extra words box and the tool will count them as triggers.

How can I improve deliverability beyond wording?

Authenticate your domain, send only to people who asked, remove bounces, keep complaint rates low and send content people want. Warm up new domains slowly.

Can I save or print the report?

Yes. Copy the report, download it as a text file or print or save a one-page report.
