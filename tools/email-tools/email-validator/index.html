What this free online email validator does

Every email list gets dirty. People mistype their address, sign up with a throwaway inbox, change jobs or sign up twice, and imported spreadsheets add stray commas, spaces and columns. Sending to a dirty list means bounces, a damaged sender reputation and, on many platforms, a bigger bill. This free online email validator and email list cleaner gives you a quick first pass in your browser, before you import a list into your email platform.

Paste addresses or upload a text or CSV file and the tool checks each one against the rules for a valid email address format, explains why an address is invalid, fixes common typos such as gmial.com, flags role-based addresses like info@ and disposable email domains, removes duplicates and produces a clean list you can copy or download, together with a full report that shows the reason for every removal.

How to clean an email list step by step

  1. Paste or upload your list. Any mix of lines, commas and semicolons works. CSV columns are fine when Ignore text without an @ sign is on.
  2. Choose your options. Lowercase, remove duplicates and fix typos are on by default. Turn on role-based removal for marketing lists.
  3. Read the summary. It shows how many addresses were kept, how many were invalid or duplicated and which domains are most common.
  4. Review the results. Filter by invalid, typo, role-based or disposable to see what was found.
  5. Copy or download the clean list. Import it into your email platform.
  6. Keep the report. The CSV report records why each address was removed.

What counts as a valid email address

An email address has a local part, an @ sign and a domain. The standards behind it (RFC 5321 and RFC 5322) allow many unusual forms, but real mail systems accept a narrower set, and this tool checks for the practical rules:

What a format check can and cannot tell you

A format check is the cheapest and fastest step, and it catches a surprising amount of bad data: typing slips, pasted text, missing parts and system placeholders. It cannot tell whether the mailbox exists, whether the person still uses it or whether the mail server will accept your message. Full verification services connect to the receiving mail server and test the mailbox, but even they cannot always be sure, because some servers accept all mail and sort it out later. The best protection is a layered approach: validate the format when someone types an address, ask them to confirm with a link (double opt-in), watch your bounce reports and remove addresses that bounce.

Role-based, disposable and free addresses

  • Role-based addresses (info@, admin@, sales@, support@, no-reply@) reach groups. They are fine for business contact but poor for newsletters, because nobody feels personally responsible for unsubscribing, and complaints go up. Some, such as abuse@ and postmaster@, are special system mailboxes.
  • Disposable addresses belong to temporary inbox services. People use them to download something without signing up for real. They stop working fast, so they inflate your list without adding readers.
  • Free provider addresses such as Gmail and Outlook are normal for consumers. For business lists they can signal a personal address rather than a company one, which you may want to treat differently.
  • Possible typos are valid in format but probably wrong, such as bob@gmial.com. Correcting them recovers real subscribers.

Why list hygiene protects deliverability

Mailbox providers watch how recipients treat your mail. Sending to invalid addresses creates hard bounces, and a high bounce rate tells providers that you do not look after your list. Old addresses can also turn into spam traps, mailboxes used to catch senders who buy or scrape lists. Clean data is only one part of good deliverability. You also need authentication, which you can set up with the DMARC and SPF Record Generator, content that does not look like spam, which you can test with the Email Spam Word Checker, and an honest consent process, such as a form built with the Newsletter Sign-up Form Generator. If your messages still land in spam, the Email Header Analyzer shows what the receiving server saw.

Good practice for collecting and keeping addresses

  • Validate the format on your sign-up form, and offer a gentle did-you-mean message for typos.
  • Use double opt-in so that only real, willing owners of an address join your list.
  • Never buy, rent or scrape lists.
  • Remove hard bounces straight away and unengaged contacts after a set period.
  • Keep a record of when and how each person agreed to hear from you.
  • Make unsubscribing easy and honour requests quickly.
  • Extract addresses from documents or web pages with the Email Extractor, then run them through this validator before you use them for permission-based contact.

Privacy and the law

Emailing people is regulated. In the United States, the CAN-SPAM Act sets rules for commercial email, including honest subject lines, a physical address and a working unsubscribe link. In the European Union and the United Kingdom, GDPR and the ePrivacy rules (PECR in the UK) generally require consent for marketing email to individuals. Other countries have similar laws. This tool is not legal advice. It processes your list in your browser only and does not send, store or share it.

Accuracy, limits and responsible use

The format rules follow the practical requirements of mail systems, and typo, role and disposable detection use built-in lists that are useful but not complete: new disposable services appear all the time, and a clever typo can be a real address. Treat the clean list as a good starting point, not a guarantee of delivery. This free online email validator is for lawful, permission-based use only.

Frequently asked questions

How do I use this free email validator?

Paste your addresses or upload a text or CSV file. The tool checks each one as you type, shows a summary, a results table and a clean list. Copy the clean list or download it as TXT or CSV.

Is this email validator really free?

Yes. It is a free online email validator and list cleaner with no sign-up, no credits and no limit on list size other than your browser's memory.

Does it check if an email address really exists?

No. A browser tool cannot contact mail servers. It checks that the format is valid, finds typos and flags role-based and disposable addresses. Only sending a message, ideally with a confirmation link, proves a mailbox exists.

What makes an email address invalid?

Common causes are a missing @ sign, more than one @, spaces, characters that are not allowed, two dots in a row, a domain without a dot, or an ending that is not a valid domain ending. The tool explains the reason for each invalid address.

What is a role-based email address?

A role-based address such as info@, support@, sales@ or admin@ goes to a team or function, not one person. They often have lower engagement and higher complaint rates for marketing email, so many senders remove them.

What is a disposable email address?

A disposable address comes from a temporary inbox service such as Mailinator or Guerrilla Mail. It stops working after a short time, so mail to it is wasted. The tool flags addresses from a built-in list of known services.

Can it fix typos like gmial.com?

Yes. When Fix typos is on, the tool corrects common domain mistakes such as gmial.com, hotmal.com and yahoo.con to the right provider, and tells you what it changed. Turn it off to just flag them.

How does the duplicate remover work?

Addresses are compared without regard to capital letters. Optionally, Gmail dots and plus tags are ignored, so j.doe+news@gmail.com and jdoe@gmail.com count as the same mailbox.

Is an email address case sensitive?

In theory the part before the @ can be case sensitive, but in practice almost every provider treats it as case insensitive, and the domain is always case insensitive. Lowercasing is the safe standard.

Can I clean a CSV file?

Yes. Upload the CSV or paste its contents. With Ignore text without an @ sign turned on, other columns are skipped and only the email addresses are checked.

How many emails can I check at once?

Tens of thousands in most browsers. The results table shows the first 300 rows to stay fast, but the clean list and downloads include every row.

Is my email list uploaded or stored?

No. Everything happens in your browser. The list is not sent to a server, saved or shared.

Why should I clean my email list?

Invalid and abandoned addresses cause bounces, which hurt your sender reputation and deliverability, and waste money on platforms that charge by list size. Cleaning before you import is a simple first step.

What is a good bounce rate?

Most email platforms want a hard bounce rate below about 2 percent and warn or suspend accounts that go much higher. Keeping lists clean and using double opt-in helps.

Should I remove free email addresses such as Gmail?

Not for consumer marketing, because most customers use them. For business-to-business lists you may want to prioritise company addresses. The tool shows free-provider counts but never removes them.

What is the difference between this and an email verifier service?

Verifier services connect to mail servers to test whether a mailbox exists, and they charge for it. This tool does everything that can be done safely in the browser, which catches a large share of bad data before you spend credits.

Can I print or export the results?

Yes. Download the clean list, download a full CSV report with the reason for each removal, or print or save a one-page summary.
