What this free scroll wheel test does

A scroll wheel that jumps, skips or scrolls the wrong way is annoying and easy to miss. The page leaps up while you scroll down, a list overshoots, or the wheel seems to ignore a notch. This scroll wheel test checks your mouse wheel in seconds. Turn the wheel over the test pad and the tool records every wheel event, counts scrolls up and down, shows the delta value and flags any single event that goes against the direction you were scrolling.

It also counts middle button presses and sideways tilt, measures your fastest burst of events and draws a live chart of the last 60 events. You can copy the results, download them as a CSV file or print a summary for a repair or warranty claim. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded.

How to use the test

  1. Put the pointer on the pad and turn the wheel steadily downward, one notch at a time, for about 10 notches.
  2. Turn it the other way for another 10 notches. Keep the speed even and avoid changing direction on purpose in the middle.
  3. Try a fast spin for a few seconds. Jumps often appear only at speed.
  4. Check the chart. Green bars go up, blue bars go down, and red bars are flagged. All bars should sit on one side during a steady scroll.
  5. Read the verdict, then press Reset to run it again after a fix.

How a mouse scroll wheel works

Inside the mouse, the wheel turns a small part called an encoder, which sends pulses as it rotates. Two signals, slightly offset in time, tell the mouse which way the wheel is turning. This is called a quadrature or rotary encoder. Most mice use a mechanical encoder with metal contacts, while some use optical or magnetic sensing. The mouse sends the result to the computer, and your browser reports it as a wheel event with a delta value for each step.

One notch on a classic wheel often reports a delta of around 100 pixels in Chromium browsers on Windows, or about 3 lines in Firefox. The exact number depends on the browser, the operating system, display scaling and mouse settings. What matters for the test is the direction: during a steady scroll, every event should point the same way.

What scroll jumping looks like

  • The page jumps back. You scroll down and the page briefly goes up, then continues down.
  • Skipped or extra notches. The page moves more or less than the number of clicks you felt.
  • Wrong direction. Scrolling down moves the page up, which can be a setting such as natural scrolling instead of a fault.
  • Random scrolling. The page scrolls by itself with no input, which suggests a dirty or failing encoder.

Why a scroll wheel misbehaves

  • Dirty or worn encoder. Dust, hair and skin oil build up on the contacts, and the signals become noisy.
  • Weak ratchet spring. The notches feel mushy and the wheel can rest between steps.
  • Failing contacts. Contacts wear out over years of use and cause missed or reversed pulses.
  • Software. Smooth scrolling, a browser extension or a mouse driver setting can alter wheel behaviour even when the hardware is fine.
  • Cable, dongle or port. A poor connection can drop signals, so try another port or a wired mouse.

Up and down counts are far apartExpected if you scrolled further one way. Compare to what you did.

The tool flags an event only when it points against the direction of the events before and after it, all within a short time. That avoids flagging a deliberate change of direction, and it keeps false alarms low.

How to fix a scroll wheel that jumps

  1. Rule out software. Turn off smooth scrolling in your browser, disable scroll extensions and test in another browser.
  2. Try another port or computer. If the problem moves with the mouse, the hardware is at fault.
  3. Clean around the wheel. Unplug the mouse, turn it over and use compressed air around the wheel, spinning it slowly while you blow.
  4. Deeper clean. Some people apply a little isopropyl alcohol with a cotton swab to the encoder area, then let it dry fully. This can void the warranty, so only do it if the mouse is out of warranty.
  5. Replace the encoder. The encoder is a cheap part in many mice and can be replaced with basic soldering skills.
  6. Claim warranty or replace the mouse. A wheel that jumps inside the warranty period is a defect. Keep the results from this test as evidence.

Scroll settings that are not faults

Several settings change how the wheel feels without any fault. Windows lets you choose how many lines one notch scrolls, three by default, in the mouse settings. Natural scrolling, common on macOS and some touchpads, reverses the direction so content follows your finger. Smooth scrolling in browsers animates movement and can break one notch into many small events. Mouse software can add acceleration or a scroll speed. If something looks wrong, check these before blaming the mouse.

Accuracy and privacy

The test uses the wheel events your browser reports. Different browsers and systems report different delta values, and a touchpad produces many small events with inertia, so this test is meant for a mouse wheel. The tool measures direction and timing, not how a notch feels. It does not upload your scrolling and stores nothing. It cannot see events outside the test pad, and while your pointer is on the pad the page itself will not scroll.

Frequently asked questions

How do I test my mouse scroll wheel?

Put the pointer on the test pad and turn the wheel steadily one way for about 10 notches, then the other way. Watch the chart and verdict. A healthy wheel shows all bars on one side during each steady scroll.

What is scroll jumping?

Scroll jumping is when the page briefly moves the wrong way, or by the wrong amount, while you scroll steadily. It is usually caused by a dirty or worn scroll wheel encoder inside the mouse.

Why does my page jump up when I scroll down?

A failing encoder can send a stray signal in the opposite direction. It can also be smooth scrolling or a browser extension, so test in another browser and on another port before opening the mouse.

What does a flagged event mean?

A flagged event is a single wheel event pointing the opposite way from the events right before and after it, all within a short time. It is a strong sign of scroll jumping rather than a deliberate change of direction.

How many notches should I test?

At least 20 notches in total, 10 each way. For intermittent faults, add a few seconds of fast spinning, because jumps often appear only at speed.

How do I fix a scroll wheel that jumps?

Turn off smooth scrolling and extensions first. Then try another port, clean around the wheel with compressed air, and if needed replace the encoder, claim the warranty or buy a new mouse.

Can I clean a mouse scroll wheel?

Yes. Unplug the mouse and use compressed air around the wheel while turning it slowly. A little isopropyl alcohol on a cotton swab near the encoder may help, but it can void the warranty, so let it dry fully and use it only on out-of-warranty mice.

What does the delta value mean?

The delta value is the size of each wheel step reported by your browser. Positive means scrolling down and negative means scrolling up. Its size depends on your browser, operating system and settings.

What is the delta mode?

The delta mode tells you the unit of the delta: pixels, lines or pages. Many browsers report pixels, while some report lines for a classic wheel. The test shows the mode of your last event.

Why do I see many small events for one notch?

Smooth scrolling, a high-resolution wheel or a touchpad can split one step into many small events. That is not a fault. The test notes when your events look like a touchpad.

Does the test work on a touchpad?

It records touchpad scrolling too, but a touchpad creates many tiny events with inertia, so the jump detector is meant for mouse wheels. Use a mouse for a reliable result.

How many lines does one notch scroll?

On Windows, the default is three lines per notch, which you can change in the mouse settings. Browsers and apps may also change the scroll amount, and smooth scrolling changes how it looks.

Does the test check the middle button?

Yes. Middle clicks on the pad are counted, and the middle click is not allowed to start autoscroll while the pointer is on the pad.

Why does the page not scroll when my pointer is on the pad?

The pad captures the wheel on purpose so the page does not move while you test. Move the pointer off the pad to scroll the page again.

What is the difference between a ratchet and a free-spin wheel?

A ratchet wheel clicks at each notch, and a free-spin wheel turns smoothly. Both report direction the same way, so the test works for both, though a free-spin wheel produces many events per turn.

Can the browser cause scroll jumping?

Yes. Smooth scrolling, extensions and some page scripts can change how wheel events are used. If the test finds no jumps but one site misbehaves, the problem is likely in the site or browser.

Does the test upload my scrolling?

No. The test runs entirely in your browser. No wheel data is sent anywhere, and it is cleared when you press Reset or close the page.

Does the test count sideways tilt?

Yes. Events with a horizontal delta are counted separately, so you can see whether your tilt wheel or a horizontal scroll gesture reaches the browser.
