The eight moon phases explained

The lunar cycle passes through eight named phases. The four primary phases are the anchor points:

  • New Moon — the Moon is between Earth and the Sun, so the side facing Earth is dark.
  • First Quarter — the right half of the Moon (in the Northern Hemisphere) is illuminated.
  • Full Moon — the entire face of the Moon is illuminated.
  • Last Quarter — the left half is illuminated.

The four intermediate phases are the Waxing Crescent, Waxing Gibbous, Waning Gibbous and Waning Crescent. "Waxing" means growing; "waning" means shrinking.

Understanding the orbit diagram

The orbit diagram above shows a top-down view of the Earth–Moon–Sun system, as seen from above the North Pole. The Sun is far to the left. Earth is in the center. The Moon travels counterclockwise around Earth over about 29.5 days.

Half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun — the half facing the Sun. What we see from Earth depends on where the Moon is in its orbit:

  • New Moon: the Moon sits between Earth and Sun, so the lit side faces away from us. We see nothing.
  • First Quarter: the Moon is a quarter of the way around. We see half of the lit side.
  • Full Moon: the Moon is on the opposite side of Earth from the Sun, so the lit side faces us completely.
  • Last Quarter: the Moon is three quarters around, and we see the other half of the lit side.

This is why the phase changes gradually over a month rather than all at once.

Moon age, illumination and distance

  • Moon age — days since the most recent new moon (0 to about 29.5).
  • Illumination — percentage of the visible disc that is lit (0% at new moon, 100% at full moon).
  • Moon–Earth distance — varies from about 356,500 km at perigee to 406,700 km at apogee, averaging 384,400 km.

Supermoon, micromoon and blue moon

Supermoon: a full moon occurring near perigee — the Moon looks up to 14% larger and 30% brighter.

Micromoon: a full moon occurring near apogee — the Moon appears smaller and dimmer than usual.

Blue moon: traditionally the second full moon within a single calendar month, roughly every 2–3 years.

Practical uses for moon phase tracking

Astrophotography and stargazing

New moon nights are best for deep-sky astrophotography because the Moon is invisible and does not wash out faint stars. Full moon nights are best for observing the Moon's own surface features.

Fishing and hunting

Traditional wisdom ties fishing and hunting success to lunar phase. The two days around new and full moons are often described as peak feeding times.

Gardening and biodynamic planting

Traditional gardeners plant above-ground crops during the waxing moon and root crops during the waning moon.

Photography and events

Planning a wedding, photoshoot or night hike? Check the phase to know whether to expect moonlight or darkness.

Frequently asked questions

What is a moon phase calculator?

A moon phase calculator is a tool that tells you the phase of the Moon, its illuminated fraction and its age in days for any given date.

How many moon phases are there?

There are four primary phases (new, first quarter, full, last quarter) and four intermediate phases (waxing crescent, waxing gibbous, waning gibbous, waning crescent).

What is moon age?

Moon age is the number of days since the last new moon, ranging from 0 to about 29.5 days.

What is a supermoon?

A supermoon is a full moon near perigee, making the Moon appear up to 14% larger and 30% brighter.

Why does the moon have different phases?

The Moon reflects sunlight. As it orbits Earth, different fractions of the lit side face us, producing the phases.
