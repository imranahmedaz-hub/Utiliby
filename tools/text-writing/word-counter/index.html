What this free word counter does

Whether you are trimming an essay to a limit, checking a caption before you post or briefing a writer, you need to know how long a piece of text is. This free online word counter and character counter gives you the answer as you type. Paste your text and it reports words, characters with and without spaces, sentences, paragraphs and lines, plus the number of unique words, the average word and sentence length and the longest word.

Counting is only the start. The same page estimates the reading time, speaking time and handwriting time of your text, shows the words and phrases you repeat most with their keyword density, measures progress against a word goal and against the character limits of popular platforms, and scores readability with five standard formulas. A live chart shows which letters you use most and how long each sentence is, so you can see where a draft drags.

Everything runs in your browser. Your text is never uploaded, stored or shared, which matters for drafts, client work and anything confidential.

How to use the word counter

  1. Type or paste your text into the box. You can also open a plain text file from your device, or try the sample.
  2. Read the live totals above the tabs: words, characters, sentences, paragraphs, reading time and speaking time.
  3. Open the Counts tab for the full breakdown, and change the reading, speaking or handwriting speed to suit your audience.

  6. Review readability on the fourth tab to see the reading ease score, the grade level and any sentences that run past 25 words.
  7. Copy, download or print the report. The printout holds only the report, not the page.

How words, characters, sentences and paragraphs are counted

Different tools disagree by a few words because they define a word differently. This one follows the rules most writers expect. A word is a run of letters or digits. Apostrophes and hyphens inside a run keep it together, so it's and well-known count once, and a number with separators such as 1,200 or 3.50 counts once. Spaces, line breaks, dashes between words and most punctuation separate words. The approach follows the idea of word boundaries in the Unicode text segmentation standard, simplified for everyday use.

Characters are counted as Unicode characters, so an accented letter or a symbol counts as one. Characters with spaces include spaces and line breaks. Characters without spaces leave them out. Sentences end at a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark followed by a space or the end of the text. Abbreviations such as Dr., e.g. and p.m. and decimals such as 3.5 are recognised so they do not split a sentence. A line with no end punctuation, such as a heading, counts as its own sentence. Paragraphs are blocks of text separated by a blank line.

Reading time, speaking time and handwriting time

Reading time is the word count divided by a words-per-minute speed. The default of 238 words per minute reflects a large review of reading research, which found that adults read non-fiction silently at about that pace and fiction a little faster. Speed varies with difficulty, purpose and the reader, so the figure is best read as a guide. Blog platforms use a similar calculation to show a read-time estimate on articles.

Speaking time works the same way with a lower speed. A relaxed presentation runs at about 130 words per minute, conversation is faster, and a slow, careful delivery or a talk with pauses and slides runs slower. A 1,000-word script therefore takes about eight minutes to deliver. Handwriting is slower still, at roughly 13 words per minute for most adults, which explains why a handwritten page takes so long. Change any of the three speeds to match your situation.

Keyword density without the myths

The keyword tab lists the words and phrases you repeat most and what share of the text each makes up. It ignores common words such as the and of by default, because they would otherwise fill the table. It is useful for two jobs. The first is editing: a draft that uses the same adjective eleven times needs a thesaurus. The second is checking that a page about a topic actually mentions the terms a reader would look for.

Treat any density target with caution. Google has said for years that there is no ideal keyword density and that it looks at the meaning of the page as a whole. Repeating a phrase to hit a number makes the text worse to read and does not help. A better approach is to cover the topic completely, use the natural variations people search for, and use the keyword table to spot accidental repetition rather than to chase a figure. Two- and three-word phrases are the most useful view, since they show the topics a text is really about.

Word goals and platform character limits

Many writing jobs come with a limit: 650 words for a college application essay, 250 for an abstract, 160 characters for an SMS, around 60 for a page title and 155 to 160 for a meta description. The goal tab tracks your count against a target of your choice with a progress ring, and shows bars for the typical limits of popular platforms so you can see at a glance what is left or over.

Readability scores explained

The readability tab applies five well-known formulas. Flesch Reading Ease rewards short sentences and short words and gives a score from 0 to 100, where 60 to 70 is plain English that most adults follow easily. Flesch-Kincaid grade turns the same inputs into a US school grade. The Gunning Fog index and SMOG focus on long words of three or more syllables. The Coleman-Liau index and the Automated Readability Index use letters per word rather than syllables, which makes them less sensitive to syllable-counting errors.

Each formula is a rough guide. They cannot judge logic, tone or vocabulary, and they ignore layout. Specialist writing can be perfectly clear to its audience and still score as difficult. What they do well is flag the common causes of heavy prose, which are long sentences and long words. The sentence chart marks each sentence in green, amber or red by length, so you can find the ones to split. Public writing guidance recommends plain language for general audiences, and a grade level between 7 and 9 suits most web content.

Tips for writers, students and marketers

  • Count before you polish. Check the length early so you do not spend an hour tightening a section you will cut.
  • Write to the limit, then trim. Aim slightly above the target, then cut weak words, repeated phrases and filler openings.
  • Use the paragraph and sentence counts. Walls of text and sentences above 25 words are the most common readability problems.
  • Check repeated phrases. If your keyword table is dominated by one phrase, vary the wording.
  • Time your talk. Divide your word count by 130 and add time for slides and questions.
  • Check the character count for adverts and meta tags before you paste into the platform, where text may be cut without warning.
  • Keep the official count for submissions. Use this tool to get close, then confirm in the submission portal.

Common mistakes when counting words

  • Counting the title, references or captions by accident. Decide whether the limit includes them and paste only what counts.
  • Trusting one number. Tools differ by a few words. Leave a small margin under a strict limit.
  • Mixing up characters with and without spaces. Social platforms and meta tags count spaces.
  • Chasing keyword density. A natural, complete answer beats a target percentage.
  • Applying English readability formulas to other languages. The formulas were built for English text.
  • Treating reading time as exact. It is an average, and your reader may be faster or slower.

Frequently asked questions

How does this word counter count words?

It counts every run of letters or digits as one word. Hyphenated words such as well-known, contractions such as it's and numbers such as 1,200 each count once. Spaces, line breaks and punctuation separate words.

Is the word count the same as in Microsoft Word or Google Docs?

Usually it is within a word or two, but not always. Word processors treat some symbols, dashes and numbers differently. If an exam portal or publisher sets a limit, treat their count as the official one and use this tool as a close guide.

What is the difference between characters with and without spaces?

Characters with spaces counts every character including spaces and line breaks. Characters without spaces leaves out spaces, tabs and line breaks. Social platforms and meta descriptions usually count with spaces.

How many words is one page?

A page of single-spaced 12-point text holds roughly 500 words and a double-spaced page roughly 250. The exact figure depends on font, margins and spacing. The words-to-pages calculator on this site works it out for your settings.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time is the word count divided by a reading speed. The default is 238 words per minute, a widely cited average for silent reading of non-fiction by adults. You can change the speed to match your audience.

How is speaking time calculated?

Speaking time divides the word count by a speaking speed, 130 words per minute by default, which suits a relaxed presentation. Fast talkers reach 160 or more. Change the speed to match your own pace.

What is keyword density and what is a good value?

Keyword density is how often a word or phrase appears as a share of all words. Google does not publish an ideal figure. Write naturally, and treat a single term above about five percent as a prompt to check for repetition.

Does the tool count emojis and special characters?

Emojis and symbols count as one character each in the character total. Some platforms weigh them differently: X, for example, counts most emojis as two and every link as 23 characters, so check the platform's own rules.

Can I check the character limit for X, Instagram or LinkedIn?

Yes. The goal and limits tab shows progress bars for typical limits including X, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, page titles, meta descriptions, SMS and Google Ads, and shows how many characters are left or over.

What do the readability scores mean?

Flesch Reading Ease runs from 0 (very hard) to 100 (very easy), and about 60 to 70 is plain English. The grade-level scores estimate the US school grade needed to follow the text. They are guides, not verdicts.

Why are the readability scores blank or unreliable for short text?

The formulas use averages across sentences and words, so a sentence or two gives a poor estimate. Aim for at least a hundred words and three sentences before you rely on the scores.

Can it count words in other languages?

It counts words in any language that separates words with spaces, including accented and non-Latin scripts. Languages written without spaces, such as Chinese and Japanese, do not split into words, so use the character count there. Syllable and readability formulas are designed for English.

Can I upload a file?

You can open a plain text file from your device and the tool reads it inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded to a server. Copy and paste text from Word or PDF files if you need to count those.

Is my text stored or sent anywhere?

No. All counting runs in your browser, and nothing is saved or transmitted. Closing or refreshing the page clears the text, so save your work first if you need it.

Is there a limit on text length?

There is no fixed limit. Very long documents of several hundred thousand words may update more slowly on older phones, because the tool recalculates as you type.

How do I print or save only the report?

Use the print or save button. The printout contains the word count report, keyword table and readability section only, not the page layout or article. You can also copy the summary or download a CSV.

Is this word counter free?

Yes. It is free to use online, with no sign-up and no limit on use.
