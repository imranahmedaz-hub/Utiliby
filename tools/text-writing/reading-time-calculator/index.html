What this free reading time calculator does

How long will this article take to read? How many words do I need for a ten-minute talk? How long is an 80,000-word novel as an audiobook? This free online reading time calculator answers all three. Paste text and it estimates the reading time and speaking time at once. Switch to the second tab to turn words into minutes or minutes into words. Use the third tab to plan a speech, with allowance for pauses, question time and slides.

Unlike simple calculators that use a single fixed speed, this one gives you thirteen speed presets, from careful study and proofreading to skimming, reading aloud and audiobook narration, plus a custom setting for your own pace. It adds time for images, draws a clock and a bar chart comparing speeds, and builds a quick reference table for common word counts. Everything runs in your browser.

How to use the reading time calculator

  1. Paste your text on the first tab, or press Try a sample.
  2. Pick a reading speed that matches your reader, such as average adult or skimming.
  3. Pick a speaking speed if you plan to read the text aloud.
  4. Add the number of images to include viewing time.
  5. Read the result and the comparison chart, which shows the same text at other speeds.
  6. Use the second tab to convert a word count to time, or a time to a word count, without any text.
  7. Use the third tab to plan a talk from its time slot.

How fast do people read?

The most cited figure for adults reading non-fiction silently is about 238 words per minute, from a large review of reading research published in 2019. Fiction is read a little faster, near 260. Students reading for study go slower, often 150 to 200 words per minute, and proofreading is slower again because the reader checks each word. Skimmers move at 400 words per minute or more, but they take in less.

Planning a speech or presentation

Speakers talk more slowly than people read. Most speeches are delivered at 130 to 150 words per minute, and a conversation or podcast runs a little faster. If you write a speech to the full reading speed of 238 words per minute, you will overrun. The planner on the third tab starts from your time slot, subtracts question time, applies your pace and then allows for pauses and ad-libs. The result is the number of words to write, split into a short introduction, a main body and a conclusion.

A useful rule is to leave about 10 percent of the time for pauses. Pauses give the audience time to take in a point, and they also give you room if you stumble. Rehearse aloud with a timer. If you finish early or late, adjust the word count rather than rushing.

Slides and pacing

A common guide is one slide every one to two minutes, so a ten-minute talk needs five to ten slides. Slide-heavy decks with a slide every 20 seconds feel rushed. The planner converts your seconds per slide into a slide count. Keep text on slides short, because the audience reads faster than you speak and will stop listening while they read.

Reading time labels for blogs and articles

Many publishers show a line such as 6 min read at the top of a post. It sets expectations, and some studies suggest it helps readers decide whether to start. Readers also use it to judge whether to save an article for later. The label is usually the word count divided by a speed between 200 and 250, rounded up to a whole minute. The tool shows that label for your text. Include extra time if your post has many images, charts or code samples, because readers slow down for them.

Audiobooks and narration

Professional narrators read at about 150 to 160 words per minute, with pauses between paragraphs and chapters. A 60,000-word book therefore runs for about six and a half hours and an 80,000-word novel for just under nine. Listeners often play audiobooks at 1.25 times speed or faster, which shortens those times by a fifth or more. The words and time tab lets you change the speed to model this.

Words to minutes and minutes to words

The second tab does both conversions. Enter a word count and a speed to get a time, or enter minutes and a speed to get the number of words. It also shows page counts for single-spaced and double-spaced text and a reference table of common word counts, such as 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 5,000 words, at silent, spoken and skimming speeds.

How images are counted

Images slow readers down. Many blogging platforms add time using a simple schedule: 12 seconds for the first image, 11 for the second, and so on, down to a minimum of 3 seconds for every image after the tenth. The calculator uses the same method. Ten images add about 75 seconds. It is an estimate for photos and illustrations. Dense charts and diagrams can take much longer.

Tips for better estimates

  • Use a speed that matches the reader. Technical readers move more slowly than general readers.
  • Time yourself reading a page to find your own speed, then use the custom setting.
  • Allow extra time for tables, code and equations.
  • For speeches, rehearse aloud with a timer and adjust the word count.
  • Round reading time labels up, not down.
  • Do not treat any figure as a promise. Individual readers vary a lot.

Common mistakes

  • Using reading speed for a speech. People talk much more slowly than they read silently.
  • Forgetting pauses. Breaths, pauses and audience reactions use time.
  • Counting the wrong text. Include or exclude headings, captions and references on purpose.
  • Assuming speed reading helps. Comprehension falls as speed rises beyond roughly 400 to 600 words per minute.
  • Believing an average applies to everyone. Treat it as a starting point.

Limits and privacy

The tool estimates time from word counts and average speeds. It does not measure comprehension, difficulty or language. Languages differ in word length, so word-based figures are less reliable outside English. Your text is processed in your browser and is not uploaded or stored.

Frequently asked questions

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time is the number of words divided by the reader's speed in words per minute. At the common average of 238 words per minute, 1,000 words take about four minutes.

What is the average reading speed?

Research on adult silent reading of non-fiction gives an average of about 238 words per minute. Fiction is a little faster, and difficult or technical material is slower.

How long does it take to read 1,000 words?

About four minutes at 238 words per minute, around six and a half minutes aloud at 150, and just over two minutes when skimming at 450.

How long does it take to read 500, 2,000 or 5,000 words?

At 238 words per minute, 500 words take about two minutes, 2,000 words about eight and a half minutes and 5,000 words about 21 minutes. The quick reference table on the second tab lists more.

What is the average speaking speed for a speech?

Most speakers talk at 130 to 170 words per minute. Presentations are usually delivered near the slower end, about 130, because speakers pause and use slides.

How many words do I need for a 10-minute speech?

About 1,300 words at 130 words per minute, or about 1,500 at 150. The speech planner on the third tab also allows for pauses and question time.

How do I calculate the words for a five-minute speech?

Multiply the minutes by the pace. At 130 words per minute a five-minute speech needs about 650 words. Allow for pauses and ad-libs if you want to finish on time.

How does the calculator treat images?

It uses the method many blogs follow: 12 seconds for the first image, 11 for the second and so on down to a minimum of 3 seconds for each later image.

How long does an audiobook take to narrate?

Narrators read at about 150 to 160 words per minute. A 80,000-word novel runs for roughly eight to nine hours.

Why does my reading speed differ from the average?

Speed depends on practice, vocabulary, the topic, the font and screen, the purpose of reading and fatigue. Average speeds are only a guide, and your own pace is the best figure to use.

Is faster reading always better?

No. Skimming is fast but comprehension drops. Studies of speed reading find that readers lose understanding as they pass about 400 to 600 words per minute.

How do I set my own reading speed?

Choose Custom speed, then enter your words per minute. To find yours, time a page of text you have not read before and divide its word count by the minutes.

What is a good reading time label for a blog post?

Many sites round up to whole minutes, such as 5 min read, using a speed near 200 to 240 words per minute. The tool shows that rounded label under the result.

Can it plan how many slides I need?

Yes. Enter the seconds you spend on each slide and the planner shows the slide count for your talking time.

How do I print or save only the results?

Use the print or save button. The printout has the results tables and chart for the tab you are on, not the page layout or the article. You can also copy the results or download them as a CSV file.

Is my text uploaded?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing is sent to a server or saved.

Is this reading time calculator free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up and no limit.
