What this free online vendor comparison tool does

Choosing a vendor is one of the most expensive decisions a business makes, and one of the easiest to get wrong. The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest supplier once late deliveries, defects, support and switching costs are counted, and the loudest voice in the meeting is rarely the best judge. A vendor comparison matrix, also called a supplier evaluation matrix or weighted decision matrix, gives the decision a structure everyone can see.

This free online vendor comparison tool lets you define your criteria and weights, score each vendor from 0 to 10, add costs and see a ranked answer straight away. It calculates total cost of ownership and scores price for you, supports pass or fail must-haves, draws a stacked chart that shows where each vendor wins and loses, and runs a robustness test that tells you whether the winner would change if the weights moved. Templates for software, suppliers, agencies and logistics get you started in seconds.

How to use this vendor comparison tool

  1. Pick a template or write your own criteria, such as quality, delivery, support and risk.
  2. Set the weights. They are rescaled to 100%, so you can use any numbers.
  3. Add your vendors and enter their one-time cost, yearly cost and contract length.
  4. Mark must-haves. Untick the box for any vendor that fails a non-negotiable requirement.
  5. Score each vendor from 0 to 10 on the Scores tab.
  6. Open Results for the ranking, chart, cost table and robustness test, then copy, download or print.

How weighted scoring works

Weighted score = sum of (weight / total weight) x score across all criteria. Dividing by the total weight keeps the result on the same 0 to 10 scale as your scores, whatever numbers you used for the weights.

For cost, the tool uses total cost = one-time cost + yearly cost x years. The cheapest vendor receives 10 points and others receive 10 x cheapest total / their total. A vendor that costs twice as much scores 5.

These ranges are starting points. A hospital buying a medical supplier would weight compliance much higher than a cafe buying napkins.

Why total cost of ownership matters

Quotes show the price, not the cost. Over a contract you also pay for set-up, integration, training, maintenance, shipping, returns, downtime and the staff time spent managing the relationship. A supplier that is 10% cheaper per unit but ships late can cost far more in lost sales. Entering one-time and yearly costs, and the contract length, lets the tool compare vendors on a like-for-like basis.

A simple vendor selection process

  • Define needs first. Write requirements before you talk to vendors.
  • Shortlist three to five. Use referrals, reviews and a request for information.
  • Request proposals that follow a common format.
  • Score independently, then compare and discuss differences.
  • Check references and run a pilot for the top one or two.
  • Negotiate and document service levels, exit terms and penalties.

Avoiding bias in vendor scoring

  • Fix the weights before scoring.
  • Define what a low, middle and high score mean.
  • Use several scorers and average them.
  • Do not let a good demo overrule the evidence on reliability.
  • Record why you chose, so the decision can be reviewed later.

Vendor comparison for different purchases

The method is the same for every purchase, but the criteria change. When buying software, weigh features, integrations, security, scalability and the quality of support, and watch for lock-in and exit costs. When choosing a product supplier, quality, delivery reliability, lead time, minimum order quantities and financial stability matter most. For an agency or contractor, relevant experience, references, communication and timeline weigh heavily. For logistics, on-time delivery, coverage, tracking and claims handling decide the outcome. The templates in the tool reflect these differences, and you can change every name and weight.

From shortlist to contract: where the matrix fits

A weighted matrix is most useful after you have a shortlist and comparable proposals. Start with a request for information to filter out unsuitable vendors, send a request for proposal to the shortlist with the same questions and format, and score the responses. Then check references, run a pilot or sample order for the top candidates and negotiate. The matrix gives a record of why you chose, which helps with internal approval, audits and any later dispute with the vendor.

  • Document the weights and who agreed them.
  • Keep the scoring notes that justify each score.
  • Re-score after demos, references and negotiation, since the facts change.

Vendor risk and what scores can miss

Scores capture how well a vendor fits today, not every risk. Look separately at concentration risk, which arises when one vendor supplies too much of your business, at financial health, at data protection and at the contract terms that govern termination, service levels and liability. Use the must-have gate for items that are non-negotiable, such as a required certification, and use criteria for everything that is a trade-off. If the robustness test shows the winner is sensitive to weights, treat the decision as close and look for extra evidence.

Frequently asked questions

What is a vendor comparison tool?

A tool that scores several suppliers against the same criteria, applies weights for importance and ranks them. It replaces gut feel with a clear, shareable result.

What is a weighted decision matrix?

A table with criteria as rows and options as columns. Each score is multiplied by its criterion weight and the products are added up to give a total per option.

How many criteria should I use?

Between four and eight is usually enough. Too few misses important factors and too many makes every weight small and the scoring tedious.

How should I choose the weights?

Agree them before looking at any vendor, with the people affected. Give the biggest weights to what would hurt most if it went wrong, not to what is easiest to measure.

Do weights need to add up to 100?

No. The tool rescales them to 100%, so you can use any numbers.

How does the tool score cost?

It calculates total cost of ownership as one-time cost plus yearly cost times years, then gives the cheapest vendor 10 points and scales the others in proportion.

What is total cost of ownership?

The full cost of a purchase over its life, including set-up, licences, maintenance, training, shipping and the cost of problems, not just the quote.

What does 'meets must-haves' mean?

It is a pass or fail gate for non-negotiable requirements, such as a certification or a data-security standard. A vendor that fails is shown as excluded no matter its score.

What does the robustness test show?

It removes each criterion in turn and then doubles it to see whether the winner changes. If it does, your result depends heavily on weights and deserves a second look.

Is a high score a guarantee of a good vendor?

No. It is only as good as the information you enter. Verify references, financial health and contract terms.

Can I compare more than three vendors?

Yes, up to ten vendors and twelve criteria. Most teams shortlist three to five before scoring in detail.

How should I score subjective criteria like support?

Define what a 3, 6 and 9 mean before scoring, use references and trial periods, and have more than one person score and average.

What should I do if two vendors tie?

Add a criterion that separates them, run a pilot or negotiate on price. A margin under 0.3 points is a close call.

Can I use this for software, suppliers and contractors?

Yes. The templates cover software, suppliers, agencies and logistics, and you can edit every name and weight.

How do I share the result?

Use Copy result for a text summary, Download table for a CSV file, or Print to save a clean PDF report.

Why should I involve other people?

Different teams notice different risks. Scoring independently and then comparing results reduces bias and helps everyone support the final choice.

Is my data stored?

No. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is sent or saved.
