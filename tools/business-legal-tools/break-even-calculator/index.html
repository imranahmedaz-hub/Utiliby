What this free online break-even calculator does

Every business owner eventually asks the same question: how much do I have to sell before I stop losing money? The answer is the break-even point, and it is one of the most useful numbers in small business finance. It tells you whether a price makes sense, how risky a new product is, how many customers a subscription needs, and how much a new hire really costs in extra sales.

This free online break-even calculator finds that point from three numbers: your fixed costs, your price per unit and your variable cost per unit. It then goes further than a simple formula. It draws a live chart with the profit and loss zones, your expected sales and the exact crossover, and it works out the contribution margin, margin of safety, operating leverage, the units needed for a target profit, the price you would need at your expected volume, and how long a startup investment takes to pay back.

A second tab shows how break-even moves when price or cost changes, and a third handles several products with one set of fixed costs. You can print or save a one-page report that contains only your result, copy it as text or download the chart data as a spreadsheet file. Everything runs privately in your browser.

How to use this break-even analysis calculator

  1. Pick a preset if you want typical numbers for a restaurant, online shop, software business, agency, retailer or manufacturer. You can edit every figure.
  2. Enter fixed costs for one period, such as a month, and choose the period.
  3. Enter the price and the variable cost of one unit. Their difference is the contribution margin.
  4. Add your expected units for the same period to see the margin of safety and profit.
  5. Set an optional target profit, tax rate and startup investment.
  6. Read the result and the chart. The dot is break-even, the green area is profit and the red area is loss.
  7. Use the other tabs to test price changes or a product mix.

The break-even formulas

Break-even units = fixed costs / (price - variable cost per unit). The bottom of the fraction is the contribution margin, the amount each sale contributes towards fixed costs and then profit.

Break-even revenue = fixed costs / contribution margin ratio, where the ratio is the margin divided by the price. It gives the same answer as units multiplied by price, and it is handy when you sell many products.

Units for a target profit = (fixed costs + target profit) / contribution margin. If profit is after tax, the target is first divided by one minus the tax rate.

Margin of safety = (expected sales - break-even sales) / expected sales. It measures how far sales can fall before you lose money.

Worked example

A small business has 10,000 of monthly fixed costs. It sells a product at 50 with a variable cost of 30. The margin is 20 per unit, or 40% of the price.

If the price rose by 10% to 55, the margin would be 25 and break-even would fall to 400 units. That is why pricing is the strongest lever in the model.

High-margin models break even on far lower volume, and they scale faster because each extra sale drops more into profit. Low-margin models need volume and tight cost control.

Fixed, variable and mixed costs

Getting the split right is the biggest source of error. Fixed costs include rent, salaried staff, insurance, loan repayments, accounting fees and software. Variable costs include materials, packaging, payment fees, delivery, sales commissions and hourly workers scheduled around demand. Mixed costs, such as utilities and phone plans, have a fixed base and a usage part, so split them.

Payroll deserves care. Salaried staff are fixed, hourly staff tied to output are variable. Do not forget your own pay: if you draw a regular amount from the business, it is a fixed cost.

Using break-even to make pricing decisions

  • Raise the price by 10%. The margin rises and break-even falls. Often this needs fewer lost customers than people fear.
  • Cut the price by 10%. In a low-margin business this can double the volume you need. Price wars destroy profit for this reason.
  • Cut variable cost. Better supplier terms or lower payment fees lift the margin on every sale.
  • Cut fixed cost. A cheaper lease lowers the break-even without touching price.

The Price what-if tab shows both effects together in a table and a chart, so you can compare the units you would need with the volume the market can realistically give you.

Break-even for several products

When you sell more than one product, a single break-even is misleading. The calculator finds a blended contribution margin by weighting each product by its share of units sold. Fixed costs divided by that blended margin gives total break-even units, which are then shared out by mix. If the mix changes towards low-margin items, the real break-even rises, so revisit the mix often.

Limits of break-even analysis

  • Constant price and cost. Discounts, bulk purchases and overtime bend the lines.
  • Capacity. A workshop that can make 400 units cannot break even at 500.
  • Cash timing. You can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash.
  • Step costs. A second site or extra shift adds a new block of fixed cost at a certain volume.
  • Tax and finance. The basic model is before tax and interest unless you add them.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using revenue instead of contribution margin.
  • Leaving the owner's pay out of fixed costs.
  • Putting hourly labour in fixed costs.
  • Mixing monthly fixed costs with annual unit forecasts.
  • Forgetting payment processing fees and returns in variable cost.
  • Calculating once at launch and never again. Costs and prices move, so update quarterly.

Frequently asked questions

What is a break-even calculator?

It shows how many units you must sell, or how much revenue you need, before your sales cover all costs. Below that point you lose money; above it you make a profit.

How do I calculate the break-even point?

Divide fixed costs by the contribution margin per unit. With 10,000 of fixed costs, a 50 price and a 30 variable cost, the margin is 20 and break-even is 500 units.

What is the break-even formula in revenue?

Break-even revenue equals fixed costs divided by the contribution margin ratio, which is the margin divided by the price. In the example, 10,000 / 0.40 = 25,000.

What is contribution margin?

The part of each sale left after variable costs. It first pays fixed costs and then becomes profit.

What is margin of safety?

The percentage by which expected sales exceed break-even sales. A 33% margin means sales can fall by a third before you start losing money.

What counts as a fixed cost?

Costs that do not change with sales volume in the short term, such as rent, salaries, insurance, software subscriptions and loan repayments.

What counts as a variable cost?

Costs that rise with each unit sold, such as materials, packaging, card fees, delivery and sales commissions.

How do I handle a cost that is partly fixed and partly variable?

Split it. Utilities, for example, have a fixed connection charge and a usage charge. Put the first in fixed costs and the second in variable cost per unit.

Can I find break-even for a target profit?

Yes. Add your desired profit to fixed costs before dividing by the margin. If you enter a tax rate, the tool grosses the profit up to a pre-tax figure first.

How do I find break-even for more than one product?

Use the Several products tab. It weights each product's margin by its share of units sold, then divides fixed costs by that blended margin.

What is operating leverage?

The ratio of contribution to profit. A high figure means profit swings sharply when sales change, which raises both opportunity and risk.

Should I use monthly or annual figures?

Either, as long as fixed costs and expected units use the same period. The tool labels results with the period you choose.

Does break-even include the owner's pay?

It should. If you draw a salary or regular drawings, count them as a fixed cost, otherwise your break-even is too low.

How does a price rise change break-even?

A higher price raises the margin and lowers the units you need. The Price what-if tab shows the effect of changes from -20% to +20%.

What is the payback period?

How many periods of profit it takes to recover a startup investment. It is shown when you enter an investment and expect a profit.

Why is break-even not the same as cash flow?

Break-even is a profit concept. Late customer payments, stock purchases and loan principal can drain cash even when you are above break-even.

Is my data stored?

No. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is sent or saved.
