What this free online SBA loan calculator does

Small Business Administration loans are some of the most useful borrowing tools for small businesses in the United States. They offer long terms, modest down payments and rates that are often lower than other business credit. They are also confusing: there are several programs, rates are built from the prime rate and a capped spread, and fees depend on the loan size and the borrower. This free online SBA loan calculator turns those rules into a payment you can see.

Choose a program, enter the amount and term, and the tool shows your monthly payment, total interest, up-front fees, effective APR, debt service coverage and final payment date. It draws a yearly chart of principal, interest and remaining balance. Separate tabs compare a 7(a) loan with a 504 loan for the same project, show what happens if prime rises, show how an extra monthly payment shortens the loan, and list the schedule year by year. You can download the full monthly schedule as a CSV file or print a clean report.

How to use this SBA loan calculator

  1. Pick the program. Use 7(a) for general purposes, Express for faster smaller loans, 504 for property and equipment, and Microloan for up to 50,000.
  2. Enter the amount and term. The term list changes with the program.
  3. Set the rate. For 7(a) and Express, enter the current prime rate and the spread your lender quotes. The tool tells you the maximum spread allowed for that loan size. For 504, enter the bank rate and the CDC fixed rate.
  4. Choose how fees are paid. Tick the box to roll fees into the loan, or leave it clear if you will pay them in cash.
  5. Add optional details, such as an extra payment or your yearly cash flow, to see months saved and coverage.
  6. Read the result on the Loan tab, then compare programs, test rate changes and print the report.

How SBA 7(a) rates and spreads work

Most 7(a) loans are variable. The lender takes the prime rate and adds a spread. The SBA caps that spread, and the cap falls as the loan grows. At the time of writing the caps were 6.5 points on loans up to 50,000, 6 points from 50,001 to 250,000, 4.5 points from 250,001 to 350,000 and 3 points above 350,000. With prime at 7%, a 500,000 loan can therefore cost up to 10%. Lenders may charge less, and strong borrowers often get a lower spread.

The calculator fills in the maximum spread when you change the amount and warns if you type a spread above it. It is a sensible default for budgeting because it gives a cost ceiling.

SBA fees: guarantee fee, servicing and closing costs

Most of the extra cost of an SBA loan comes from fees, so look at them closely.

  • Guarantee fee. The 7(a) fee applies to the guaranteed portion, which is 85% of loans up to 150,000 and 75% above. Rates step up with size, from about 2% to around 3.5% or more, and the fee is much lower on very short loans. These tiers are approximate in the tool.
  • Annual service fee. The SBA charges the lender about 0.55% of the guaranteed balance. It is not passed to you, though the lender may reflect it in pricing.
  • CDC and 504 fees. Standard 504 loans carry an up-front fee of about 0.5% and an annual servicing fee near 0.209%, plus CDC processing and closing costs.
  • Manufacturer waiver. Eligible manufacturers pay no 7(a) up-front fee on loans up to 950,000 and no 504 fees.
  • Packaging and lender fees. These are negotiated, so use the other fees box.

Rolling fees into the loan keeps cash in the business but raises the amount you borrow and the interest. The effective APR shows the true price of that choice.

How an SBA 504 loan is split

A 504 loan pairs a bank loan with a fixed-rate CDC loan, so the two parts have different rates. Averaging them into a single rate, as many simple calculators do, hides the structure. This tool calculates each loan on its own schedule and then adds them together, which also shows the payment each lender will bill you.

Worked example

A business borrows 500,000 on a 10-year 7(a) loan. Prime is 7% and the lender charges the maximum 3-point spread, so the rate is 10%. The guaranteed amount is 75%, so the up-front fee at 3% is 11,250, added to the loan.

7(a) or 504: which is cheaper?

  • 504 usually wins for large real estate purchases because the CDC portion is fixed and long, and the bank portion needs only 50% of the cost.
  • 7(a) is better when you need flexibility, working capital, a faster close or a smaller project.
  • 504 closes more slowly because two lenders and the SBA are involved.
  • Compare total interest plus fees, not just the payment. The comparison tab does this.

What lenders look for

Lenders weigh credit history, time in business, collateral and cash flow. Debt service coverage of 1.15 to 1.25 is a common minimum. Owners of 20% or more usually sign a personal guarantee. A good business plan and up-to-date financial statements speed approval. The coverage figure in the calculator uses the yearly cash flow you enter, so use a realistic number after owner pay.

Ways to lower the cost of an SBA loan

  • Shop several SBA-approved lenders. Spreads vary a lot.
  • Pay fees in cash if you can, to avoid interest on them.
  • Choose the shortest term you can afford.
  • Make extra payments, but check for a prepayment fee.
  • Ask about the manufacturer waiver if it applies.
  • Consider a fixed rate if you cannot absorb a rate rise.

Frequently asked questions

What is an SBA loan calculator?

It estimates the monthly payment, total interest and fees on a loan backed by the U.S. Small Business Administration. This one covers 7(a), Express, 504 and Microloans.

How are SBA 7(a) interest rates set?

Variable rates are the prime rate plus a lender spread. The SBA caps the spread by loan size: 6.5 points for loans up to 50,000, 6 up to 250,000, 4.5 up to 350,000 and 3 above that. Check current caps with your lender.

What is the prime rate used in the tool?

It defaults to 7.00%, which was the published rate at the time of writing. Change it to today's prime rate, because it moves with Federal Reserve decisions.

What is the SBA guarantee fee?

An up-front fee on the guaranteed part of a 7(a) loan, roughly 2% to 3.75% depending on size, and much lower for loans of 12 months or less. The tool treats the tiers as approximate and can add the fee to the loan.

Do manufacturers pay the guarantee fee?

For eligible manufacturers (NAICS 31 to 33), the SBA has waived the 7(a) up-front fee on loans up to 950,000 and waived 504 fees. Tick the manufacturer box to model this and confirm eligibility with your lender.

How does an SBA 504 loan work?

The project is funded by a bank loan for about 50%, a CDC loan backed by the SBA for about 40% and your down payment of about 10%. The CDC part has a fixed rate, which is why this tool prices the two pieces separately.

What can I use a 504 loan for?

Owner-occupied commercial real estate, major equipment and certain renovations. It cannot be used for working capital or inventory.

What can I use a 7(a) loan for?

Working capital, equipment, inventory, refinancing certain debt, purchasing a business and some real estate. It is the most flexible SBA program.

What are the maximum loan sizes?

7(a) loans go up to 5,000,000, Express loans up to 500,000 and Microloans up to 50,000. The SBA-backed 504 portion is typically up to 5,000,000 or more for some projects.

What loan terms are available?

Up to 10 years for working capital and equipment, up to 25 years for real estate on 7(a), and 10, 20 or 25 years on 504. Microloans run up to 6 years.

What is the effective APR shown?

The rate that makes the payments equal to the cash you receive after fees. It is higher than the interest rate when fees are included, so it is the fairer number for comparing offers.

What is debt service coverage?

Yearly cash flow before debt divided by yearly loan payments. Lenders commonly want 1.15 to 1.25 or more. Enter your cash flow to see where you stand.

What happens if prime rises?

On variable-rate 7(a) loans the payment rises. The Rates tab shows the payment and total interest if prime moves by -1 to +3 points.

Can I pay an SBA loan off early?

Yes, but loans with terms of 15 years or longer may charge a prepayment fee in the first three years. Use the extra payment box to see months and interest saved.

Do I need collateral and a personal guarantee?

Usually. Lenders must take available collateral, and anyone owning 20% or more of the business generally signs an unlimited personal guarantee.

How accurate is the calculator?

It is a good planning estimate. Real offers include lender-specific fees, packaging charges, insurance and rate adjustments that change the figures.

Is my data stored?

No. Calculations run in your browser and nothing is sent or saved.
