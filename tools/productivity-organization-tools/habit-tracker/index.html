What this free online habit tracker does

Most good intentions fail quietly. You mean to exercise, read or drink more water, and a few weeks later you cannot say whether you have done it twice or twenty times. A habit tracker fixes that by making the behaviour visible. Every day you mark a square, and over time the squares form a picture of your consistency. That picture is motivating when it is full of ticks and informative when it is not, because it shows exactly which days and habits slip.

This free online habit tracker shows one month at a time, with a row for each habit and a column for each day. Give each habit an emoji and a colour, and the tracker calculates your completion rate, current and best streaks and progress against a weekly goal. A year heatmap shows the whole year at a glance. Everything saves automatically in your browser, so there is nothing to install and no account to create. Print a blank template, copy the month into Google Sheets or Excel, download a CSV or back up your data.

How to use the habit tracker

  1. Add a habit. Type it or tap a suggestion, and pick an emoji and colour if you like. Make it specific and small, such as read two pages.
  2. Choose a weekly goal. Daily suits tiny habits. Three or four days a week suits exercise.
  3. Tick each day. Tap the square for the day you did it. Tap again to undo.
  4. Check progress. The summary shows your monthly percentage, streaks, this week's count and your last 30 days.
  5. Review monthly. Check the year heatmap, move to the next month, keep what works and change or drop what does not.

To go faster, use the keyboard: press N to add a habit, T for this month, [ and ] to change month, and the number keys 1 to 9 to tick or untick today for the first nine habits.

Year heatmap and statistics

The monthly grid is good for today and this week, but it hides the long view. The year heatmap draws one small square for every day of the year, in weeks from left to right, shaded by how much you did. With all habits selected, a darker square means you completed a larger share of your habits that day. Choose a single habit and each square is simply done or not done, in that habit's colour. Use Previous and Next to look at other years.

The heatmap is useful for spotting patterns that a month cannot show. Long dark runs are streaks. A pale band every Saturday means weekends need a different plan. A blank fortnight points to a holiday, an illness or a habit that was too big. Heatmaps like this are common in coding and fitness tools because they make consistency visible without any numbers.

Below the chart, the progress panel adds three statistics: completion over the last 30 days, your all-time check-ins, and your most active weekday once you have a week of data. Treat them as feedback, not scores. They are calculated only from what you have ticked in this browser.

Printable habit tracker and blank template

Some people prefer paper. A printed tracker on the fridge or in a notebook is hard to ignore, and ticking a box with a pen can feel more satisfying than tapping a screen. This page supports both. Print blank template makes a clean grid for the month you are viewing, with a column for every day, your habit names in the first rows and empty rows below for new ones, and no ticks. Print / save month prints your actual ticks with a summary table of percentages and streaks.

To get a PDF, open either print option and choose Save as PDF as the destination in your browser's print dialog. Landscape orientation fits a full month best. Because the printout is built from the same grid as the screen, it always matches the month you are looking at.

Habit tracker for Excel and Google Sheets

If you like spreadsheets, you do not need a template file. Press Copy for Sheets / Excel, click a cell in a blank sheet and paste. You get a header row with the days of the month, one row per habit with a 1 for each day you did it, and a total column. Because the ticks are numbers, a simple SUM works across a row, and you can add your own formulas, conditional formatting or charts. Download CSV gives the same table as a file that both Excel and Google Sheets can open or import.

Spreadsheets are flexible but fiddly on a phone, and a long grid is easy to break by accident. Many people therefore track here day to day and copy the month into a sheet only when they want to analyse it.

Online habit tracker or habit tracker app?

A habit tracker app is installed from a store, usually has an account, and can send reminder notifications and sync between devices. This free online habit tracker works differently. It is a web page that runs in your browser, with no download and no sign-up. Your habits stay on the device you use, which keeps them private but means they do not sync. Use Download backup to move them to another browser or device.

The trade-off is simple. If you want push reminders and sync, an app suits you. If you want something you can open in seconds on any computer or phone, without handing over an email address, a web tracker is enough. Either way, the tracking only works if you use it, so pick the one you will actually open each day.

What research says about forming habits

Habits form when a behaviour is repeated in a stable context until it becomes more automatic. A well-known study by Phillippa Lally and colleagues at University College London followed volunteers who chose a new daily behaviour. On average it took about 66 days for the behaviour to reach its automaticity plateau, but the range was wide, from 18 to 254 days, and missing a single day did not significantly harm the process. This is useful: perfection is not required, and a long timeline is normal. Research on implementation intentions by Peter Gollwitzer shows that specific plans of the form when X happens, I will do Y make people more likely to follow through than general goals.

How to build a habit that lasts

  • Start tiny. Two minutes of reading is better than a plan for an hour that you skip.
  • Anchor it. Attach the habit to something you already do, such as after I brush my teeth, I will stretch.
  • Make it obvious and easy. Put the book on the pillow, the trainers by the door.
  • Never miss twice. One missed day is normal. Two starts a new pattern.
  • Celebrate briefly. A small reward helps the brain link the behaviour with satisfaction.
  • Track one to three habits. More dilutes your attention.

Habit tracker ideas and examples

For health, try drinking water, a ten-minute walk, stretching, going to bed at a set time or eating a portion of vegetables. For learning, read ten pages, practise a language for fifteen minutes or review flashcards. For focus and calm, meditate for five minutes, write three lines in a journal or take a phone-free hour. For home and money, make the bed, tidy for ten minutes or record your spending. For relationships, send one message to a friend or family member. For work, plan tomorrow before you finish or clear your inbox once a day. Pick the ones that matter to you, not the ones that look good on a list.

Bullet journal style

The month grid is the layout most bullet journals use for habits: a row for each habit and a column for each day. If you keep a notebook, plan your habits here, then print the blank template and tape it in, or copy the habit names across by hand. Bullet journal fans often add a mood or sleep row, which you can do here by adding them as habits, such as slept at least seven hours.

Using streaks without being controlled by them

Streaks motivate because breaking the chain feels costly. They can also backfire: when a streak snaps, some people feel they have failed and quit entirely. Treat the best streak as a record to beat rather than a test of worth, and use the monthly percentage as a more forgiving measure. A person who completes a habit 80 per cent of days is doing very well. For habits that are not daily, the weekly goal shows whether you are on track without demanding perfection.

Reviewing your month

At the end of each month, look at the chart. Habits at or above 80 per cent are working, so keep them. Habits at 30 to 60 per cent may be too ambitious or tied to a poor trigger, so make them smaller or change when you do them. Habits near zero were probably wishes rather than commitments, so replace them. Look for patterns by weekday in the grid: if weekends are always empty, build a weekend version of the routine. Then save a backup before starting the next month.

Habit tracker, to-do list and journal: what is the difference?

A to-do list holds one-off tasks that you finish and cross out. A habit tracker holds behaviours you want to repeat, so the same item returns every day and success is measured by consistency instead of completion. A journal records how you feel and what happened, which can explain why a habit slipped. Many people use all three: the to-do list for projects, the habit tracker for routines and a short journal entry for reflection. If you keep a Pomodoro log, you can also add a daily focus habit here, such as complete two focus sessions, and tick it when you do.

Whatever you choose, keep the system simple enough to use on your worst day. A tracker that takes ten seconds to update will still be in use in six months. One that needs a ritual will not.

Common habit tracking mistakes

  • Too many habits at once.
  • Vague habits. Be specific about what counts as done.
  • Habits that are too big. Shrink them until they feel easy.
  • Judging yourself for a bad week. Return the next day.
  • Ticking in advance. Only tick what you have done.

Habit tracking should support wellbeing, so stop or change any habit that causes distress.

Frequently asked questions

What is a habit tracker?

A tool for recording whether you did a habit each day. Seeing a row of ticks makes progress visible, which can help you stay consistent.

How do I use this free online habit tracker?

Add a habit, then tap the square for each day you complete it. The tracker updates your monthly percentage, current streak, best streak and weekly goal, and fills in the year heatmap.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up. Your data stays in this browser.

Where is my data saved?

In your browser's local storage on this device. It is not uploaded. Download a backup to keep a copy or move it to another device.

What is a streak?

The number of consecutive days you completed a habit. The current streak counts back from today, or from yesterday if you have not ticked today yet.

Does missing one day break my streak?

Yes, a missed day ends the current streak, but your best streak is kept. Many people follow a never miss twice rule to recover quickly.

Can I set a weekly goal instead of every day?

Yes. Choose every day or any number from one to six days a week. The chart shows how many days you have done this week against your goal.

Can I use it as a monthly habit tracker or a 30-day challenge?

Yes. The grid shows one month at a time with a column for every day. For a 30-day challenge, add one habit and tick it each day. Use Previous and Next to move between months and This month to return.

Can I look at past or future months?

Yes. Use Previous and Next to move between months and This month to return. You can fill in missed days for earlier dates.

How many habits should I track?

Start with one to three. Tracking too many at once makes it harder to keep them all going. The tracker supports up to 20.

What are good habits to start with?

Small, specific ones such as drinking a glass of water after waking, reading ten pages, a ten-minute walk or writing one line in a journal.

How long does it take to form a habit?

It varies. A study in the European Journal of Social Psychology found an average of about 66 days, with a range from 18 to 254 days depending on the person and the habit.

Is there a printable habit tracker or a PDF?

Yes. Print blank template gives a month grid with empty boxes and spare rows to fill in by hand. Print / save month prints your own ticks and a summary table. In the print dialog, choose Save as PDF to get a PDF. Landscape fits best.

Is there a habit tracker template for Excel or Google Sheets?

This is a web tool, not a spreadsheet file. Copy for Sheets / Excel copies the month as a table you can paste straight into either program, and Download CSV opens in both. Ticks are 1s, so you can add up a row with a simple SUM.

What is the year heatmap?

A grid of small squares, one per day of the year, shaded by how many of your habits you completed. Pick one habit to see only that habit. It makes streaks, gaps and busy seasons easy to spot at a glance.

Can I choose an emoji and colour for each habit?

Yes. Pick an emoji and a colour when you add a habit, or change them later from the habit list under Progress and streaks. The colour is used in the grid, the chart and the heatmap.

Are there keyboard shortcuts?

Yes. Press N to start a new habit, T to jump to this month, [ and ] to change month, and 1 to 9 to tick or untick today for the first nine habits. Shortcuts are ignored while you are typing in a box.

Is this a habit tracker app? Can I install it?

It is a free web tool that runs in your browser, so there is nothing to download. Unlike many apps it has no notifications, accounts or syncing between devices. Use a phone alarm for reminders and a backup file to move data.

Can I export to a spreadsheet?

Yes. Download CSV gives one row per habit and one column per day of the month, and Copy for Sheets / Excel pastes the same table directly into a sheet.

Can I reorder or delete habits?

Yes. Use the arrow to move a habit up and Delete to remove it and its history.

Does it send reminders?

No. Pair each habit with an existing routine or a phone alarm, and use the tracker to record progress.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. The grid scrolls sideways and squares are easy to tap.

What habit tracker ideas work for a bullet journal?

The monthly grid matches the classic bullet-journal habit spread, so you can plan here and copy it into a notebook, or print the blank template and stick it in. Common rows are water, sleep, movement, reading and a daily one-line entry.

Yes. It is free to use online and your habit data stays in your browser.
