What this free online Kanban board does

Kanban is a Japanese word meaning visual signal. It began on Toyota's production lines in the 1940s and was adapted for knowledge work in the 2000s. The idea is simple: show every piece of work as a card on a board whose columns represent stages, and limit how much can be in progress at once. A free online Kanban board gives you that picture without sticky notes or a subscription, so you can see what is waiting, what is being done and what is finished.

This board lets you add cards, drag them between columns, label and prioritise them, set due dates, add notes and cap the work in progress. It starts with a sample so you can see how it works, and you can rename every column. Your board saves automatically in your browser, and you can download it as a file or CSV, copy it as text or print it.

How to use the Kanban board

  1. Set up your columns. The default To do, In progress, Review and Done fits many workflows. Rename or add columns, such as Backlog or Blocked.
  2. Add cards. Type a task in a column and press Enter. Keep titles short and specific.
  3. Add detail. Press Edit to set a label, priority, due date and notes.
  4. Pull work forward. Drag a card to the next column when you start it, and again when it is done.
  5. Set WIP limits. Enter a limit on busy columns, such as three for In progress.
  6. Review. Look at the summary, clear what is finished and plan what comes next.

The core principles of Kanban

Kanban rests on a few ideas. Visualise the work so that nothing is hidden. Limit work in progress, because starting many things at once slows all of them. Manage flow by watching how cards move and where they get stuck. Make the rules explicit, so everyone knows what Done means. Improve gradually by changing one thing at a time. These principles are why a plain board can change how a team works: when a column fills up, the answer is not to start more, but to help finish what is already there.

Why work-in-progress limits matter

It is tempting to start every task as soon as it arrives, but switching between tasks has a cost, and unfinished work delivers no value. A WIP limit forces a conversation: if In progress is full, help move something to Review before pulling something new. Teams often begin with a limit of two or three per person and adjust. If a column is constantly at its limit, it may be a bottleneck; if it is often empty, the limit may be too low or earlier stages may be starved. The board flags a column in red when it holds more cards than its limit.

Kanban board ideas for work, study and home

  • Software and product teams. Backlog, Ready, In progress, Review, Done.
  • Content calendars. Ideas, Drafting, Editing, Scheduled, Published.
  • Job search. Interested, Applied, Interview, Offer, Closed.
  • Students. To read, Reading, Notes written, Revised.
  • Home projects. Wishlist, Planned, Doing, Done.
  • Small business. Leads, Contacted, Quoted, Won.

Using labels, priorities and due dates well

Labels answer what kind of work a card is. Priorities answer how soon it matters. Dates answer when it is due. Use all three sparingly. A board where every card is urgent communicates nothing. Try restricting high priority to a few cards and reserving due dates for genuine deadlines. A label such as Bug or Research lets you filter the board to a single type of work during planning.

Kanban compared with other planning methods

A to-do list tells you what is left but not what stage it is at. A Gantt chart shows when tasks should happen but is less useful for day-to-day flow. Scrum organises work into fixed-length sprints with planned scope, while Kanban lets work arrive and flow continuously. The Eisenhower matrix helps decide what to do first, and a Kanban board then shows where each chosen task stands. Choose Kanban when work arrives unpredictably, priorities change often or you want to see bottlenecks quickly. Choose a Gantt chart when dates and dependencies drive the project.

A simple way to start this week

Begin with three columns: To do, Doing and Done. Empty your head onto the board by adding every task you can think of. Move only the two or three most important cards into Doing and set the limit of that column to three. Work until a card is finished, drag it to Done, then pull the next card. At the end of the week, look at the Done column to see what you achieved, remove old cards, and move leftovers back to To do. After a week or two, add columns such as Review or Blocked only if you feel the need, because a simple board is easier to maintain.

Common Kanban mistakes

  • No WIP limits. The board becomes a long to-do list.
  • Vague cards. Write a task that can be finished.
  • Too many columns. Start simple.
  • Cards that never move. Break them down or remove them.
  • Unclear Done. Agree what finished means.
  • Never reviewing. Look at the board regularly.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Kanban board?

A visual board with columns such as To do, In progress and Done. Each task is a card that moves across the columns as work advances, so everyone can see what is happening at a glance.

How do I use this free online Kanban board?

Add cards to a column, drag them to the next stage as you work, and use labels, priorities and due dates to add detail. Rename, add or remove columns to match your process.

Do I need to sign up?

No. There is no account. Open the page and start building your board.

Where is my board saved?

In your browser's local storage on this device. It is not uploaded. Use Save board to keep a file copy or move it to another device.

Can I share the board with a team?

Not live. This is a single-user board. You can share it by copying it as text, printing it or sending the saved board file.

What is a WIP limit?

A work-in-progress limit caps how many cards a column should hold. When it is exceeded the column is flagged. Limits reduce multitasking and show bottlenecks.

How do I move cards?

Drag a card onto another column or onto another card to place it before that card. You can also use the left and right arrows to change column and up and down to change order.

Can I rename columns or add new ones?

Yes. Edit the column name in its header, use Add column for more, and the arrows to reorder. Up to 10 columns are supported.

What happens to cards when I delete a column?

They move to the neighbouring column, so nothing is lost.

Can I add labels, priorities and due dates?

Yes. Press Edit on a card to set a label such as Bug or Feature, a priority, notes and a due date. Overdue cards not in the last column are flagged.

Can I search or filter?

Yes. Search by title or notes and filter by label. Filtering only hides cards in view and does not delete anything.

Can I print the board?

Yes. Print / save board prints only a clean list of columns and cards, not the rest of the page.

Can I export to a spreadsheet?

Yes. Download CSV lists every card with its column, notes, label, priority and due date for Excel or Google Sheets.

What is the difference between Kanban and Scrum?

Scrum works in fixed sprints with defined roles. Kanban is continuous and focuses on visualising work and limiting work in progress. Many teams combine them.

Can I use it for personal projects?

Yes. A board works for study plans, job applications, content calendars, home projects and more.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. Columns scroll sideways, and the arrow buttons let you move cards without dragging.

Is it free and private?

Yes. It is free to use online and your board stays in your browser.
