    Anatomy of a good TikTok caption

    Strong short-video captions usually have four parts, in this order.

    • Hook. One sentence that names the topic and gives a reason to care. It should work even if nothing else is read.
    • Context. One or two lines that add the detail, the promise or a keyword phrase people might search.
    • Call to action. A single clear ask: comment, save, share, follow or visit the link in your bio.
    • Hashtags. A small, relevant set at the end, so the readable part comes first.

    The generator builds this structure automatically, and the Long style adds a short list with bracketed placeholders for your own points. Replace those before posting; the checker will warn you if any remain.

    TikTok caption character limits

    Limits are set by the platform and change from time to time, so confirm the current figure in the app. According to scheduling-tool documentation, captions typed into the TikTok app are commonly cited at 4,000 characters, while many publishing and scheduling tools cap TikTok captions at 2,200. Hashtags and emoji count toward the total.

    The limit matters less than the cut-off. In the feed, only the beginning of a long caption is visible before a more link. The exact point depends on the device, the font size and the language, and a commonly quoted range is roughly the first 100 to 150 characters. This tool uses an estimate, shades it on every caption and shows it in the feed preview, so you can check that your hook is not hidden.

    Characters are counted as visible characters, so a single emoji counts as one. Platforms can count some characters differently, so leave a small margin when you are close to a limit.

    Hashtags: how many and which

    Hashtags work best as labels that describe the video, not as a lottery ticket. There is no official number, and the hashtag guidance published by scheduling tools generally points to a few focused tags, around three to five, instead of long lists.

    A useful mix, sometimes called the 3-3-3 approach by creators, combines tag types:

    • Topic tag. Built from your own words, such as #MorningRoutine. It names the video precisely.
    • Niche tags. Describe the audience and format, such as #HabitTips or #CleaningHacks.
    • Broad tag. A general tag such as #fyp. It is very competitive and carries little information, so use at most one.
    • Community tag. One community label, such as #BookTok, only if the video truly belongs there.

    The Hashtag lab colours each tag by type, counts the mix and warns you when a set is all broad tags or too long. The lists are curated suggestions, not live trend data, so check a tag in the app's search before using it. Avoid tags that do not describe your content, and never use a tag you do not understand.

    Keywords and TikTok search

    Many people now search inside social apps. TikTok has said that search results draw on information such as video text, captions and hashtags, which means plain-language phrases in your caption can help the right viewers find the video. Nothing here guarantees ranking, but it costs nothing to be clear.

    • Write the way a viewer would search: "budget meal prep for one" rather than "meal prep tips".
    • Prefer specific, long-tail phrases over single broad words. They match real queries and face less competition.
    • Say the topic in the caption, in the on-screen text and in the spoken words, so all three agree.
    • Use a keyword once or twice in natural sentences. Repeating it looks like spam and reads badly.

    Enter up to five phrases in the Keywords field. The generator adds a natural search sentence and builds hashtags from the strongest phrases.

    How long should a caption be?

    Short captions let the video do the work and suit entertainment and aesthetic posts. Standard captions of a few lines suit most tips, stories and product posts. Long captions suit tutorials, recipes and step lists that viewers will save. In every case the first sentence has to carry the point.

    Goals and calls to action

    A caption with no ask leaves a decision to the viewer. Choose one goal per post, because several competing asks dilute each other.

    • Comment works for questions and opinion posts.
    • Save works for lists, recipes and tutorials.
    • Share works for relatable and funny posts.
    • Follow works for series and part-two content.
    • Link in bio and shop work for guides, offers and products. Only use them when the link is ready.
    • Stitch or duet works when you invite a response in video form.

    You can also type your own call to action; it replaces the generated one.

    Common caption mistakes

    • Burying the hook. If the point arrives after the cut-off, most viewers never see it.
    • Hashtag stuffing. Long lists look spammy and weaken the topic signal.
    • Only generic tags. Tags such as #fyp say nothing about your video.
    • No call to action. A simple question can invite replies.
    • Mismatch. A hook that overpromises hurts trust.
    • Posting placeholders. Leaving [brackets] in a published caption looks careless.
    • Copying the same caption every time. Vary your hooks and formats.

    Honesty, ads and disclosure

    Some hooks describe personal experience, such as a 30-day test, a confession or a story. They are prompts, not facts. Keep them only if they are true for you, and never invent results, reviews, income claims or testimonials. Cards that use those styles carry a reminder.

    If you were paid, gifted a product or have another material connection to a brand, you are generally expected to disclose it clearly. Use the platform's own branded-content or paid-partnership control, and add a clear label such as #ad where appropriate. Rules differ by country, and regulators such as the United States Federal Trade Commission publish guidance for endorsements. The Caption templates tab includes a disclosure example.

    Respect copyright, music licensing and platform community guidelines. A caption cannot make an infringing or misleading video acceptable.
