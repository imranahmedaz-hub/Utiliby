TikTok caption character limits in 2026

TikTok has two different caption limits, and knowing which applies to you matters:

  • 2,200 characters — the limit enforced by the TikTok Content Posting API. Every scheduling tool and third-party publishing app uses this limit. If you post through a scheduler, this is your working cap.
  • 4,000 characters — the in-app limit when typing directly into the TikTok app.

But the number that actually matters is much smaller. TikTok truncates captions in the feed at roughly 100 to 150 characters, showing a "more" link that most viewers never tap. This means the first sentence — the hook — must stand alone. This tool marks exactly where TikTok will truncate so you can see at a glance whether your hook fits.

The hook: the single most important part of a caption

The hook is the opening line of your caption and, on TikTok, it is doing the same job as the first three seconds of your video: stopping the scroll. A good hook falls into one of a small number of proven patterns:

  • Curiosity gap. "I tried the 5-minute air fryer recipe everyone is talking about." The brain wants closure.
  • Contrarian take. "Your morning routine is not the problem." Challenges an assumption.
  • Specific number. "Three budget tips that saved me $400 this month." Numbers signal concrete value.
  • Relatable call-out. "POV: you promised yourself you would meal prep." Instantly mirrors the viewer.
  • Open loop question. "Why does nobody talk about this?" Forces the brain to formulate an answer.
  • Negation / myth buster. "It is not your hashtags — it is your first three seconds." Short, punchy, memorable.

The best captions often combine two hooks from different categories — a stop-scroll hook (negation, number, question) with a retention hook (story, confession, emotional). This tool's hook library is organised by category so you can mix and match.

The 3-3-3 hashtag rule

Hashtag strategy on TikTok has changed dramatically. In the early days, creators piled on generic tags like #fyp and #viral hoping for a lucky break. In 2026, hashtags are about relevance and indexing, not virality. The algorithm reads them as category signals.

A widely adopted framework is the 3-3-3 rule: aim for three to five hashtags that mix different reach levels.

  • 1–2 broad tags. High-traffic categories like #fyp, #tiktok or #viral. These give your video a small chance of reaching a wide audience but are extremely competitive.
  • 2–3 niche tags. Specific search-intent tags that describe the video. A cooking video might use #AirFryerRecipes and #5MinuteMeals rather than just #Food.
  • 1 community tag. If your content sits inside a known TikTok community — #BookTok, #FitTok, #CleanTok, #StudyTok — this tag connects you with an audience that is highly engaged and actively looking for your kind of content.

Hashtags vs keywords: the 2026 shift

The biggest change in TikTok caption strategy over the last two years is the rise of keywords over hashtags. TikTok now reads your caption, transcribes your audio and analyses your on-screen text to determine what your video is about. This means natural language keywords in the caption do as much work as the hashtags themselves — sometimes more.

Practical implication: write your caption the way your audience types into search. "How to meal prep for one person on a budget" beats "meal prep, budget, single" because it matches the query format. This tool embeds your topic keyword naturally into the caption body, not just the hashtag set.

Common TikTok caption mistakes

  • Burying the hook. If the point of the video is not clear in the first 100 characters, most viewers will never see it.
  • Hashtag stuffing. Ten to fifteen hashtags do not help and can dilute the signal. Three to five focused tags perform better.
  • Using generic tags only. #Fyp and #Viral are used on billions of videos. They give your content almost no categorical information.
  • Missing the CTA. A caption without a call to action leaves engagement on the table. Even a simple question doubles comment rates.
  • Ignoring the niche. Community tags like #BookTok connect you with a smaller but far more engaged audience than broad tags.
  • Copy-pasting the same caption. The algorithm treats repetitive captions as low-effort. Vary the hook and formula.
