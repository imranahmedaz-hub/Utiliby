What this free social media image resizer does

Every social network wants a different shape: a tall Story, a square profile photo, a wide banner, a four-by-five post. Making all of them from one picture takes time in an editor. This free social media image resizer lets you choose a photo, tick any of 30 popular sizes for Instagram, Facebook, X, LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest and more, and get properly cropped images in one click or one ZIP file.

You stay in control of the crop. Click on your photo to set a focus point, and every size keeps that area in view. Choose fill, fit with a blurred background or stretch, zoom in, show safe zone guides, pick JPG, PNG or WebP and see each result before you download. The photo never leaves your device, and the process removes hidden metadata such as GPS location.

How to resize an image for social media

  1. Choose your photo. Pick a file or drop it on the box.
  2. Tick the sizes you need. Press Select popular sizes for a quick start.
  3. Set the focus point. Click or drag on the photo to mark the subject.
  4. Pick how to resize. Fill and crop works for most photos, fit keeps the whole picture.
  5. Check the previews. Turn on safe zones, and look for the upscaled warning.
  6. Download. Use the button under one preview or download all sizes as a ZIP.

Fill, fit or stretch: which resize mode to choose

Fill scales the photo until it covers the whole frame and crops what does not fit. It is the best choice for most photos, because nothing looks empty or distorted, and the focus point decides what stays. Fit shows the entire photo and fills the leftover space with a blurred copy of the picture or a solid color, which suits images with text, logos or group shots that you cannot crop. Stretch forces the image to the new size without keeping proportions, which makes faces and logos look wrong, so use it only for abstract backgrounds.

Aspect ratios explained

An aspect ratio is the width compared with the height. A square is 1:1, a portrait Instagram post is 4:5, a Story or vertical video is 9:16, a standard widescreen image is 16:9 and a link preview is about 1.91:1. When the shape of your photo does not match the target, something has to be cut or padded. The previews show exactly what will happen so you can choose a different crop, a smaller zoom or another focus point before downloading.

Safe zones and why they matter

Apps draw buttons, names and captions on top of your image. In a Story or a Reel, the top and bottom parts are often covered by the profile name and the reply bar. The safe zone guides shade roughly the top 14% and the bottom 20% of tall formats and a thin margin on others. Keep text, faces and logos inside the clear area. Profile photos are shown as circles, so keep the face in the middle. The guides are approximate and not official, so test with a real post when it matters.

Keeping images sharp

  • Start big. A larger original gives sharper crops. Avoid screenshots of photos.
  • Avoid upscaling. The tool warns you when it must enlarge your photo.
  • Pick the right format. JPG for photos, PNG for text and logos.
  • Keep quality at 85% to 95%. Lower values make files small but may show blocks.
  • Do not recompress repeatedly. Resize from the original each time.
  • Upload at the stated size. Apps compress large files again.

Metadata, privacy and the right to use an image

Photos from a phone often contain EXIF data, including the date, the camera and sometimes the exact GPS position. Because the tool re-draws the image on a canvas, the downloaded files do not contain that data, which helps protect your privacy when you post. The processing happens in your browser, and nothing is uploaded. Make sure you have the right to use the photo: images you did not create or license may be protected by copyright, and photos of people may need their consent.

Tips for each platform

  • Instagram. Portrait 4:5 takes the most space in the feed. Use 9:16 for Stories and Reels, with text inside the safe zone.
  • Facebook. The cover photo is cropped differently on mobile, so keep the subject centered.
  • X. The header sits behind the profile photo at the lower left. Keep the important part to the right and center.
  • LinkedIn. Personal banners are wide and thin, so use simple images with little text.
  • YouTube. Keep thumbnails bold and readable at small size, and leave the bottom-right corner clear for the time badge.
  • Pinterest. Tall 2:3 pins get the most room in the feed.

Limits of this tool

Large photos use a lot of memory, and very big images may be slow or fail on a phone. Animated GIFs are saved as a single still image. The tool does not edit colors, add text or remove backgrounds. Presets are sizes, not guarantees: platforms may crop or compress differently from how they did when this page was written. If a size is missing, use the closest one or ask us to add it.

Frequently asked questions

What size should a social media image be?

It depends on the platform and the placement. For example, an Instagram portrait post is 1080 x 1350, a YouTube thumbnail is 1280 x 720 and an X header is 1500 x 500. The tool lists 30 common sizes.

How do I resize a photo for Instagram, Facebook and X at once?

Choose your photo, tick the sizes you want and download. Each size is cropped from the same photo using one focus point, and you can get all of them in a single ZIP file.

What is the focus point?

The area of your photo that must stay visible. Click or drag on the photo preview to set it. When the tool crops to a new shape, it keeps that area in view.

What is the difference between fill, fit and stretch?

Fill crops the photo to cover the whole size, with no empty space. Fit shows the whole photo and fills the rest with a blurred copy or a color. Stretch changes the proportions and can distort people, so use it rarely.

Will my image lose quality?

Cropping and shrinking keeps images sharp. Enlarging a small photo makes it soft, and the tool tells you which sizes were upscaled so you can use a larger original.

Does it upload my photo?

No. The image is processed in your browser and is never sent to a server.

What are safe zone guides?

They show areas where an app may place buttons or crop the image, such as the top and bottom of a Story. Keep text and faces inside the clear area. The guides are approximate.

Which format should I download?

JPG is smallest and best for photos. PNG keeps sharp text and logos and supports transparency, but files are larger. WebP is small but not accepted everywhere.

What file size do platforms allow?

Limits differ, and often are a few megabytes. JPG at 90% quality usually stays well within them. Check the app for its current limit.

Why do my photos look blurry after upload?

Apps recompress images. Upload at the recommended size, avoid screenshots of screenshots and use JPG or PNG at high quality to give the app the best starting point.

Can I resize several photos?

Process one photo at a time. Choose a new file after downloading the ZIP and keep the same size selection.

Does it remove metadata?

Yes. Re-drawing the image removes EXIF data such as GPS location and camera details, which is good for privacy. The photo is also rotated to match how it looks.

What are the best sizes for a profile photo?

Use a square image of at least 400 x 400 and keep your face in the middle, since most apps show a circle crop.

What size is a YouTube thumbnail?

1280 x 720 pixels, a 16:9 ratio, and a file under the limit shown in YouTube Studio. It is included in the preset list.

What size is an Open Graph or link preview image?

1200 x 630 pixels is the common size for the image shown when a link is shared on Facebook, LinkedIn, Slack and other apps.

Is the tool free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up, no watermark and no limit on downloads.

Do platforms change these sizes?

Sometimes. Treat the sizes as the best current guidance and check the app if a crop looks wrong.
