What this free YouTube thumbnail downloader does

Every YouTube video has a preview image, called the thumbnail, and YouTube stores it in several sizes. The site shows only a small version, and there is no download button in the video page. This free YouTube thumbnail downloader turns any video link into links to all the sizes, shows each one with its pixel dimensions, tells you which ones exist, and lets you save them as JPG, PNG or WebP.

It also goes further than most downloaders. You can paste up to 25 links at once, get every available size in a ZIP file, copy the links, see how the best thumbnail looks in a video list with your own title and length, and test a thumbnail of your own against YouTube's size, ratio and file-size guidance. The page runs in your browser, and your links are not sent to Utiliby.

How to download a YouTube thumbnail

  1. Copy the video link. Use the address bar, the Share button or the video ID.
  2. Paste it into the box. Add more links on new lines for a bulk download.

The 640 x 480 and 480 x 360 sizes have a 4:3 shape, so YouTube adds black bars at the top and bottom of a 16:9 image. Only the 1280 x 720 and 320 x 180 sizes are true 16:9. If you want a clean widescreen image, use maximum resolution or the 320 x 180 size.

How to find a video ID and the thumbnail address

A video ID is the 11 characters after v= in a watch link, after youtu.be/ in a short link, or after /shorts/ in a Shorts link. For the video at youtube.com/watch?v=dQw4w9WgXcQ, the ID is dQw4w9WgXcQ. The thumbnail images are stored at addresses that follow the same pattern: the image server, the letters vi, the ID and the size name, for example i.ytimg.com/vi/ID/maxresdefault.jpg. This tool builds those addresses for you, checks which ones return a real image and shows the result, so you never have to edit a link by hand.

Why some sizes are missing

Not every video has every size. Maximum resolution is created only when the uploaded thumbnail, or the video, is large enough, so older videos and videos that use an automatic frame often lack it. Private, deleted or region-blocked videos have no public thumbnail at all. When a size is missing, YouTube may return a small gray placeholder instead of an error, so the tool measures the returned image and marks it as not available if it is only 120 x 90. Use the next size down in that case.

Common legitimate uses for thumbnails

  • Your own videos. Save the thumbnail you uploaded, or recover it if you lost the source file.
  • Reference and research. Study layout, text size and color use across a niche, without copying anything.
  • Commentary and criticism. Use a small image to identify a video you are reviewing, where fair use or fair dealing applies.
  • Archiving and documentation. Keep a record of your channel's design over time.
  • Blog and slide references. Use thumbnails with permission or a license, with credit.

Copyright, permission and fair use

A thumbnail is a creative work, usually owned by the channel that uploaded it, and sometimes it includes photos licensed from others. Viewing it on YouTube does not give you the right to republish it. For your own channel there is no problem. For someone else's, ask for permission, check for a license, or rely on a lawful exception such as quotation, commentary or news reporting, where it applies in your country. Credit the source, do not suggest that the creator endorses you, do not edit the image to mislead and do not use it as your own thumbnail. This is general information and not legal advice.

What makes a good thumbnail

  • Use the right size. 1280 x 720 pixels, with a 16:9 ratio and at least 640 pixels wide.
  • Keep it readable when small. Most viewers see a thumbnail the size of a postage stamp, so use large, high-contrast text of a few words.
  • Leave the corner clear. The video length badge covers the bottom-right corner.
  • Show one clear subject. A face or a single object reads faster than a busy scene.
  • Match the title. The two should work together, not repeat each other.
  • Be honest. A thumbnail that misleads gets clicks but loses trust and watch time.
  • Keep the file small. Stay under YouTube's file size limit.

The Check my thumbnail tab tests your image against these points and shows it next to a sample title and length badge, so you can see what viewers will see.

Testing your own thumbnail before you upload

Choose an image file and the checker reads its width, height, ratio, format and weight. It reports whether it is a JPG, PNG, GIF or BMP, whether it is at least 640 pixels wide, whether it is exactly 1280 x 720, whether the ratio is 16:9 and whether it is under 2 MB. YouTube has changed its limits over the years, so confirm the current rules in YouTube Studio. The preview next to a title and a duration badge lets you see whether text is still readable at list size and whether the badge hides anything important.

Troubleshooting

  • No thumbnails appear. Check the link. Private, deleted or very new videos may not have thumbnails yet.
  • Everything says not available. The images may be blocked by an extension or a network. Try another browser or network.
  • The image opens instead of downloading. Right-click or long-press and choose Save image.
  • PNG or WebP conversion fails. Switch to JPG, which needs no conversion.
  • The picture has black bars. The 640 x 480 and 480 x 360 sizes have bars. Use maximum resolution.

Frequently asked questions

How do I download a YouTube thumbnail?

Paste the video link into the box. Every available size appears with its resolution. Press Download under the size you want, or use the ZIP button to get all of them.

What is the best thumbnail size?

The largest, 1280 x 720 pixels, called maximum resolution. It exists when the video owner uploaded a 720p or larger image. Otherwise the best you can get is 640 x 480 or 480 x 360.

Why does maximum resolution say not available?

The creator did not upload a high-resolution thumbnail, or the video is very old, or the video is private or removed. In that case use the next size down.

Can I download a thumbnail from a Shorts link?

Yes. Paste a Shorts link or the video ID. The tool finds the same set of thumbnails, although Shorts often use a frame from the video.

Which links does it accept?

Standard watch links, youtu.be short links, Shorts, embed, live and mobile links, plus a bare 11-character video ID. You can paste many at once, one per line.

Can I download in PNG or WebP?

Yes. Choose the format before pressing Download. The tool converts the image in your browser. JPG is the original format and is the fastest.

Why did the image open in a new tab instead of downloading?

Your browser did not allow a direct save. Right-click or long-press the opened image and choose Save image.

Is it legal to download a thumbnail?

A thumbnail is the property of the video's owner. Downloading your own is fine. Using someone else's may need permission, unless a legal exception such as fair use or fair dealing applies. This is not legal advice.

Do you save my links or the images?

No. The links are handled in your browser, and the images load directly from YouTube to your device. Nothing is uploaded to Utiliby.

Can I download thumbnails from private videos?

No. Private and deleted videos have no public thumbnail, so the sizes show as not available.

What are the three auto-generated frames?

Small frames that YouTube takes from different points in the video. They are 120 x 90 pixels and are mostly useful for picking a frame for your own custom thumbnail.

What size should my own YouTube thumbnail be?

1280 x 720 pixels with a 16:9 ratio, at least 640 pixels wide, as a JPG, PNG or GIF, and small in file size. Use the Check my thumbnail tab to test your image.

Does the checker upload my image?

No. It reads the image in your browser and reports size, ratio, format and file weight.

Can I use this on my phone?

Yes. The page works on phones and tablets. On some phones the image opens in a new tab and you save it from there.

Does it work for other video sites?

No. It works only with YouTube links and video IDs.

Is the thumbnail the same as the video quality?

No. The thumbnail is a separate image. The video can be 4K and the thumbnail still 480 x 360 if that was the uploaded size.
