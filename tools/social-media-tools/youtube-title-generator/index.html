What this free YouTube title generator does

A video title does two jobs at once. It tells YouTube what the video is about, and it tells a person scrolling past why the video is worth their time. Writing one that does both is harder than it sounds. This free online YouTube title generator gives you 20, 30 or 40 ideas from your topic in ten proven formulas, including how-to, numbered, curiosity, question, review, comparison, beginner, challenge, story, news and bracketed titles. Each one is scored on length, keyword placement, numbers, power words and structure.

A second tab checks a title you wrote yourself. It shows the score, the length against the 100-character limit, a list of strengths and fixes, and previews of how the title will appear on mobile and desktop. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing is uploaded.

Titles and YouTube search

YouTube search reads the title, the description, the tags, the spoken words and the viewer's behavior. The title carries the most weight of the written fields, so it should contain the main phrase a person would type. Put it early, keep the wording natural and avoid stuffing several keywords into one title. To find phrases, type your topic into the YouTube search bar and note the suggestions, which come from real searches. Use a related tool on this site, such as the YouTube tag generator and the thumbnail downloader, to research competitors and tidy your video details.

Click-through rate, thumbnails and titles

Click-through rate is the share of people who see your video and click it. A good title raises it by making a clear promise. Titles and thumbnails should work together: let the thumbnail show an emotion or a result in a few words, and let the title carry the keyword and the detail. Do not repeat the same words in both. Check the click-through rate and average view duration in YouTube Studio. A high click-through rate with a low view duration usually means the title promised more than the video gave.

Honest titles, clickbait and policy

There is a difference between a curious title and a deceptive one. A curious title makes the viewer want the answer, and the video delivers it. A deceptive title promises something that never appears. YouTube's policies restrict misleading titles, thumbnails and descriptions, and viewers who feel tricked leave and do not come back. Use curiosity and urgency, but make sure the video keeps the promise. The checker flags common clickbait phrases and loud formatting such as many all-caps words and repeated exclamation marks.

Title length and what viewers see

YouTube allows 100 characters, but not all of them are visible. Mobile feeds show about 60 characters, desktop search about 70 and suggested videos fewer. Because of that, treat the first 60 characters as the real title and the rest as a bonus. The preview in this tool marks where the cut-off would fall. If your best words come after the cut, rewrite the title so they come first, or shorten it.

How to improve a weak title

  • Move the keyword to the front. Start with the phrase people search for.
  • Add a specific benefit. Replace tips with 7 tips to save an hour.
  • Cut filler. Words such as really, very and just rarely add value.
  • Test two versions. Change one element at a time and compare results.
  • Read it aloud. If it sounds like a headline you would click, it is probably working.

Titles for Shorts, streams and series

Shorts titles can be brief because the video loops quickly and the algorithm leans on watch behavior more than on text. Aim for a clear hook of about 40 characters. For live streams, put the topic and the time first, then the host. For a series, keep a consistent pattern, such as Episode numbers at the end, so viewers recognize the set and playlists look tidy.

Privacy, accuracy and responsible use

The generator works in your browser from templates and your topic, and nothing is stored or sent. The titles are suggestions and the score is a rule of thumb, so judge each title on its merits. Make sure the title is true, that you have the right to any names or brands you mention and that you follow YouTube's policies on misleading metadata, sponsored content and sensitive subjects. Platform features and interfaces change, so verify details in YouTube Studio.

Testing and improving titles over time

A title is a hypothesis about what your audience wants. The only way to learn if it works is to compare. Watch the click-through rate and the average view duration of each video in YouTube Studio during the first days and again after a few weeks, and note the pattern across videos rather than judging one result. If your channel has access to features that test titles or thumbnails, use them. If not, change one element at a time, such as moving the keyword to the front or adding a number, and give the new version enough time before you decide. Keep a simple sheet with the old title, the new title, the date and the result. After ten or twenty videos you will see which formulas your audience prefers, and you can use that list to set the order in which you try the ideas from this generator.

Do not change titles too often. Each change resets some of the signals the video has built up, and constant edits can confuse regular viewers. A sensible rule is to review a video after it has had at least one full week of impressions, to change it only if the click-through rate is clearly below your channel average and to leave strong performers alone.

Frequently asked questions

What is a YouTube title generator?

A tool that suggests video titles from your topic. This one uses ten proven formulas such as how-to, numbered lists, questions, reviews and comparisons, then scores each title on length, keyword placement, numbers and power words.

How do I use this free YouTube title generator online?

Type your topic or main keyword, choose a video type, and the titles appear. Click a title to see it as a video card, copy the ones you like, or paste your own title in the Check tab to get a score.

How long can a YouTube title be?

100 characters. About 60 to 70 are visible in many places, such as mobile feeds and desktop search results, so the most important words should come first.

What is the best length for a YouTube title?

Between 30 and 60 characters works for most videos. It is long enough to carry a keyword and a benefit and short enough to display in full on a phone.

Where should the keyword go?

Near the front. This helps viewers and search understand the topic at a glance and keeps the keyword visible when the title is cut off.

Does the score predict views?

No. It is a rule-of-thumb check that rewards a good length, an early keyword, a number, a power word and clear structure. Real performance depends on the thumbnail, the audience, the topic and the video itself.

What is a power word?

A word that adds emotion or clarity, such as easy, best, mistakes, secret, free, proven or beginner. Use them where they are true, and do not overpromise.

Should I use numbers in titles?

Often yes. Numbers set expectations and make the content easy to scan, as in 7 Tips or 5 Mistakes. Use the number you actually deliver.

Is clickbait bad?

A curious title is fine, but a misleading one is not. If the title promises something the video does not deliver, viewers leave early, which hurts the video, and YouTube's policies restrict deceptive metadata.

Should I put the year in the title?

For content that changes, such as guides, reviews and software, a year signals freshness. Avoid it for timeless videos, because you will have to update it.

What are the brackets for?

Square brackets and parentheses add context, such as Full Course or 2026 Guide, without lengthening the main phrase. They can improve clarity and click-through.

Title Case or sentence case?

Both work. Title Case looks formal and is common on YouTube, and sentence case feels conversational. Pick one style and use it across the channel.

How do I write a title for a Short?

Keep it brief and put the hook first. Shorts can use hashtags in the title, but a clear phrase of 40 characters or less is usually enough.

Does the title matter more than the thumbnail?

They work as a pair. The thumbnail catches the eye, and the title adds the promise and the keyword. Do not repeat the same words in both.

Is my text uploaded?

No. The titles are built in your browser.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up.

Can I change a title after publishing?

Yes. If a video underperforms, test a new title and thumbnail after a few weeks and compare the click-through rate in YouTube Studio.
