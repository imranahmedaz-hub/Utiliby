What this free YouTube tag generator does

Tags are the small keywords you can attach to a YouTube video in the details section. They are not shown on the watch page, yet creators spend a lot of time on them. A YouTube tag generator speeds up the work by suggesting phrases around your topic. This free online tool builds up to forty tags from your main keyword and related words in several patterns, including how-to, tutorial, for beginners, tips, best, review, year and question formats, and it adds a few broad terms that suit your type of video.

You stay in control. Every tag is a button that you can switch on or off, a meter shows how much of the 500-character total you have used and one click selects the tags that fit. A second tab cleans a list you already have by counting characters, removing duplicates and flagging problems. The tool runs in your browser, with no sign-up and no upload.

How to use the tag generator

  1. Enter your topic. The phrase a viewer would search, such as meal prep.
  2. Add related keywords. Optional words such as healthy or chicken create extra tags.
  3. Pick the video type. How-to, list, review, vlog or news adjusts the patterns.
  4. Choose your tags. Click tags to include or remove them and watch the meter.
  5. Copy. Paste the comma-separated list into YouTube Studio.
  6. Add hashtags. Use one to three of the hashtag ideas in your title or description.

How much do tags matter?

YouTube's own guidance says that tags play a minor role in how your video is discovered. They help most when a word in your topic is often misspelled. What matters far more is the title, the thumbnail, the description, the spoken words and captions, and how viewers react: whether they click, watch and return. This is why the tool does not promise ranking. Treat tags as housekeeping that supports your main work, and do not spend hours on them.

The 500-character limit

All tags together may not exceed 500 characters. YouTube counts commas between tags, and tags that contain a space are counted as if they were in quotes, so each of those adds two characters. A list of twenty long phrases fills the limit fast, which is why the meter in this tool uses the same rule. If you go over, remove the least relevant tags from the end rather than shortening the main one. The Fit to 500 button keeps the highest-priority tags in order and drops the rest.

How to choose good tags

  • Start with the exact topic. Your main keyword as the first tag.
  • Add natural variations. How to, for beginners, tips, tutorial.
  • Describe the video honestly. Every tag should be true.
  • Include a few broad terms. They give context, such as tutorial or review.
  • Add common misspellings sparingly. Only if people really make them.
  • Keep it short. Fifteen focused tags beat fifty vague ones.

Tags versus hashtags

Tags are hidden. Hashtags are visible and clickable. A hashtag in your title or description makes the video appear on a page of videos with that hashtag, and the first three appear above the title. Use one to three that are relevant. If you add more than 15 hashtags, YouTube ignores all of them. The generator suggests three hashtags from your topic to start with. Tags do not use a hash sign, so avoid typing them into the tags field.

Researching tags and keywords

To find phrases people really search, type your topic into the YouTube search bar and note the suggestions, then do the same on Google. Look at the titles of top videos on your topic, and use YouTube Studio's search analytics to see which queries lead to your channel. Write down a short list of exact phrases and use them in your title, description, spoken script and tags. Common tag mistakes

  • Irrelevant popular tags. They mislead viewers and can violate policy.
  • Keyword stuffing. Dozens of near-identical tags add nothing.
  • Using the # sign. Hashtags belong in the title or description.
  • Ignoring the title. Tags cannot rescue a weak title.
  • Never updating. Revisit old videos that still get traffic.

Privacy, accuracy and responsible use

The generator works in your browser and does not store or send your keywords. The tags are template-based suggestions, so check each one for accuracy and relevance. YouTube may change how tags work and how they are counted, and this tool does not connect to your account. Follow YouTube's policies on spam, deceptive practices and scams, which cover misleading metadata, and do not use another creator's brand name in tags unless your video is truly about it.

A worked example of a tag set

Take a video titled How to Meal Prep for the Week (Beginner Guide). A sensible tag set starts with the exact topic: meal prep. It continues with close variations that match what people type: how to meal prep, meal prep for beginners, meal prep tutorial and meal prep ideas. Add one or two related terms such as healthy meals or weekly meal plan, and a broad word or two like tutorial. That is about ten tags and well under 150 characters, far less than the 500 allowed. Adding thirty more near-duplicates would not help, and unrelated popular tags would only risk a policy problem.

Updating tags on existing videos

Tags are easy to change after publishing, which makes them a good small task for older videos. Review videos that still get steady traffic and compare their title and description with what viewers now search. If the topic has a new common name, a new year or a popular alternative phrase, add it to the tags and the description. If a video earns views for a query you did not expect, as shown in the search report of YouTube Studio, add that phrase as a tag and mention it in the description in a natural way. Remove tags that no longer describe the video. Keep a log of what you changed and when, so you can see later if a change was followed by more search traffic.

Frequently asked questions

What is a YouTube tag generator?

A tool that suggests tags for a video from your topic. This one builds up to forty ideas from templates and your keywords, lets you pick which to keep and checks them against the 500-character total.

How do I use this free YouTube tag generator online?

Type your video topic and optional related keywords, choose a video type and click tags to include or remove them. Press Fit to 500 characters, then copy the tags into YouTube Studio under More options.

Do YouTube tags still matter?

YouTube says tags play a minor role in discovery, mostly for commonly misspelled words. Titles, thumbnails, descriptions and the content itself matter much more. Tags are still worth adding accurately.

How many tags should I use?

There is no ideal number. Use the ones that describe the video well, with the main keyword first, and stay within the 500-character limit. Five to fifteen focused tags are common.

What is the character limit for tags?

The combined length of all tags is limited to 500 characters. Tags with spaces are counted with quotes around them, so each adds two characters, and commas count too. The counter applies this rule.

Where do I add tags?

In YouTube Studio, open the video, choose Show more or More options and find Tags. Paste the comma-separated list there.

Should the first tag be my main keyword?

Yes. Putting the exact phrase first is a common practice and keeps the most important term clear.

Should I use hashtags or tags?

They are different. Tags are hidden metadata. Hashtags in the title or description are visible and clickable. Use up to three hashtags, since YouTube ignores all of them if there are more than 15.

Should tags start with #?

No. Tags are plain words or phrases. The # sign belongs to hashtags in the title or description.

Is it a good idea to copy competitors' tags?

Use them only if they describe your video accurately. Misleading tags can break YouTube's policies on deceptive metadata.

Should I use long phrases or single words?

Mix them. Specific phrases such as how to meal prep for beginners describe the video, and a few broad words give context.

Can I include misspellings?

YouTube suggests tags are most useful for commonly misspelled words in your topic. Add one or two if the misspelling is common, and avoid filling the field with errors.

Can I use tags from this tool for Shorts?

Yes. Shorts also have tags, but the title, description and hashtags matter more.

What does Fit to 500 characters do?

It selects tags in order of priority until the limit is reached and deselects the rest.

Is my text uploaded?

No. Everything runs in your browser.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up.

Can I clean tags I already have?

Yes. Use the second tab to paste your tags, remove duplicates, count characters and see warnings.
