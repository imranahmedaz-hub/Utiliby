What this free engagement rate calculator does

An engagement rate calculator turns raw counts of likes, comments, shares and saves into one percentage that tells you how strongly your audience responds. This free online tool supports four formulas: engagement by followers, by reach, by impressions and by views. It shows the result on a gauge from low to excellent for your platform and account size, explains how many more interactions you would need to reach the next level and flags warning signs.

It also includes a compare accounts tab for measuring yourself against competitors and an analyze posts tab that scores each post individually, with average, median, best, weakest and a chart. You can copy the results, download a CSV or print a clean summary. Everything is calculated in your browser, so you never share a password or an account connection.

How to use the engagement rate calculator

  1. Choose the platform and formula. By followers is the standard way to compare accounts.
  2. Enter totals or averages. Use totals for several posts and tell the tool how many, or enter averages for a single post.
  3. Fill in the interactions. Likes, comments, shares and saves. Leave blank what you cannot see.
  4. Read the gauge. It places your rate between low and excellent for your account size.
  5. Try the other tabs. Compare accounts or paste a list of posts to see which ones perform best.

The four engagement rate formulas

FormulaCalculationBest for
ER by followersInteractions per post ÷ followers × 100Comparing accounts and tracking over time
ER by reachInteractions ÷ accounts reached × 100Judging how a single post performed with its viewers
ER by impressionsInteractions ÷ impressions × 100Ads and paid posts
ER by viewsInteractions ÷ video views × 100Video, Reels, TikTok and YouTube

The right choice depends on what you want to learn. A follower-based rate is easy to find for any public account, because follower counts and likes are visible, which is why it is the common way to compare brands and influencers. Reach and impressions require access to the account's insights, but they describe the real audience of each post better, because not every follower sees every post.

Engagement rate benchmarks by platform

The calculator rates your result using rough thresholds for ER by followers, and for ER by views on YouTube. Thresholds are for an account of 10,000 to 100,000 followers. How to improve your engagement rate

  • Study your best posts. The analyze tab shows which formats and topics work. Make more of them.
  • Open with a hook. The first line or the first two seconds decides whether people stop.
  • Ask for one action. A question or a save prompt gives people something easy to do.
  • Reply to comments quickly. Early replies encourage conversation.
  • Post when your audience is online. Use your own insights rather than a generic best time.
  • Use formats the platform favors. Carousels, Reels, short video and polls are often shown to more people.
  • Be consistent. A steady schedule keeps your audience warm.
  • Clean up inactive followers. A real audience makes the rate more meaningful.

Comparing accounts and competitors fairly

When you compare two accounts, use the same formula and the same type of posts. A video-heavy account will usually have a different rate from a photo account. Smaller accounts have higher rates, so a 4% rate on 2,000 followers and a 1% rate on 500,000 followers do not mean the larger account is weaker, since the larger account may have far more total interactions. The compare tab shows both the rate and the interactions per post. For public accounts, estimate averages from the last 10 to 20 posts rather than the latest few.

Reading a post-by-post analysis

An average hides the story. A single viral post can lift the average and mask a weak typical result, so the analyzer also gives the median, which is the middle post. Compare the best and weakest post: what do the best have in common? Look at the format, the topic, the day, the length of the caption and the first line. If most posts are near the median and a few are far above, copy the traits of the outliers. If results are all over the place, test one change at a time.

What engagement rate cannot tell you

Engagement rate shows reaction, not results. A post can get many likes and no sales, and a post with few interactions can bring valuable customers. The rate does not show whether the audience is the right one, and interactions can be inflated by giveaways or engagement pods. Platforms count interactions differently, and some hide counts or do not show saves and shares for other accounts, so figures for a competitor are incomplete. Use engagement rate together with reach, clicks, sign-ups and sales.

Privacy, limits and responsible use

The calculator does only arithmetic on numbers you type. It does not connect to any social network, ask for a password, store your data or send it anywhere, and the print and CSV options contain only what is on screen. Benchmarks are estimates. Do not buy followers or engagement to improve your numbers, since that breaks platform rules and gives you a misleading picture. If you publish a comparison of other accounts, use only public information and be fair and accurate.

Frequently asked questions

What is engagement rate?

It is the percentage of your audience that interacts with your content. You add up likes, comments, shares and saves and divide by a base such as followers, reach, impressions or views.

How do I calculate engagement rate?

Add the interactions, divide by the base and multiply by 100. For example, 700 interactions on a post from an account with 12,500 followers is 5.6%. The calculator does it for you for any of four formulas.

What is a good engagement rate on Instagram?

Around 1% to 3% by followers is generally considered good for a mid-sized account, and averages across the platform are often reported below 1%. Smaller accounts usually have higher rates, which the calculator takes into account.

What is a good engagement rate on TikTok?

TikTok rates are higher than other networks. Between 5% and 9% by followers is commonly seen as good, and a rate above that is excellent for most accounts.

Which formula should I use?

Use ER by followers to compare accounts, ER by reach to judge how well a post performed with people who saw it, ER by impressions for ad-like measurement, and ER by views for video content.

Why is my engagement rate by reach higher than by followers?

Reach is usually smaller than your follower count, because not every follower sees every post. A smaller base gives a larger percentage.

Should I include saves and shares?

Yes if the platform shows them to you. They are strong signals of value. If you do not have them, leave those boxes empty, and compare only with rates calculated the same way.

Do smaller accounts have higher engagement?

Usually. As an audience grows, a smaller share interacts with each post, so the calculator uses a higher benchmark for small accounts and a lower one for large accounts.

Can I compare myself with a competitor?

Yes. Use the Compare accounts tab. Enter followers and typical interactions per post, which you can read from public posts, and compare the rates.

What does the Analyze posts tab do?

It takes your posts, one per line, and shows the engagement rate of each, the average, the median, your best and weakest posts and a bar chart, so you can see which formats work.

How many posts should I average?

At least 10 to 20 recent posts. A single viral post or a flop can distort a small sample, so the median is a good second check.

Is a low engagement rate bad?

Not necessarily. It is a signal to look at your content, posting times and audience. Rates also fall naturally as an account grows.

Can bought followers affect my rate?

Yes. Fake or inactive followers increase the base without adding interactions, which lowers your rate. Real audiences give a more useful number.

Are the benchmarks official?

No. They are my own rough estimates, based on public industry reports and scaled by account size. Platforms do not publish an official benchmark.

Does it connect to my social account?

No. You type the numbers yourself. It never asks for a password and never connects to a social network.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up.
