What this free social media character limits checker does

Every network has its own limits, and they are easy to forget. Is a TikTok bio 80 characters or 150? How long can a YouTube title be before it is cut? Does a LinkedIn headline allow 120 or 220? This free online social media character limits checker answers all of these in one table. Paste a bio, a caption, a title or a description and see at once which fields it fits, how many characters you have left and where it is too long.

The table covers more than sixty fields on fifteen networks and services, from Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook, YouTube and LinkedIn to Pinterest, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon, Reddit, Snapchat, Twitch and Discord, plus search results and SMS. You can filter by platform, search for a field, show only the ones that fit or only the ones that do not, sort by limit and export the result. It all runs in your browser.

Limit versus what people see

A limit is the most you can write. It is not the amount people read. Feeds cut long text and show a more link, so the first line carries most of the weight. Instagram shows about 125 characters of a caption. LinkedIn shows about 210. YouTube search results show perhaps 60 to 70 characters of a title, and Pinterest feeds show about the first 40 characters of a pin title. The notes beside each row in the table remind you of these cut-offs. Write the main message first and treat the rest as extra.

How characters are counted

Most networks count characters as the number of visible symbols, so that letters, digits, punctuation, spaces and line breaks each count as one. Emoji usually count as one visible character in the checker, although some apps count them as two or more. X is the main exception: it uses weighted counting, with links as 23 and emoji and characters from Chinese, Japanese and Korean as two. Text pasted from a word processor may contain hidden characters, and special Unicode fonts use characters that count the same but may be read differently by screen readers.

How to shorten text without losing meaning

  • Lead with the point. Remove introductions and greetings.
  • Use short words. Use rather than utilize, help rather than assist.
  • Drop filler. Very, really, just and actually rarely add value.
  • Use numerals. 5 uses fewer characters than five.
  • Move detail elsewhere. Put it in a link, a comment, a thread or an image.
  • Avoid unusual abbreviations. They save little and confuse readers.

Search and SMS lengths

Two rows cover things that are not social networks but matter for sharing. Search engines show about 60 characters of a page title and about 155 of a meta description, though the real cut-off depends on pixel width. Text messages are 160 characters in the standard alphabet and 70 with emoji or non-Latin characters, and longer ones are split into parts. These are display lengths and carrier behavior, not strict limits, so treat them as targets.

Why limits change

Platforms adjust limits to support new features or to shape behavior. X offered longer posts to paying users, TikTok raised the caption limit from 2,200 to 4,000, and LinkedIn changes the limits for headlines and posts from time to time. Some changes roll out to a share of accounts first, so you might see a different limit from a friend. If the app lets you type more or less than the table says, the app is right. Check the platform's help center for the latest information.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming one size fits all. A caption for Instagram is not a post for X.
  • Forgetting the first line. Feeds cut the rest.
  • Ignoring name and bio limits. A truncated bio loses its call to action.
  • Trusting old tables. Limits change, so verify.
  • Counting words instead of characters. Limits are in characters.

Privacy, accuracy and responsible use

The checker works in your browser and does not store or send your text. The limits come from public documentation and general reports, so they can be out of date or differ by account type, country or app version. This tool is independent and has no connection to any platform. Always check the final post in the app, follow each platform's rules and respect copyright and disclosure requirements.

Adapting one message for many platforms

The same idea needs a different shape on each network. Start with the shortest version, the one that fits the tightest limit you care about, and expand from there. A message written for X can be reused as the first line of a LinkedIn post, the hook of an Instagram caption or the title of a YouTube video. Add detail only where the space and the audience allow.

Frequently asked questions

What is a social media character limits checker?

A tool that compares your text with the limits of many fields at once. This free one covers about sixty fields on fifteen networks and services, from Instagram bios to YouTube descriptions, and shows which ones your text fits.

How do I use this free character limits checker online?

Paste your text. The table shows, for each field, the limit, the characters used, the characters left and a fill bar. Filter by platform or search for a field such as bio or title.

How many characters can an Instagram bio and caption have?

As we understand current rules, 150 for a bio and 2,200 for a caption. The feed shows only about the first 125 characters of a caption before more.

What is the limit for a TikTok caption?

4,000 characters according to current public information, raised from 2,200 in 2025. The bio limit is 80 characters.

What is the character limit for X?

280 for a standard post and 25,000 for Premium posts. Links count as 23 characters and emoji count as 2, which the checker applies to the X post rows.

What are the YouTube limits?

100 characters for a title, 5,000 for a description, 500 in total for tags and 10,000 for a comment. Only the start of a title and description is visible in many views.

What are the LinkedIn limits?

3,000 for a post, 220 for a headline, 2,600 for the About section and 1,250 for a comment, as we understand current rules.

Do spaces and emoji count?

Yes on nearly all platforms. Spaces and line breaks count as characters, and emoji count as one visible character, except on X where they count as two.

Are the limits always accurate?

No. Platforms change limits, test them on some accounts and have different rules for business, premium or older accounts. Use the table as a guide and confirm in the app.

Why does the checker count differently from the app?

Apps may count some characters differently, for example links, emoji or special scripts. If the count differs, trust the app.

What is the SMS row for?

A single text message holds 160 standard characters or 70 if it contains emoji or non-Latin characters. Longer messages are split.

What are the Google title and description rows?

These are the display lengths in search results, about 60 characters for a title and 155 for a description. They are not hard limits.

Can I check only one platform?

Yes. Use the platform menu or the search box. The filter can also show only the fields where your text fits, or only where it is too long.

Can I export the table?

Yes. Download the CSV for a spreadsheet, copy the results as text or print the summary.

Is my text uploaded?

No. It is checked in your browser.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up.

What should I do if my text is too long?

Cut filler words first, put the key message at the start and move details to a link or a comment. For long ideas, consider a thread on X or a carousel.
