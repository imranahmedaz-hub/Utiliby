What this free content calendar generator does

Most people do not fail at social media because they run out of talent. They fail because every morning they have to decide what to post. A content calendar solves that by deciding in advance. This free online generator builds a 7, 14 or 30-day plan from your topic, your goal and the platforms you use, with a date, a pillar, a format, an idea and a caption starter for each post.

The plan is shown as a month-style grid and as a list that you can sort through. A chart shows the share of each pillar, so you can see at a glance whether the mix is balanced. You can swap any single idea, refresh the whole plan, and export it as a CSV for a spreadsheet, as an .ics file for your calendar app or as printed pages. Everything runs in your browser.

How to use the content calendar generator

  1. Describe your topic. Use a few specific words, such as sourdough baking or freelance design.
  2. Pick dates and a rhythm. Choose a start date, the number of days and how many days a week you will post.
  3. Choose platforms. Tick every network you use. Each posting day is adapted to each platform.
  4. Set your goal and promotion level. Awareness, engagement, sales and leads change the call to action. The promotion level changes how many posts sell.
  5. Review the plan. Look at the calendar and the pillar chart. Swap ideas that do not fit.
  6. Export. Download the CSV or .ics, copy the text or print it.

How often to post

Consistency matters more than volume. A plan you can keep for six months will beat a daily schedule that you abandon after two weeks. Three posting days a week suit most small businesses and creators, and five suit teams with a person who owns content. Daily posting suits a few accounts with strong capacity, such as news and short-video creators. If you use several platforms, reuse one idea across all of them, changing only the format, so that planning does not multiply your work.

Formats for each platform

The same idea needs a different shape on every network. On Instagram, carousels suit tips and Reels suit stories. On TikTok, short videos with a hook in the first seconds work best. LinkedIn favors text posts with a lesson or a story, and X favors short posts and threads. Facebook suits images, albums and community questions, YouTube suits how-to videos and Shorts, and Pinterest suits tall images and infographics that people save. The calendar suggests a format for each platform and pillar, and you can change it to fit your resources.

Batching and workflow

Once you have a plan, work in batches. Write all captions for a week in one session, film several videos on one day and design images in a block. Batching reduces the time spent switching between tasks and makes it easier to keep a consistent style. Keep a spreadsheet with columns for date, platform, status and link, and mark each post as drafted, scheduled or published. The CSV from this tool already has these columns, with a status column ready to update.

  • Write captions in one session.
  • Film or photograph in a batch.
  • Schedule posts with the platform's own tools or a scheduler.
  • Leave a slot each week for news or a trending topic.

When to post

The times in the plan are common starting points: late morning for Instagram, evening for TikTok and Pinterest, early morning on weekdays for LinkedIn and midday for X and Facebook. Studies of best times differ widely because audiences differ. Treat the times as your first experiment, then check your analytics after a few weeks to see when your own followers are active, and move your schedule to match. Time zones matter if your audience is in another region.

Measuring what works

At the end of each plan, look at the posts with the best reach, saves, shares and comments, and see which pillar and format they belong to. Do more of that. Look at the posts that did poorly too, and ask if the hook, the timing or the topic was the problem. Over several months a clear picture appears. You can use the engagement rate calculator on this site to compare posts fairly across accounts of different sizes.

Common content planning mistakes

  • Selling too often. Constant promotion makes people tune out.
  • Planning too far ahead. Leave room for news and feedback.
  • Copying the same post everywhere. Adapt the format to each platform.
  • No call to action. Tell people what to do next.
  • Ignoring results. Review and adjust every month.
  • Posting without disclosure. Label paid partnerships clearly.

Privacy, accuracy and responsible use

The calendar is built in your browser from templates, and your topic is not uploaded. The ideas are general, so check every claim before you publish, especially in areas such as health, finance and law, and make sure you have the right to use any photos, music or quotations. Mark paid posts and gifts clearly as required by advertising rules in your country. The suggested times are only starting points, and platform features change.

Frequently asked questions

What is a content calendar generator?

A tool that lays out what to post, where and when. This free one turns a topic, a goal and your chosen platforms into a dated plan with ideas, formats and caption starters.

How do I use this free content calendar generator online?

Enter your topic, pick a start date, length and platforms, then choose how many days a week you want to post. The plan appears as a calendar and a list. Use New ideas to refresh it, and Swap idea to change a single post.

What is a content pillar?

A content pillar is a recurring theme that you post about. This tool rotates six: educate, inspire and story, behind the scenes, social proof, promote and engage. A balanced mix keeps the feed varied and avoids constant selling.

How many times a week should I post?

Choose a rate you can keep up. Three posts a week with good quality usually beats daily posting that you abandon. You can start with three days and increase as your routine grows.

What is the 80/20 rule for content?

A common guideline is that about four of every five posts give value, such as tips, stories and entertainment, and one in five promotes your offer. The light, balanced and heavy settings change this ratio.

Does it write the posts for me?

It gives an idea, a format and a caption starter for each post, built from templates and your topic. You write the final post with your own details and voice.

Can I plan for several platforms at once?

Yes. Tick as many as you like. Each posting day uses one idea and adapts the format to each platform, such as a carousel on Instagram and a short video on TikTok, to make repurposing easy.

What are the suggested times based on?

They are general starting points for each platform, such as late morning on Instagram and early morning on LinkedIn. The best times depend on your audience, so check your own analytics and adjust.

Can I export the calendar?

Yes. Download a CSV that opens in Excel or Google Sheets, an .ics file that you can import into Google, Apple or Outlook calendars, copy it as text or print it.

How do I import the .ics file?

In Google Calendar choose Settings, then Import and export, and upload the file. In Apple Calendar use File, then Import. In Outlook use Open and Export, then Import.

Can I change an idea I do not like?

Yes. Press Swap idea in the list view to move that post to the next idea in the same pillar, or press New ideas to refresh the whole plan.

What should I do with the plan once I have it?

Copy it to your scheduling tool or spreadsheet, write the posts in batches, add images or video, and schedule them. Review results at the end of the period and keep what worked.

How far ahead should I plan?

Plan two to four weeks ahead. That is long enough to batch your work and short enough to react to news and feedback.

How do I choose a topic?

Write the thing you want to be known for in a few words, such as vegan baking or small business bookkeeping. A clear topic gives better ideas than a broad one.

Is my topic sent anywhere?

No. The plan is built in your browser.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free to use online and needs no account.

Can I use the plan for a business, a client or a personal account?

Yes. It works for any account. Check that your ideas and claims are accurate, and disclose partnerships and ads clearly.
