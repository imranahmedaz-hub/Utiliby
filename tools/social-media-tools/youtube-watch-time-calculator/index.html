What this YouTube watch time calculator does

Monetization on YouTube starts with numbers. Creators ask the same questions: how many hours do I have, how far am I from the target, and how long will it take? A YouTube watch time calculator answers them in one place. This free online tool compares your channel with both tiers of the YouTube Partner Program, draws your progress, projects the week you should reach each goal and works backwards from a deadline to the views you need.

It is more than a progress bar. The projection combines your weekly views, average view duration, subscriber rate and growth. The Shorts section shows the weekly views required for the 3 million and 10 million thresholds. The Plan tab turns a deadline into hours per week, views per week and views per video, and a table shows how a longer average view duration shrinks the number of views you need. Nothing is uploaded.

How to use the calculator

  1. Enter your numbers. Subscribers, public watch hours for the last 12 months, Shorts views for the last 90 days and public uploads for the last 90 days.
  2. Read your status. Bars show progress to each requirement, and a banner tells you which tier your numbers meet.
  3. Open Projection. Add your weekly views, average view duration, subscriber rate and growth. See the week each goal is reached on the chart.
  4. Open Plan a goal. Pick a target and a deadline to see hours, views per week and views per video.
  5. Save. Copy the summary, download a CSV or print the report.

How watch time is counted

Watch time is the total time viewers spend watching your public long-form videos, summed over the last 12 months. It is measured in hours. A video with 10,000 views and an average view duration of 3 minutes adds 500 hours. Private and unlisted videos do not count, and deleted videos lose their hours. Shorts do not add to the long-form watch hours total, which is why Shorts have their own views route.

The formula is simple: hours = views × average view duration in minutes ÷ 60. You can raise hours by getting more views, by raising the average view duration or by both. A 20 percent longer view duration is worth as much as 20 percent more views, and it often costs less effort because it comes from tighter editing.

How to raise watch time

  • Hook the first 30 seconds. State the payoff and show something early.
  • Cut dead air. Remove pauses, repetition and long intros.
  • Use chapters. They help viewers find value, which keeps them watching. See the YouTube timestamp generator.
  • Make playlists. A series keeps viewers moving from one video to the next.
  • Use end screens and cards. Point to the next video that fits.
  • Study retention. In Studio, find where viewers leave and fix the pattern.
  • Publish consistently. A steady rhythm builds the habit.

The Shorts route

Shorts views over any 90 days can qualify a channel instead of watch hours. The thresholds are large: 3 million for the first tier and 10 million for the full tier. The calculator converts them to weekly views, about 233,000 and 778,000, so that you can compare them with your own pace. Remember that subscribers are still required, and that Shorts pay a different share. Many channels mix both, using Shorts to bring subscribers and long videos to build hours.

A worked example

A channel has 320 subscribers, 1,450 watch hours and 4 uploads in 90 days. It gets about 4,000 views a week at a 4-minute average view duration, and gains 10 subscribers per 1,000 views, with growth of 3 percent a month. The status tab shows it is not yet eligible: subscribers are at 64 percent of 500 and hours at 48 percent of 3,000. The projection finds the week both targets are met for each tier, and the chart shows the line crossing 3,000 and then 4,000 hours. Raising the average view duration to 5 minutes or the growth to 5 percent moves both dates closer, and you can test each change by editing one number at a time.

Common mistakes when chasing monetization

  • Counting private or unlisted hours. They do not qualify.
  • Ignoring subscribers. Hours alone are not enough.
  • Forgetting the 12-month window. Old hours expire.
  • Buying views or subscribers. It breaks the rules and can end the channel.
  • Chasing length rather than value. Padding lowers retention.
  • Using other channels' averages. Use your own analytics.

Accuracy, limits and responsible use

This calculator works in your browser and stores nothing. It uses published thresholds and the numbers you enter. It cannot see your channel, and projections assume steady trends, which rarely hold. YouTube may change the thresholds, and it alone decides if a channel is accepted, so check your status in YouTube Studio. Never buy views, hours or subscribers, because it breaks YouTube's policies.

Frequently asked questions

What is a YouTube watch time calculator?

A tool that compares your subscribers, watch hours and Shorts views with the Partner Program thresholds, projects when you will reach them and works out the views per week you need.

How many watch hours do I need to get monetized on YouTube?

For the full ad revenue tier, 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months with 1,000 subscribers. For the first tier, 3,000 hours with 500 subscribers and three public uploads in 90 days.

Can I get monetized with Shorts views instead of watch hours?

Yes. YouTube's page lists 3 million public Shorts views in 90 days for the first tier and 10 million for the full tier as an alternative to watch hours. The subscriber requirement still applies.

How many subscribers do I need?

500 for the first tier and 1,000 for the full tier.

What counts as qualifying watch hours?

Public watch hours on your long-form videos in the last 12 months. Private and unlisted videos, and hours from Shorts, do not count toward the watch hours route.

How do I check my watch hours in YouTube Studio?

Open YouTube Studio, go to the Earn tab to see your progress, or use Analytics and set the range to the last 365 days and look at watch time.

How does the projection work?

It takes your weekly views, average view duration, subscriber rate and monthly growth, adds each week's new hours and subscribers and finds the first week both targets are met.

What is a good average view duration?

It depends on video length and topic. Raise it by hooking viewers in the first seconds and cutting slow sections. The plan tab shows how many views you need at each duration.

How many views do I need for 4,000 hours?

At a 4-minute average view duration you need 60,000 views for 4,000 hours. At 8 minutes you need 30,000. The table in the Plan tab shows more cases.

Do watch hours expire?

Yes. The requirement counts the last 12 months, so hours from older views drop out. The projection does not model this, so keep your pace steady.

Do live streams count?

Public live stream watch hours can count toward the long-form total. Check YouTube's current rules for details.

What else is needed besides numbers?

Your channel must follow YouTube's policies, have no active strikes and meet the other conditions on the Partner Program page, such as 2-step verification and availability in your country.

How long does it take to reach 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 hours?

It varies enormously. Use the projection tab with realistic numbers from your own analytics rather than averages from other channels.

What is a realistic subscriber rate?

It varies by niche and content. Check your own figures in Studio: subscribers gained divided by views, times 1,000. The default of 10 per 1,000 views is only a placeholder.

Is my data uploaded?

No. The calculator works in your browser and does not send your numbers anywhere.

Is this calculator free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up, and you can copy, download as CSV or print the report.

Is the result a guarantee of approval?

No. It is an estimate. YouTube reviews applications and alone decides eligibility, so check your status in YouTube Studio.
