The three percentage formulas

A percent is a fraction of 100. The word comes from the Latin per centum, meaning by the hundred. Every basic percentage problem links three quantities: the part, the whole and the percent. If you know two, you can find the third.

The partpercent ÷ 100 × whole15% of 240 = 0.15 × 240 = 36
The percentpart ÷ whole × 10036 of 160 = 36 ÷ 160 × 100 = 22.5%
The wholepart ÷ (percent ÷ 100)45 is 30% of what = 45 ÷ 0.30 = 150

Percent change, increase and decrease

Percent change measures how much something moved relative to where it started. The formula is (new − old) ÷ old × 100. If rent rises from 800 to 860, the change is 60 ÷ 800 × 100 = 7.5%. If a stock falls from 50 to 40, the change is −10 ÷ 50 × 100 = −20%. A positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease.

The starting value is always the base. That is why a rise followed by an equal fall does not return you to the start. Gain 50% on 100 and you have 150. Lose 50% of 150 and you have 75, which is 25% below where you began. To get back from 150 to 100 you need a fall of one third, or 33.3%. Understanding this asymmetry helps with investments, price changes and pay.

Percent difference versus percent change

Sometimes there is no before and after, only two numbers to compare, such as the price of the same item in two shops or the heights of two people. Percent difference treats the two values equally by dividing their gap by their average: |a − b| ÷ ((a + b) ÷ 2) × 100. For 80 and 100, the gap is 20 and the average is 90, so the difference is 22.2%. By contrast, percent change from 80 to 100 is 25%, and from 100 to 80 is −20%. Choose percent change when one value came first and percent difference when neither did.

Reverse percentages: finding the original value

A common puzzle is to find the original value when you only know the final one and the percent that was applied. A jacket costs 84 in a sale after 30% off. What was the original price? The sale price is 70% of the original, so the original is 84 ÷ 0.70 = 120. In the same way, if a salary is 33,000 after a 10% rise, the earlier salary was 33,000 ÷ 1.10 = 30,000. The wrong approach, taking 30% of 84 and adding it back, gives 109.20, which is not the original. Use the reverse percentage box in tab 2 whenever the percent was applied to a value you no longer know.

Chaining percentages and percentage points

When several percent changes happen one after another, multiply the factors instead of adding the percents. A price that rises 10% in year one and 15% in year two has gone up by 1.10 × 1.15 − 1 = 26.5% overall, not 25%. A 20% discount followed by a further 10% off is 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, a total of 28% off, not 30%. The chain tool lists each step and the running value.

Percentage points are a different idea. They describe the gap between two percentages. If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, that is a rise of one percentage point but a 25% increase in the rate. News stories mix these up often, so check which one is meant. If a poll shows support moving from 40% to 44%, that is four percentage points, or a 10% relative increase.

Common percentage mistakes and how to avoid them

  • Using the wrong base. Percent change uses the old value, not the new one.
  • Adding percents that should be multiplied. Two successive changes combine as factors.
  • Confusing percent with percentage points. Say which you mean.
  • Subtracting a percent from the final price. Use the reverse percentage instead.
  • Forgetting to divide by 100. 15% is 0.15, not 15.
  • Dividing by zero. Percent change from zero is undefined, because there is no base.

Quick mental-math tricks

  • 10%. Move the decimal point one place left.
  • 5%. Take half of 10%.
  • 1%. Move the point two places left.
  • 15%. Add 10% and 5%. For 240 that is 24 + 12 = 36.
  • 20%. Double 10%.
  • 25%. Divide by 4. 50%. Divide by 2. 75%. Take 25% off.
  • Swap the numbers. 8% of 50 equals 50% of 8, which is 4. Pick the easier one.

Frequently asked questions

What is a percentage calculator?

A tool that solves percentage problems: finding a percent of a number, working out what percent one number is of another, calculating percent change, increasing or decreasing a value by a percent and reversing a percentage. This one shows every step.

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Divide the percent by 100 and multiply by the number. For example, 15% of 240 is 0.15 x 240 = 36. Enter 15 and 240 in the first box to check.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. For example, 36 out of 160 is 36 / 160 x 100 = 22.5%.

How do I calculate percent change?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. Going from 80 to 100 is (100 - 80) / 80 x 100 = 25% increase. A negative answer is a decrease.

What is the difference between percent change and percent difference?

Percent change has a direction and uses the starting value as the base. Percent difference compares two values with no order, using their average as the base. Use change for before and after, and difference for two peers.

How do I increase a number by a percent?

Multiply by 1 plus the percent as a decimal. To raise 250 by 20%, multiply by 1.20 to get 300. To decrease, multiply by 1 minus the decimal.

How do I find the original price before a percentage increase?

Divide the final value by 1 plus the percent as a decimal. If a price is 120 after a 20% increase, the original was 120 / 1.20 = 100. This is the reverse percentage.

Why is it wrong to subtract a percent increase from the new price?

Because the percent applies to the original, not the new value. A 20% increase to 100 gives 120, but 20% of 120 is 24, so taking 20% off 120 gives 96, not 100.

What happens if I increase a number by 20% and then decrease it by 20%?

You end up 4% lower. Percent changes multiply: 1.20 x 0.80 = 0.96. The successive changes tool in the third tab shows this for any list.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A rise from 10% to 12% is 2 percentage points but a 20% increase, because 2 is 20% of 10. Use percentage points for the gap between two rates.

How do I convert a percent to a fraction or decimal?

Divide by 100 for the decimal, and write the decimal over 1 and simplify for the fraction. For example, 12.5% = 0.125 = 1/8. The converter in the third tab does this for you.

Can a percentage be more than 100?

Yes. 150% of a number is one and a half times it. A value that doubled has risen by 100%, and one that tripled has risen by 200%.

How do I calculate a percentage of a percentage?

Convert both to decimals and multiply. 20% of 50% is 0.20 x 0.50 = 0.10, which is 10%.

How do I calculate a discount or a tip with a percentage?

Use the percent-of tool: a 15% tip on 80 is 0.15 x 80 = 12. For stacked discounts, taxes and buy-one-get-one deals use the discount calculator on this site.

Does the calculator round answers?

It shows up to six decimal places and removes trailing zeros. Round the final result to the precision you need.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. All calculations run in your browser and nothing is uploaded or stored.
