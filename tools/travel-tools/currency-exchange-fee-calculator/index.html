What this free currency exchange fee calculator does

Changing money abroad is one of the easiest places to lose money without noticing. A 3 percent foreign fee here, an 8 percent kiosk mark-up there, and a pay-in-your-own-currency prompt that quietly adds 5 percent. This free online currency exchange fee calculator shows what each way of getting foreign money really costs. Enter the amount, the mid-market rate and the charges, and it ranks eight common methods by how much you receive.

The comparison tab shows the amount you get, the cost in your home currency and the effective cost as a percentage for each method, with a bar chart. The purchases tab compares paying in local currency with the dynamic currency conversion offered at terminals and works out how much to exchange to reach a target amount. The trip tab totals the fees for a whole trip based on your card and cash split. Everything runs in your browser and there are no live rates, so you stay in control of the numbers.

How to use the currency exchange fee calculator

  1. Enter the amount you plan to convert and your home currency symbol.
  2. Enter the mid-market rate for your pair, as foreign units for one unit of home currency.
  3. Edit the charges for each method to match your bank, card and the providers you may use.
  4. Read the ranking and the effective cost of each option.
  5. Check purchases and reverse amounts on the second tab.
  6. Total your trip fees on the third tab.

How the money you receive is calculated

For each method the tool subtracts any flat fee and percentage fee from the amount, converts the remainder at the mid-market rate, and then reduces the result by the rate mark-up. The effective cost is how much less you receive than the fair mid-market value of your original amount. Take $1,000 converted at a mid-market rate of 0.92 euros per dollar, which is fairly worth 920 euros. A kiosk with a $4 flat fee and an 8 percent mark-up gives (1,000 - 4) x 0.92 x 0.92 = 843.01 euros, a cost of 8.37 percent or about $83.68.

The dynamic currency conversion trap

When a card terminal or ATM abroad asks whether you want to pay in your home currency, it feels like the safe option, because you can see the price in your own money. But the exchange rate used is set by the merchant or the ATM operator and typically includes a margin of several percent. For a purchase of 120 euros, with a mid-market cost of $130.43, paying in euros on a card with a 3 percent fee costs $134.35, while accepting a 5 percent mark-up on the terminal costs $136.96, which is $2.61 more. If your card has no foreign fee, the gap is bigger. As a rule, decline the offer and pay in the local currency.

How to cut currency exchange costs

  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee for purchases.
  • Withdraw cash from bank ATMs and decline conversion.
  • Withdraw larger amounts less often to spread flat fees.
  • Avoid airport kiosks unless you need a small amount.
  • Compare against the mid-market rate before you exchange.
  • Check your card limits and fees before you travel.

Common currency exchange mistakes

The most common is exchanging at an airport kiosk or hotel desk out of convenience, where the effective cost is often the highest. The second is accepting the offer to be charged in your home currency on a card terminal or ATM, which looks transparent but uses a marked-up rate. A third is believing a sign that says 'no commission', because the provider can simply widen the spread in the rate, so always compare the amount you receive with the mid-market rate.

Travellers also make many small withdrawals and pay a flat fee each time, carry large amounts of cash that they then change back at a poor rate, or forget that their home bank charges for foreign use of a card. Before you travel, check your bank's foreign transaction fee, ATM fees and daily limits, consider a card with no foreign fee, and take a small amount of cash for the first few hours. In the destination, withdraw larger sums less often from a bank-branded ATM, pay in the local currency and keep your card in the same wallet as little as possible.

Frequently asked questions

What is the mid-market rate?

The midpoint between the buy and sell prices for a currency pair, the rate you see on financial sites. It is the fair benchmark for measuring what a provider charges you.

How do I find the real cost of an exchange?

Compare what you receive with the amount you would get at the mid-market rate. The difference, as a percentage, is the effective cost, including both fees and any hidden mark-up in the rate.

What is a rate mark-up?

A margin built into the exchange rate itself so the provider earns money without showing a fee. Airport kiosks and some ATMs mark up the rate by several percent.

What is dynamic currency conversion?

When a shop terminal or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency instead of the local one. It looks convenient, but the exchange rate usually includes a mark-up of several percent. It is usually cheaper to pay in the local currency.

Should I pay in local or home currency?

Usually the local currency. The Purchases tab compares your card's foreign fee with the terminal's mark-up so you can see which is cheaper.

Why are airport exchange kiosks expensive?

They have captive customers and high costs, so they often combine a poor rate with a commission or flat fee. Effective costs of 5 to 10 percent are common.

Are ATM withdrawals cheaper than exchange counters?

Often yes, if you decline dynamic currency conversion and your bank has low fees. But ATM operators and your own bank may charge flat fees, so withdrawing larger amounts less often reduces the cost.

How do I enter a flat fee?

Enter the flat fee in your home currency. It is charged once per transaction, such as one ATM withdrawal.

How do I use the percentage fee?

A percentage fee is taken from the amount you convert before the rate is applied, such as a 3 percent foreign transaction fee on a card.

Does the tool give live exchange rates?

No. It works entirely in your browser without sending data, so you enter the mid-market rate from any rate source you trust.

How accurate are the default fees?

They are illustrative examples. Real charges vary widely by bank, card and provider, so replace them with the charges in your terms.

How much will fees cost on my whole trip?

The Trip fees tab splits your spend between card and cash, counts ATM withdrawals and totals the fees, comparing them with the worst combination.

What is the reverse calculation?

It works out how much home currency you need to send or exchange to end up with a target foreign amount, given the charges.

Is a no-foreign-fee card always the best?

Usually for card spending, but check other costs such as annual fees. For cash, check the ATM fees and limits.

Should I exchange money before I travel?

A small amount of local cash for the first day can be handy, but exchanging large amounts at a counter usually costs more than withdrawing at a local ATM.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No. All calculations run in your browser.

Is this currency exchange fee calculator free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up.
