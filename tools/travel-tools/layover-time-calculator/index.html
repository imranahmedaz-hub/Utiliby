What is a free layover time calculator online?

A free layover time calculator online is a tool that tells you whether the time between two flights is long enough for a comfortable connection. You enter your departure airport, the airport where you change planes, your final destination, the scheduled layover duration, and whether both flights are on the same ticket. The tool compares your layover against the minimum connection time (MCT) for that airport and connection type, then gives a clear verdict: comfortable, tight or risky.

This layover calculator goes beyond a simple duration comparison. It factors in whether a terminal change is required, whether you need to collect and recheck bags, and whether the two flights are on the same ticket or separate bookings. Those three factors change the risk profile dramatically. A 90-minute layover at a single-terminal airport with through-checked bags is comfortable; the same 90 minutes with a terminal change, a bag recheck and two separate tickets is genuinely risky. What is minimum connection time?

Minimum connection time (MCT) is the shortest time an airport and airline consider sufficient for a passenger to transfer between flights. It varies by airport, terminal, whether the connection is domestic or international, and whether both flights are operated by the same airline or alliance. MCTs are published by airports and airlines and used by booking systems to build valid itineraries — if a connection is below MCT, the booking system will normally refuse to sell it as a single ticket.

Same ticket vs separate tickets: the critical distinction

If both flights are on the same ticket (a single booking reference, even if operated by different airlines), the airline is responsible for your connection. If your first flight is delayed and you miss the second, the airline must rebook you on the next available flight at no extra cost, and often provide accommodation and meals if the delay is overnight. This is the protection that MCT is designed around.

If the flights are on separate tickets — sometimes called a self-transfer — the second airline has no obligation to you at all. If you miss the second flight because the first was delayed, you have to buy a new ticket. This is why low-cost carrier itineraries built from two separate bookings are often much cheaper but carry real risk. For a self-transfer, add at least two hours to the MCT as a safety buffer, and consider an overnight layover instead of a same-day connection.

Factors that make a layover riskier

  • Terminal change. A terminal change adds 15–45 minutes depending on the airport, and some airports require a bus, train or shuttle between terminals.
  • Bag recheck. If your bags are not checked through to the final destination, you must collect them, clear customs and check them in again. This typically adds 45–90 minutes.
  • Immigration and security. International connections usually require a security recheck even if you do not clear immigration. Some countries (the US, for example) require you to clear immigration and recheck bags on every international-to-domestic connection.
  • Separate tickets. No protection if the first flight is delayed. Add a large buffer.
  • Late arrival risk. A flight that frequently runs late — check the airline's on-time performance — makes a tight connection riskier regardless of the scheduled time.
  • Airport size. Large hub airports can take 20–40 minutes to walk between distant gates, even without a terminal change.

What to do if your layover is tight

  • Same ticket: let the airline handle it. They will rebook you automatically if you miss the connection. Tell a gate agent as soon as you land if you are running late.
  • Separate tickets: consider rebooking the second flight to a later departure before you travel, or build in an overnight stop. If you cannot, have a backup plan and travel with hand luggage only if possible.
  • Book a longer layover next time. For international connections, aim for 2 to 3 hours. For self-transfers, aim for 4 hours or an overnight stop.
  • Check the airport map. Know which terminal you arrive at and which you depart from. Some airports publish transfer times between terminals.

Frequently asked questions

What is a free layover time calculator online?

It checks whether your layover is long enough for a comfortable connection. You enter your airports, the layover duration, and whether both flights are on the same ticket. The tool compares your layover against the minimum connection time and gives a verdict.

What is minimum connection time (MCT)?

MCT is the shortest time an airport and airline consider sufficient for a passenger to transfer between flights. It varies by airport, terminal, connection type and airline. Booking systems use MCT to decide whether a connection is sellable as a single ticket.

Is a 1-hour layover enough for an international connection?

Usually not. One hour is generally too tight for international-to-international, especially with a terminal change or bag recheck. Most international connections need 90 to 180 minutes.

What happens if I miss my connection?

If both flights are on the same ticket, the airline rebooks you at no extra cost. If they are on separate tickets, you are responsible — the second airline has no obligation to rebook you.
