What is a free layover time calculator online?

A free layover time calculator online tells you whether the time between two flights is long enough for a comfortable connection. You enter the airport where you change planes, the scheduled layover, the type of connection, and whether both flights are on the same ticket. The tool compares your layover against an approximate planning baseline for that airport and connection type, adds time for the factors that make connections harder, and gives a verdict: comfortable, tight or risky.

The baselines are planning figures chosen by this tool. They are not the official minimum connection times (MCT) published by airports, airlines or booking systems, and the tool is not affiliated with any of them. Its purpose is to help you compare options and spot an itinerary that looks too tight, before you confirm the details with your airline. Everything runs in your browser, with no sign-up and no upload.

How to use this free connection time calculator

  1. Optionally enter your departure airport and final destination. They are for your own reference and do not change the result.
  2. Enter the layover airport as a three-letter IATA code, for example JFK, LHR or DXB.
  3. Enter the scheduled layover length in hours and minutes. Use the gap between landing and departure shown on your booking.
  4. Choose the connection type: domestic or international for the arriving flight, and domestic or international for the departing flight. International arrivals usually add immigration and security time.
  5. Choose whether both flights are on the same ticket or on separate tickets.
  6. Say whether you must change terminals and whether you must collect and recheck bags.
  7. Click Check my connection. The tool shows the planning baseline, the extra time for your terminal change, bag recheck and ticket type, your buffer and a verdict. For a fuller explanation, read our guide to how long a layover you need.

How the calculation works

The calculation is simple, so you can check it yourself. The tool starts with a planning baseline for the airport and connection type. It then adds 25 minutes for a terminal change, 45 minutes for a bag recheck and 120 minutes for separate tickets. The sum is your effective minimum. Your buffer is your layover minus the effective minimum.

30 minutes or moreComfortableSome room for a modest delay or a slow queue

For example, a 2-hour layover at London Heathrow between two international flights on one ticket, with no terminal change and no bag recheck, has a 75-minute baseline and a 45-minute buffer, which the tool labels Comfortable. The same 2 hours on separate tickets with a bag recheck needs 75 + 120 + 45 = 240 minutes, so the buffer is minus 120 minutes and the tool labels it Risky.

What is minimum connection time?

Minimum connection time (MCT) is the shortest time an airport and airline treat as workable for a passenger to transfer between flights. It varies by airport, terminal, whether the connection is domestic or international, and whether both flights are operated by the same airline or alliance. Booking systems use MCT to decide whether a connection can be sold on one ticket, so a connection below the MCT is normally not offered as a single booking.

MCT is a floor for selling an itinerary, not a promise that you will be comfortable. It generally assumes that your first flight arrives on time. That is why a bookable connection can still feel tight, and why this tool shows a buffer on top of its baseline.

Same ticket vs separate tickets

If both flights are on one booking, one party is responsible for getting you to your destination. If a delay on the first flight makes you miss the second, the airline will usually rebook you on its next available flight at no extra cost, and may provide care such as meals or accommodation depending on the cause of the delay, the airline and the rules where you are travelling. Check your airline's policy and your passenger rights.

If the flights are on separate tickets, often called a self-transfer, each airline treats its own ticket on its own terms. The second airline is often not obliged to rebook you if the first flight is late, your bags may not be checked through, and you may have to buy a new ticket. Separate tickets can be much cheaper, but they need a larger buffer, and many travellers consider an overnight stay.

Factors that make a layover riskier

  • Terminal change. It can be a short walk or a trip on a train or bus, sometimes with another security check. The tool allows 25 minutes as a planning figure.
  • Bag recheck. If bags are not checked through, you collect them, clear customs where required and check them in again. The tool allows 45 minutes.
  • Immigration and security. Many international connections involve security screening, and some involve immigration. Rules differ by country.
  • Separate tickets. Little protection if the first flight is delayed. The tool allows 120 minutes.
  • Late arrival risk. A route that often runs late makes a tight connection riskier, whatever the schedule says.
  • Airport size and time of day. Large hubs can take a long time to cross, and early-morning, evening and holiday queues can be longer.

What to do if your layover is tight

  • Same ticket: tell the airline early if you are running late and ask what it will do if you miss the connection. Keep your receipts and boarding passes.
  • Separate tickets: consider moving the second flight to a later departure before you travel, or build in an overnight stop. Carry essentials in your hand luggage.
  • Look at the airport map: know which terminal you arrive at and which one you depart from.
  • Check the boarding time: the real deadline is when the second flight stops boarding, not its departure time.
  • Confirm the rules: ask your airline or booking agent for the connection rules for your itinerary, and check visa and transit requirements.

Limits of this calculator

The tool does not know your airline, terminals, flight status or the queues on the day. It has specific planning figures for 12 large airports and a general default for others. It does not model visas, airport-specific security procedures or delays, and it cannot tell you the official connection time for your booking. Frequently asked questions

What is a free layover time calculator online?

It checks whether the time between two flights is long enough for a comfortable connection. You enter the layover airport, the layover length, the connection type and whether both flights are on one ticket, and the tool compares your layover against an approximate planning baseline. It does not use official minimum connection times, so confirm your connection with your airline.

How long a layover do you need?

It depends on the airport, whether you change terminals, whether you cross immigration, and whether both flights are on one ticket. As a rough planning guide, many travellers allow about 1 hour for a domestic connection and 1.5 to 3 hours for an international one. Check the connection rules for your itinerary with your airline.

What is minimum connection time (MCT)?

Minimum connection time is the shortest transfer time that an airport and airline treat as workable for a connection, and booking systems use it to decide whether a connection can be sold on one ticket. It is not the same as a comfortable layover, and this calculator uses approximate planning baselines rather than official MCTs.

Are the calculator's baselines official minimum connection times?

No. The baselines are approximate planning values chosen by the tool, not figures published by an airport, airline or booking system. The official figure for your itinerary comes from your airline or booking agent.

Is a 1-hour layover enough for an international connection?

Usually not. One hour is generally too tight for an international-to-international connection, especially if you must clear immigration, recheck bags or change terminals. Many international connections need 90 to 180 minutes depending on the airport. Check your airline for the figure that applies to your itinerary.

Is a 2-hour layover enough?

Often it is workable for a domestic connection and for many single-ticket international connections at efficient airports. It can be tight if you must clear immigration, pass through security again or change terminals, and a late first flight can use up the margin. Many travellers prefer 3 hours when anything about the connection is unusual.

What happens if I miss my connection?

If both flights are on the same ticket, the airline will usually rebook you on its next available flight at no extra cost, though the rules vary by airline. If they are on separate tickets, you usually bear the risk: the second airline is often not obliged to rebook you, and you may have to buy a new ticket. Check your airline's policy and your passenger rights.

What is the difference between a same ticket and separate tickets?

A same ticket means both flights are on one booking, so one airline group is responsible for getting you to your destination. Separate tickets mean two independent bookings, so each airline treats its own ticket on its own terms. The calculator adds 120 minutes to the effective minimum for separate tickets.

What does a terminal change add?

The calculator adds 25 minutes as a planning allowance. In real airports a terminal change can be a short walk or a longer trip on a train or bus, sometimes with another security check, so look at the airport map and ask your airline.

What does a bag recheck add?

The calculator adds 45 minutes. If your bags are not checked through, you collect them, clear customs where required and check them in again, which can take longer than that at busy airports. Ask your airline whether your bags will be checked through to the final destination.

What do Comfortable, Tight and Risky mean?

Comfortable means your layover is at least 30 minutes above the planning baseline plus any extras. Tight means you are 0 to 29 minutes above it. Risky means your layover is shorter than the baseline plus extras. These labels are planning aids and not predictions of what will happen.

Why does the calculator ask for an airport code?

The tool has specific planning figures for 12 large airports and uses a general default for any other three-letter IATA code. The result tells you which one it used. Enter the airport where you change planes, for example LHR or DXB.

Do I need a transit visa for a layover?

It depends on your nationality, the country you transit through, whether you leave the airport and whether you stay airside. Rules differ by country and change, so check the official government or airline guidance for every country on your route before you book.

Should I book separate tickets to save money?

Separate tickets can be cheaper, but you carry the risk of a missed connection, and bags are often not checked through. If you do it, allow a much larger buffer, avoid the last flight of the day for the second leg and consider an overnight stay.

Does a Comfortable result guarantee I will make my flight?

No. A delay on the first flight, a long immigration queue or a gate change can use up any buffer. Treat the verdict as a planning aid and keep checking your flight status.

Does this calculator cover compensation for delays or missed connections?

No. For delay compensation in the EU and UK you can use our EU261 Compensation Calculator as a rough guide, and you should read your airline's policy and the official passenger rights guidance for your situation.
