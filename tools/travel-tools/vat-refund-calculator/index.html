What is a free travel VAT refund calculator online?

A free travel VAT refund calculator online shows what you actually get back from tax-free shopping abroad — not the misleading headline VAT rate. The "20% tax-free" sign at the airport hides two deductions. First, VAT is a slice of the pre-tax price, so the VAT inside a gross price at rate r is r/(1+r), not r. At a 20% headline rate, the maximum you can ever reclaim is 16.67% of the price you paid. Second, refund operators such as Global Blue and Planet keep a commission, often a quarter to a third of that VAT. A 20% headline rate typically becomes about 12% net in your pocket.

This VAT refund calculator does the arithmetic correctly. You enter the country, the purchase amount, the refund method and the operator. The tool shows the gross VAT inside the price, the operator's commission, the net refund, and the effective refund rate as a percentage of the price. It also checks the country's minimum spend rule, which is often the reason a claim is refused. Everything runs in your browser — no sign-up, no account, no upload, no tracking.

When a VAT refund is worth it

If you are already travelling, claim the refund every time — it is free money and the process at airport kiosks is quick. What rarely adds up is travelling specifically to shop tax-free. Once you add airfare, hotels and your time, a ~12% net refund does not cover the cost of the trip. The refund is a nice bonus on a trip you were taking anyway, not a reason to book one.

Minimum spend rules

The minimum spend is usually the single biggest reason a refund claim is refused. The rule normally requires a minimum purchase in a single store on a single day. Separate receipts from the same store on different days do not add up, and purchases from different stores cannot be combined. Spain has no minimum. Germany requires €50.01 on one receipt. France requires €100.01. Italy requires €70.01. Portugal requires €50. Check the specific country before you shop, and ask the retailer for the tax-free form at the point of sale.

How to claim a VAT refund

  1. Ask the retailer for the tax-free form at the point of purchase and make sure your passport details are recorded correctly.
  2. Keep the goods unused and in their original packaging, along with the receipts and the tax-free form.
  3. At your final point of departure from the EU, present the goods, receipts and form to customs for validation. Do this before checking in bags if the goods are in your checked luggage.
  4. Submit the validated form to the refund operator — either at an airport desk (for cash) or by mail (for a card refund).
  5. Keep a copy of everything you submit.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the headline rate is what you get. The real net is usually 12–16% of the price, not 20%.
  • Missing the minimum spend. A purchase below the country's threshold cannot be claimed, even if everything else is in order.
  • Not getting the form at the point of sale. You cannot claim without the tax-free form, and retailers may not be able to issue it retroactively.
  • Forgetting customs validation. The form must be stamped or digitally validated by customs at your final EU departure point.
  • Packing goods in checked bags before validation. Customs may want to see the goods. Validate before checking in.
  • Leaving it too late. The claim deadline is typically 3 months from purchase. Miss it and the refund is lost.
  • Taking a cash refund when a card refund is better. Cash at the airport carries an extra fee. A card refund is slower but cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

What is a free travel VAT refund calculator online?

It shows what you actually get back from tax-free shopping — after operator fees. It starts from the VAT inside the price, subtracts the commission, and shows the net refund and effective rate.

Why is the VAT refund less than the headline rate?

Two reasons: VAT is only r/(1+r) of the price (16.67% at a 20% rate), and operators keep a commission of roughly a quarter to a third of the VAT. A 20% headline rate becomes about 12% net.

What is the minimum spend?

It varies by country — none in Spain, €50.01 in Germany, €100.01 in France, €70.01 in Italy, €50 in Portugal, €100 in Croatia. It usually applies to a single receipt from a single store on a single day.

How long do I have to claim?

Typically 3 months from purchase, and you must export the goods within that period. Customs validation is required at your final EU departure point.
