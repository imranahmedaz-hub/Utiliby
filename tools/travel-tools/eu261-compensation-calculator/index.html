What this free online EU261 flight compensation calculator does

Few travellers know that a long delay can be worth hundreds of euros. EU Regulation 261/2004, known as EU261, and its UK twin UK261, give passengers fixed compensation when a flight is cancelled, denied or reaches its destination three hours late, provided the airline cannot blame an extraordinary event. Most websites that explain this are built to sign you up for a paid claim service that keeps a quarter to a third of your money. This free online EU261 compensation calculator does the checking for you, with nothing to sign up for.

You enter your airports and what happened. The tool works out the great-circle distance, decides whether EU261, UK261 or both apply, applies the three-hour rule and the cancellation notice rules, reads the reason the airline gave against the court rulings, and shows the amount per passenger. It then writes a claim letter, adds up your expenses and works out your deadline. A live diagram shows where your flight sits in the distance bands and how your delay compares with the three-hour line. You can print or save a clean report that contains only your result, never the whole page.

Two details catch people out. First, flights wholly inside the EU that are longer than 1,500 km pay 400 euros even when they are longer than 3,500 km, for example Helsinki to Tenerife. Second, if the airline got you to your destination less than two, three or four hours late (depending on distance) after a cancellation or denied boarding, the amount is cut by half. The same half rate is applied to long-haul delays of three to four hours, which means 300 euros or 260 pounds. This calculator applies all of these rules for you.

When you can claim: delay, cancellation, denied boarding, downgrade

Long delay

The Court of Justice ruled that a passenger who arrives three hours or more late is treated like a passenger on a cancelled flight (Sturgis, C-402/07). What counts is the arrival time at the final destination. A flight that leaves late but makes up time does not qualify, and a flight that leaves on time but lands three hours late does.

Cancellation

You are owed compensation unless you were told at least 14 days ahead, or told 7 to 13 days ahead and offered a replacement that leaves no more than two hours early and lands less than four hours late, or told less than 7 days ahead and offered a replacement that leaves no more than one hour early and lands less than two hours late. You do not need to have checked in for a cancellation claim.

Denied boarding

If you hold a valid ticket, check in on time and are refused a seat against your will, the airline must pay at once. People who accept a volunteer offer settle for whatever they agree.

Downgrade

If you are moved to a lower class, you can claim back 30 percent of the ticket price for flights up to 1,500 km, 50 percent for medium flights and intra-EU flights over 1,500 km, and 75 percent for long flights.

Missed connections

When all flights are on one booking, the delay is measured at the final destination and the distance from the first departure to the final arrival (Folkerts, C-11/11).

Extraordinary circumstances: which excuses work

Article 5(3) frees the airline from paying compensation when the cause was an extraordinary circumstance that could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken. Even when a circumstance qualifies, the airline still owes meals, hotels and a refund or re-routing. If you suspect the excuse is weak, compare the airline's statement with weather reports and with what other flights did that day.

Which flights are covered: EU261 and UK261

EU261 covers every flight that leaves an airport in the EU, Iceland, Norway or Switzerland, on any airline. It also covers flights that arrive there from outside on an airline licensed in the EU, Iceland, Norway or Switzerland, unless you already received compensation or help in the departure country. French overseas departments, the Canary Islands, Madeira and the Azores count as EU territory.

UK261 covers flights that leave UK airports on any airline and flights that arrive in the UK on UK or EU airlines. A flight from the EU to the UK on an EU airline can fall under both laws, and you choose one. Flights between two non-European airports on a non-European airline are outside both. In that case look at the airline's own policy and the rules of its home country, which may be helpful. Some countries, such as Canada, have their own schemes.

How to claim step by step

  1. Collect proof. Booking confirmation, boarding pass, a screenshot of the delay and the airline's message with its reason.
  2. Write to the airline. Use the letter from the Claim letter tab. Most airlines have an online claim form as well. Keep your reference number.
  3. Allow a reasonable wait. Many airlines reply within a few weeks. Set a clear 14 day deadline in your letter.
  4. Escalate if refused. In the EU, complain to the national enforcement body of the country of departure, or use an alternative dispute resolution scheme. In the UK, use an approved ADR scheme and the Civil Aviation Authority's guidance.
  5. Go to court if needed. Small claims routes exist in many countries and cost little compared with the sum at stake.

Your right to care and assistance

Separate from the fixed amount, Article 9 gives you meals and refreshments, two free calls or emails, and a hotel with transport if you must stay overnight. The wait that triggers care is two hours for flights up to 1,500 km, three hours for medium flights and four hours for long flights, measured at departure. If a delay reaches five hours you may cancel and get a full refund, including a flight home if you are away from your starting point. Care applies even in extraordinary circumstances, and you can claim reasonable costs back if the airline did not provide it. The Expenses tab helps you keep track.

What the 2026 reform could change

Brussels has been trying to revise EU261 since 2013. In 2025 the Council proposed higher delay thresholds of four and six hours. The Parliament pushed back, and a provisional agreement reported in mid 2026 keeps the three-hour trigger and the current amounts while adding a clearer list of extraordinary circumstances, electronic notification and faster claim handling. Because the text still needs formal adoption and a transition period, the current rules and this calculator apply today. If the rules change, this page will be updated.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting the departure delay. Only the arrival delay decides the cash amount.
  • Accepting a voucher. You are entitled to cash.
  • Believing the first no. Airlines often reject valid claims and pay after an enforcement body steps in.
  • Waiting too long. Limits are as short as two years in some places.
  • Forgetting expenses. Receipts for meals, a hotel and transport can be added to your claim.
  • Claiming under the wrong law. Flights from the EU to the UK can fall under both regimes, but you can only be paid once.
  • Paying a claim company for a simple case. A direct claim costs nothing.

Frequently asked questions

How much compensation can I get for a delayed flight?

Under EU261 you can get 250 euros for flights up to 1,500 km, 400 euros for flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km and for longer flights inside the EU, and 600 euros for other long flights. UK261 pays 220, 350 or 520 pounds. The amount is per passenger.

How late does a flight have to be to claim?

Three hours or more at your final destination. The time that counts is when the aircraft door opens, not when the plane left the gate.

Is this EU261 compensation calculator really free?

Yes. It is a free online tool with no sign-up and no fee. It runs in your browser and does not send your flight details anywhere.

How is the flight distance measured?

As the great-circle distance between the departure and the final arrival airport, not the route actually flown. For connections on one booking, the distance runs from the first departure to the final destination.

What is the difference between EU261 and UK261?

UK261 is the UK's copy of EU261 since Brexit. It covers flights leaving UK airports and flights arriving in the UK on UK or EU airlines, and pays in pounds with three simple distance bands. You can claim under one, not both.

Do I get compensation for a cancelled flight?

Yes, unless the airline told you at least 14 days ahead, or told you 7 to 13 days ahead and offered a close replacement, or told you later and offered a very close replacement. An extraordinary circumstance can also remove the payment.

What if I was denied boarding because of overbooking?

If you had a valid ticket, checked in on time and were refused against your will, you are owed compensation straight away, plus a refund or re-routing and care. Taking a volunteer deal is a separate agreement.

Can I claim if I missed a connection?

Yes, if everything was on one booking and you reached your final destination three hours or more late. The distance is counted from your first departure to your final destination.

What are extraordinary circumstances?

Events outside the airline's control that it could not have prevented even with every reasonable measure. Examples are severe weather, closed airspace, security threats and some outside strikes. The airline must prove them.

Are strikes an extraordinary circumstance?

Usually not when the airline's own staff strike. A strike by airport, security or air traffic staff can count, but the airline must show it took reasonable steps to limit the effect.

Is bad weather a valid excuse?

Only when the weather really made your flight unsafe. The airline must prove it, and if other flights left on time, the excuse is weaker.

How long do I have to make a claim?

It depends on where you claim. Commonly cited periods are 6 years in England and Wales and Ireland, 5 in Scotland, France and Spain, 3 in Germany and Austria, and 2 in Italy. Claim as soon as you can.

Does EU261 cover flights from outside Europe?

Only when the flight arrives in the EU, Iceland, Norway or Switzerland on an airline licensed there, and you did not already receive compensation in the departure country. Flights leaving the EU are covered on any airline.

Can I get a refund and compensation too?

Yes. If your flight is cancelled you can choose a refund or re-routing, and you may still be owed the fixed compensation. They are separate rights.

Should I use a claim company or claim directly?

Claiming directly is free. Claim companies usually keep a commission of roughly a quarter to a third of the payout, but they handle disputes and court cases. For a clear-cut claim, start with the airline yourself.

What if the airline offers a voucher?

You are entitled to cash. You do not have to accept a voucher, and a voucher is only valid if you agree to it.

What help must the airline give me while I wait?

Meals and drinks after a wait of 2, 3 or 4 hours depending on distance, two free calls or emails, and a hotel with transport if you must stay overnight. These rights apply even when an extraordinary circumstance caused the delay.
