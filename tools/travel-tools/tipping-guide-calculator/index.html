What this free tipping guide calculator does

Tipping is simple at home and confusing abroad. Do you leave 20 percent, round up, or avoid it altogether? This free online tipping guide calculator covers 63 countries, from the United States and Canada to Japan, Thailand, Uzbekistan and the United Arab Emirates. Choose the country, the type of service, the bill and how good the service was, and it works out the tip, the total and the share per person.

A gauge shows the usual tip range for the country and where your tip falls. You can tick that a service charge is already on the bill, and round the total up for cash payments. A second tab estimates a tipping budget for a whole trip, including meals, taxis, hotel housekeeping, porters and tour guides. The third tab is a searchable country guide with customs and notes. Print gives a clean guide to take with you. Everything runs in your browser.

How to use the tipping calculator

  1. Choose the country and the service: restaurant, cafe or bar, or taxi.
  2. Enter the bill before the tip and the currency symbol.
  3. Enter how many people are sharing the bill.
  4. Choose the quality of service. Poor, good or excellent picks the low, typical or high end of the range.
  5. Tick the service charge option if the bill already includes one.
  6. Read the tip and total, then open the other tabs for a trip budget or the country guide.

How the tip is calculated

Each country has a low, typical and high tip percentage for restaurants and taxis, drawn from widely reported customs. The tool picks the figure that matches the service you choose and multiplies it by the bill. For a bill of $85.00 in the United States with good service the typical figure is 20 percent, which is a tip of $17.00 and a total of $102.00, or $51.00 each for two people. With excellent service the 25 percent figure gives $21.25, and with poor service the 15 percent figure gives $12.75. Cafes and bars are capped at 10 to 15 percent. If service is already on the bill, the suggested tip is capped at 5 percent.

Service charge versus tip

In many countries restaurants add a service charge, often 10 to 15 percent, to the bill. It may be labelled service compris in France, servizio in Italy, propina in Latin America or simply a service charge in the United Kingdom, the Gulf and much of Asia. If one is added, you have already paid for service, and a further tip is optional. Where it is not shown, check the menu and the bill. Be careful with the 'cover charge' in Italy and Spain, which is a charge for bread and table service and is not a tip. In some places the service charge goes to the business rather than the staff, so a little cash for a server who has been helpful is still welcome.

Tipping etiquette and tips

  • Check the bill first for a service charge.
  • Carry small notes in the local currency for porters, drivers and guides.
  • Tip the person who served you when you can, using cash.
  • Do not tip in countries where it is considered rude, such as Japan and South Korea.
  • Adjust for the service, not for the size of the bill alone.
  • Ask locals or your hotel if you are unsure.

Tipping in different situations

Tipping rules change with the type of service as well as the country. In the United States a tip is expected for table service, bars, taxis, hair and beauty services and food delivery, but not usually for counter service, though tablets often prompt for it. Large parties are often charged an automatic gratuity of 18 percent or more, so check the bill before adding more. Hotel staff such as porters and housekeeping are tipped by hand in many countries, but in places where tipping is not customary, a polite thank you is better.

Cruises and all-inclusive resorts often add a daily gratuity to your account or include tips in the price, so ask before you add more. Tour guides and drivers are commonly tipped at the end of a tour, based on the group size and the length of the day. When paying by card in a country where service is included, you can leave a small amount in cash. If you are unsure, observe what locals do, ask your hotel or guide, and remember that tipping should reward good service, not feel like an obligation. The ranges in this tool are a starting point.

Limits and privacy

Frequently asked questions

How much should I tip at a restaurant?

It depends on the country. In the United States and Canada 15 to 20 percent or more is expected. In much of Europe service is included and you can round up or leave 5 to 10 percent for good service. In Japan and Singapore tipping is not customary.

How does the tip calculator work?

Choose the country, the service, the bill and how good the service was. The tool uses the low, typical or high end of the usual range for that country and shows the tip, total and amount each.

What if a service charge is already on the bill?

Tick the service charge option. The tool then suggests a small extra of up to 5 percent, since you have already paid for service.

Is tipping mandatory?

No. Customs are strong in some countries, such as the United States, where servers rely on tips, but tipping is a choice almost everywhere.

Should I tip in cash or on the card?

Where cards are common, adding the tip to the card is usually fine. In some countries cash goes straight to the staff, so carry small notes.

How much do I tip a taxi or ride-share driver?

In the US and Canada 15 to 20 percent is usual. In much of the world, rounding up the fare to the nearest convenient amount is enough.

How much do I tip hotel staff?

The trip tab uses rough US dollar guides: housekeeping of a few dollars a night and a dollar or two per bag for porters in countries where tipping is expected, and less or nothing elsewhere.

Do I tip tour guides and drivers?

Often yes, especially in countries where guides depend on tips. A common range is 10 to 20 US dollars per person per day, but it varies.

How do I split the bill with a tip?

Enter the number of people. The tool divides the total, including the tip, and also shows the tip per person.

What does round up the total do?

It rounds the total to the next whole number, adjusting the tip to match. It is convenient when paying cash.

Why is tipping not customary in Japan?

Good service is considered part of the job, and trying to tip can cause awkwardness. A polite thank you is better.

Is tipping different in Europe?

Yes. Many European countries include service in menu prices. Leaving small change or rounding up is common, and large percentages are not expected.

How accurate is the country guide?

It reflects common customs at the time of writing. Customs change and vary by venue, so use it as a guide.

What currencies can I use?

Choose the currency symbol from the list. The calculation does not convert currencies, so enter the bill in the currency you will pay.

Can I plan a tipping budget for a trip?

Yes. The second tab estimates tips for meals, taxis, hotel, porters and guides from your trip plan.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser.

Is this tipping calculator free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up.
