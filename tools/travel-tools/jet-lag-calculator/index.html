What is jet lag?

Jet lag (also called jet lag disorder or time zone fatigue) is a temporary sleep disorder caused by rapid travel across multiple time zones. Your internal body clock — the circadian rhythm governed by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the brain — controls sleep-wake timing, hormone release, body temperature and digestion. When you cross time zones quickly, the external day-night cycle shifts abruptly, but your internal clock cannot reset instantaneously. The result is circadian misalignment — you feel sleepy at 3pm local time and wide awake at 2am.

Why eastward travel is harder than westward

Your body's circadian rhythm naturally runs slightly longer than 24 hours, making it easier to delay sleep than to advance it. When you fly east, you need to advance your clock — sleep earlier and wake earlier — which runs against your natural tendency. When you fly west, you delay your clock, which feels more natural.

Research shows the circadian rhythm adjusts at roughly 1 hour per day flying east and about 1.5 hours per day flying west. This is why a flight from New York to London (5 hours east) takes longer to recover from than the return trip.

How jet lag recovery time is calculated

This calculator uses the widely accepted guideline of approximately one day per time zone crossed. The exact recovery depends on direction:

  • Eastward travel: Zones crossed ÷ 1 hour per day. A 6-hour eastward shift takes about 6 days.
  • Westward travel: Zones crossed ÷ 1.5 hours per day. A 6-hour westward shift takes about 4 days.
  • Pre-trip preparation can reduce recovery by 30–50%.

How to beat jet lag: evidence-based tips

Before you travel

  • Shift your sleep 2–3 days before departure. Flying east: go to bed 1 hour earlier each night. Flying west: go to bed 1 hour later.
  • Be well rested. Starting a trip sleep-deprived makes jet lag worse.
  • Short trip under 3 days? Consider staying on home time instead of adapting.

During the flight

  • Stay hydrated. Dehydration worsens symptoms.
  • Avoid alcohol and caffeine. Both disrupt sleep.
  • Sleep if it's nighttime at your destination. Stay awake if it's daytime.
  • Walk around periodically to reduce stiffness.

After you arrive

  • Get sunlight at the right time. Light is the strongest signal for resetting your body clock.
  • Avoid long daytime naps. Short naps (15–20 minutes) can help without disrupting nighttime sleep.
  • Eat meals on destination time from day one — meal timing helps reset peripheral clocks.

Jet lag symptoms to expect

Common jet lag symptoms include daytime fatigue, insomnia, difficulty concentrating, irritability, headaches, digestive issues (constipation, diarrhoea, nausea), reduced appetite, and muscle soreness. Symptoms are usually worst on the first 2–3 days and ease steadily as your circadian rhythm adapts.

Melatonin for jet lag: how and when

Melatonin can help reset your body clock, but timing is critical. Taking it at the wrong time can make jet lag worse.

  • Dose: 0.5 mg to 5 mg. Lower doses (0.5 mg) are as effective for shifting the clock.
  • Eastward travel: Take in the afternoon or early evening at destination time — about 4.5 hours before your usual sleep onset.
  • Westward travel: Take in the morning at destination time if you need to delay your clock.

Always consult your doctor before taking melatonin, especially if you take other medications.

Frequently asked questions

How long does jet lag last?

Recovery typically takes about one day per time zone crossed when flying east, and roughly one day per 1.5 time zones when flying west. A 6-hour eastward shift can take 5–7 days to fully resolve without intervention.

Is jet lag worse flying east or west?

Eastward travel is generally harder. The human circadian clock runs slightly longer than 24 hours, making it easier to delay sleep than to advance it. Eastward recovery takes about 1 hour per day, while westward recovery is faster at about 1.5 hours per day.

How do I calculate my jet lag recovery time?

Divide the number of time zones crossed by the daily adjustment rate. Eastward: zones ÷ 1 hour per day. Westward: zones ÷ 1.5 hours per day. Pre-trip sleep shifting and timed light exposure can cut recovery by 30–50%.

When should I take melatonin for jet lag?

For eastward travel, take 0.5 mg in the afternoon or early evening at destination time. For westward travel, take it in the morning at destination time. Timing is critical; taking melatonin at the wrong time can make jet lag worse.

How can I avoid jet lag?

Shift your sleep schedule 2–3 days before departure, stay hydrated on the flight, avoid alcohol and caffeine, get morning sunlight for eastward trips or evening light for westward trips, and eat meals on the destination's schedule from day one.

Does this jet lag calculator account for daylight saving time?

The calculator uses standard UTC offsets for each city. You may need to adjust by one hour if your travel date falls during daylight saving time in your origin or destination country.
