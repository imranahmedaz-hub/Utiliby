What this free travel budget calculator does

The best way to enjoy a trip is to know what it will cost before you leave. A travel budget calculator turns a vague idea of how much to save into a number. This free online tool adds up flights, accommodation, food, local transport, activities, insurance, visas, a phone plan, shopping and other costs, adds a contingency buffer and gives the total, the cost per person and the cost per person per day.

Four tabs cover the whole job. The budget tab builds the total with a category chart. The savings plan tab divides what you still need over the weeks left before departure. The currency tab estimates card and ATM fees and the amount of local cash to carry. The compare regions tab shows what the same trip would cost in 13 parts of the world. You can fill typical costs for a region and style in one click, then adjust each figure. It runs in your browser.

How to use the travel budget calculator

  1. Enter travellers, nights and rooms.
  2. Pick a region and travel style to fill typical daily costs, or type your own.
  3. Add flights, insurance, visas, SIM, shopping and other costs.
  4. Set a buffer and, optionally, your budget limit to see if you are over or under.
  5. Open the savings plan to see how much to put aside each month.
  6. Check currency fees and compare regions, then print or download the budget.

How the trip cost is calculated

Accommodation is the room cost multiplied by rooms and nights. Food, local transport and activities are per person per day, multiplied by travellers and by days, where days are nights plus one. Flights, insurance, visa and SIM costs are per person. Shopping and other costs are totals. The buffer is a percentage of the subtotal. For two travellers on 7 nights in Southern Europe at a mid-range style, with flights of 450 dollars each, the subtotal is 3,196 dollars and the total with a 10 percent buffer is 3,516 dollars, which is 1,758 dollars per person or about 220 dollars per person per day.

Saving for a trip

If your trip costs 3,516 dollars and you already have 500, you need 3,016 more. With 120 days to go, that is about 17 weeks, so around 176 dollars a week or 765 dollars a month. Seeing the weekly figure often makes a goal feel more real, and it also shows when a trip is not affordable yet, so you can pick a cheaper style, a closer region or a later date.

Currency, cards and cash

Foreign transaction fees are commonly 1 to 3 percent of the amount spent, and ATMs may add a flat fee on top. On 4,000 dollars of spend abroad with 30 percent in cash, a 2 percent fee costs about 80 dollars before ATM charges. A card with no foreign fee or an account built for travel can remove most of this. When you pay by card abroad, choose to be charged in the local currency rather than your own, because the shop's conversion rate usually includes a hidden mark-up.

Ways to stretch a travel budget

  • Shoulder season often means lower prices and fewer crowds.
  • Flexible dates can cut flight costs significantly.
  • Accommodation with a kitchen trims the food bill.
  • Free attractions and city passes reduce activity costs.
  • Local transport costs far less than taxis.
  • Insurance protects the money you have already spent.

Common travel budgeting mistakes

Most trips run over budget for predictable reasons. The first is forgetting costs that happen before you leave, such as visas, insurance, vaccinations, a new bag or a passport renewal. The second is underestimating daily spending: food and drink, small transport trips and entrance fees add up faster than the nightly hotel rate suggests. Many travellers also ignore currency costs, which can add 2 to 5 percent to what they spend, and forget airport transfers, baggage fees and seat selection on flights.

Another frequent mistake is not allowing a buffer. Delays, missed connections, a sudden illness or a once-in-a-lifetime excursion can all cost money, and a 10 percent contingency turns these into manageable surprises. It also pays to budget separately for the days you travel, when you tend to eat out and take taxis, and for the day you return, when you may need a meal, parking or a transfer. Finally, track your spending during the trip. If you are running ahead of the daily figure, you can adjust early, and the numbers you record make the next budget more accurate.

Frequently asked questions

How do I budget for a trip?

List the big costs first: flights, accommodation, insurance and visas. Then estimate daily costs for food, local transport and activities, multiply by people and days, add shopping and a contingency of about 10 percent.

How much should I budget per day for travel?

It depends on the region and style. The presets here range from about 30 dollars a day per person for budget travel in parts of Asia to 400 dollars or more for comfortable travel in northern Europe. These are rough estimates, so adjust them.

Why is the buffer 10 percent by default?

Trips almost always cost more than planned because of changes, fees, tips and impulse purchases. Ten percent is a common starting point, and you can raise it for long or complex trips.

Are days nights plus one?

Yes. A 7-night trip includes 8 calendar days, so daily costs such as food are multiplied by 8.

How do rooms and travellers work?

Enter the cost of one room per night and the number of rooms. Two people sharing a double room is one room. Daily costs are per person.

What does the savings plan do?

Enter your departure date and what you have saved. It divides the amount still needed by the weeks and months left to give a weekly, monthly and daily saving target.

How are currency fees estimated?

The currency tab applies your foreign transaction fee to card spending, and adds a flat ATM fee for each withdrawal plus the percentage fee on cash. It also shows how much local currency to withdraw.

How should I split cash and card?

Cards are safer and usually give better rates, but small vendors may be cash only. Many travellers carry enough cash for a few days and use cards for the rest.

Where do the regional presets come from?

They are rough planning estimates by region and travel style, designed to give a sensible starting point. They are not official statistics, so replace them with current prices for your destination.

How can I convert the presets to my currency?

Enter how many units of your currency equal one US dollar, and the presets convert when you choose a region or style.

What costs do people forget in a travel budget?

Airport transfers, baggage fees, seat selection, visa and entry fees, travel insurance, tips, city taxes, phone data, laundry and the cost of getting to the departure airport.

How can I cut the cost of a trip?

Travel outside peak season, book flights and hotels early, stay longer in fewer places, choose accommodation with a kitchen, use public transport and use a card without foreign fees.

What does the compare regions tab show?

It estimates land costs for your number of travellers and days in 13 world regions at your chosen style, so you can see where your money goes furthest.

Is travel insurance part of the budget?

It should be. A policy often costs a small share of the trip but can protect you from large medical or cancellation bills.

Can I use it for group trips?

Yes. Set the number of travellers and rooms, and the per-person figure shows each person's share.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

No. All calculations run in your browser and nothing is uploaded.

Is this travel budget calculator free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up.
