What this free meme generator does

A meme is a picture and a few words that land a joke or a feeling. You do not need design skills to make one, only a quick way to put clear text on a picture. This free online meme generator lets you add text to any image in your browser, style it, drag it into position and download the finished meme as PNG or JPG.

It offers the classic look with bold outlined capital letters, plus a modern caption-bar layout, several text boxes, five fonts, color and outline controls, and blank canvases in square, portrait, wide and tall sizes. Your pictures stay on your device, and there is no watermark and no sign-up.

How to make a meme in four steps

  1. Choose a picture. Drop your own photo or screenshot, or use a blank canvas with a background color.
  2. Write the text. Pick Text 1 or Text 2 from the Edit text list and type. Use Add text for more lines.
  3. Style and place it. Choose the font, size, colors and outline. Drag the text on the picture to move it.
  4. Download. Choose PNG or JPG and press Download meme.

Making text readable

  • Keep it short. Two short lines beat one long paragraph.
  • Use contrast. White with a black outline works on nearly everything. On a pale picture, try dark text with a white outline.
  • Raise the outline if the background is busy, but not so far that the letters look blobby.
  • Avoid faces. Place text over empty areas where possible.
  • Check on a small screen. Most memes are read on phones, so the text must still be easy to read when it is small.

How to write a caption that works

  • One idea per meme. If you need a paragraph to explain it, the joke is probably too complicated.
  • Keep each line short. Around ten words or fewer reads well on a phone.
  • Set up, then land it. Put the setup first and the punchline last, which is why top and bottom text works.
  • Match the picture to the feeling. The text and the face or scene should point the same way, or clearly contrast on purpose.
  • Read it out loud. If you stumble, simplify it.

Images, rights and good taste

The picture matters as much as the joke, and the picture usually has an owner. Using someone else's copyrighted image can infringe their rights, even if it is widely shared. The safest sources are your own photos, images in the public domain, and pictures offered under a Creative Commons license that allows your use, with the credit the license asks for. Whether a particular use counts as fair use depends on the facts and the country, so treat it as a question for a lawyer, not a guess.

Do not use memes to harass people, spread false claims or target private individuals. When you post a meme, add alt text so people who use screen readers can follow the joke.

Troubleshooting

  • The font looks different: Impact is missing on your device, so a similar font is used. Choose Arial if you want the same look everywhere.
  • The text is cut off: reduce the size or shorten the line. Long text wraps to several lines, but very large text can run over the edge.
  • I cannot drag the text: click directly on the letters. The dashed box shows which text is selected.
  • The picture will not open: HEIC photos open only in Safari. Use JPG or PNG.
  • The file is too big: set an output width such as 1080, or save as JPG.
