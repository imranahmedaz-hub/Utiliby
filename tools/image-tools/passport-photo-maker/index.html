What this free passport photo maker does

A passport photo has to be an exact size, with the head at a set size and position inside the frame. Studios charge for this, and most free online tools either upload your photo to a server, add a watermark or push you toward a paid print service. This free online passport photo maker does the cropping and sizing in your browser. Your photo stays on your device.

You choose a country or enter a custom size, drag your photo under head-size guides, adjust zoom, straightening, brightness and contrast, and download either a single photo at the exact pixel size or a ready-to-print sheet of copies on 4 × 6 in, 5 × 7 in, A4 or Letter paper. There is no sign-up and no watermark.

How to make a passport photo in five steps

  1. Take a good photo. Stand in front of a plain, light wall in even daylight. Face the camera straight on with a neutral expression and both eyes open.
  2. Add the photo. Drop it onto the drop area or click to choose it.
  3. Choose the size. Pick your country, or choose Custom and enter the width and height in millimeters.
  4. Line up your face. Drag the photo and use Zoom so the top of your head touches the top line and your chin falls inside the shaded band. Use Straighten if the picture is tilted.
  5. Download. Save the single photo for an online upload, or the print sheet for printing at a lab or at home.

How the head-size guides work

Most passport photo rules measure the head from the bottom of the chin to the top of the head, not including hair that rises above it. The tool draws a line for the top of the head and a shaded band where the chin should fall, based on the typical range for the size you chose. A faint cross shows the center line and a rough eye line. A dashed oval shows the proportion of a typical face.

The guides are a visual aid and not a measurement. They do not detect your face. Use them to get the head close to the right size and keep the face centered, then compare the result with the official examples.

Printing: sheets, paper and cut lines

The print sheet places as many copies as fit on the paper you choose, keeping a small margin and a narrow gap. It tries both paper orientations and a few margin sizes and keeps the layout with more photos. A 4 × 6 in sheet of 2 × 2 in photos has no spare room, so six copies fill the paper edge to edge. Ask the lab to print without cropping or shrinking. You can set the number of copies, or leave the field blank to fill the page. Cut lines draw a thin gray border around each photo so you can trim neatly with a ruler and a blade.

For accurate sizes, print at 100% or "actual size". Printer options like "fit to page" or "scale to fit" shrink the sheet and the photos will come out too small. Measure one photo with a ruler after printing. A photo lab will print the sheet on real photo paper, which usually looks better than home paper.

Getting a photo that will be accepted

  • Plain background: a light, even wall with no pattern and no shadows behind your head.
  • Even light: face a window. Avoid a flash that makes glare or red eyes.
  • Straight on: camera at eye level, head not tilted, neutral expression, mouth closed.
  • No heavy edits: do not smooth skin or change your features. Offices reject photos that no longer look like you.
  • Sharp and recent: use a recent, in-focus photo at full resolution, not a screenshot.
  • Check the current rules: hats, glasses and head coverings are treated differently in each country.

Uploading to an online form

Some governments let you upload a digital photo. They usually ask for a minimum and maximum pixel size, a maximum file size and the JPG format. Use the 300 dpi setting for the pixel size, choose JPG, and type the limit in Max file size. The tool lowers the JPG quality step by step until the file fits. The download reports the final size. If the form rejects the photo, read its message, because many automatic checks look at the lighting, background and head position, not only the size.

Troubleshooting

  • The photo looks soft: your original is too small for the size. Use the full camera file.
  • There are colored corners: you zoomed out or rotated. Zoom in until the photo fills the frame, or change the fill color.
  • The printed photo is too small: print at 100% scale with no fit-to-page option.
  • My face looks wide: a phone's wide-angle lens distorts close-up faces. Retake the photo from farther away and zoom in.
  • The file is above the limit: set Max file size, or choose 200 dpi.
  • HEIC does not open: HEIC opens only in Safari. Convert it to JPG first.

Privacy and how it works

Your browser opens the photo and draws it onto a canvas that has the exact pixel size you chose. The tool applies zoom, rotation, brightness and contrast on that canvas, and saves the result as a JPG or PNG with the resolution written into the file. Nothing is uploaded, stored or logged. The saved file has no camera data or GPS location.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a passport photo online for free?

Add a front-facing photo of yourself, choose your country or enter a custom size, and drag the photo until your face sits between the guide lines. Press Download photo for a single image at the exact pixel size, or Download print sheet for a page of copies you can print.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The photo is opened and resized in your own browser. Nothing is sent to Utiliby or to any other server, which matters for an ID photo.

What size is a passport photo?

It depends on the country. A United States passport photo is 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm). The United Kingdom, Schengen countries, Australia and Japan use 35 × 45 mm. Canada uses 50 × 70 mm and China 33 × 48 mm. Choose your country in the list or enter any size in millimeters.

What is the pixel size of a US passport photo?

The US online photo upload asks for a square image between 600 × 600 and 1200 × 1200 pixels. The 2 × 2 in setting at 300 dpi gives 600 × 600 pixels. Check the current State Department page for the latest rules before you upload.

Does this tool remove the background?

No. It does not remove or replace backgrounds. Take the photo in front of a plain, light wall with even light, or the office may reject it. The fill color only colors empty areas that appear if you zoom out or rotate the photo.

How do I know my face is the right size?

Turn on the head-size guides. The shaded band shows where your chin should fall and the top line marks the crown of your head. The guides use the approximate head-height range for the country you chose, so confirm the exact rule on the official site.

Can I print passport photos at home?

Yes. Download the print sheet, which is a 4 × 6 in, 5 × 7 in, A4 or Letter page with several copies. Print it on photo paper at 100% scale, which means no "fit to page", then cut along the lines. Many photo labs and stores will print the sheet for a small fee.

What is a 4 × 6 print sheet?

It is the common photo-lab size. A 4 × 6 in sheet holds two 2 × 2 in photos across and three down, or eight 35 × 45 mm photos in a grid when it is rotated. The tool picks the layout that fits the most copies.

What resolution should a passport photo be?

300 dpi is the usual print resolution. Some online upload forms care about pixels or file size rather than dpi. Use 300 dpi for printing and set the Max file size field if a form has a limit such as 240 KB or 500 KB.

How do I keep the file under a size limit?

Type the limit in the Max file size field and choose JPG. The tool lowers the JPG quality until the file fits. If the file is still too big, choose 200 dpi or a smaller custom size.

Can I use a photo taken on my phone?

Yes, if it is sharp, evenly lit and taken straight on at eye level. Avoid wide-angle selfies, which distort the face. Ask someone to take it from about arm's length away, or use a tripod and a timer.

What are the usual passport photo rules?

Most countries ask for a recent, in-focus photo with a neutral expression, both eyes open, a plain light background, no shadows and no glasses glare. Rules differ for hats, glasses and religious head coverings. Read the current rules from the official passport office.

Will my photo be accepted?

This tool cannot promise that. It sizes and positions your photo, but acceptance depends on the quality of the original, the lighting, the background and the rules of the receiving office. Keep a spare set of prints and read the official checklist.

Can I make a visa photo or an ID card photo?

Yes. Choose the matching size or use the custom millimeter fields. Many visas and ID cards use 35 × 45 mm or 2 × 2 in, but some use other sizes, so check the application.

Why is the image blurry when I zoom in?

Zooming enlarges the pixels of your original. A photo that is too small cannot produce a sharp result at 300 dpi. Use the original, uncompressed photo from your camera, and avoid screenshots or messaging apps that shrink pictures.

Does it work on phones?

Yes. Drag with your finger to move the photo and use the zoom slider. Very large photos may use a lot of memory on older phones. If the photo does not open, try a smaller copy.

What can I print or save from the page?

Download photo saves one image at the exact size. Download print sheet saves a page of copies. Print / save summary prints a short report with the settings and the sheet. Copy summary copies the settings as text.
