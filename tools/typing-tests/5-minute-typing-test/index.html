What this free 5 minute typing test does

This 5 minute typing test gives you 300 seconds to type as much of the text as you can. The clock starts with your first key. When time is up you get your net words per minute (WPM), gross speed, accuracy, word and character counts, a chart in 30-second steps and a table that shows your WPM and accuracy for each minute. That breakdown is the main reason to take a longer test. It shows not only how fast you type, but whether you can keep that speed.

The test defaults to paragraphs, which are close to real writing, and you can switch to common words, sentences with punctuation or words with numbers. You can copy the results, download them as a CSV file or print a summary. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you type is uploaded. For a shorter check, use the 1 minute typing test, or pick any length on the typing speed test.

How to take the test

  1. Set up first. Sit with straight wrists, a relaxed back and the screen at eye level. Have water nearby so you do not need to stop.
  2. Choose a text type. Paragraphs are the default. Common words give the cleanest speed score.
  3. Click the box and start. The 300 seconds begin with your first key.
  4. Pace yourself. Aim for a speed you can hold for five minutes, a little below your one-minute best.
  5. Check the breakdown. Compare the first and last minute in the table. Press Reset or Esc to try again with new text.

How to read your results

Net WPM is your correct characters divided by five, then divided by the five minutes. Gross WPM counts every character you typed. Accuracy is the share of key presses that were correct, with every wrong key press counted even if you fixed it. The table gives each minute its own net WPM and accuracy. To find your fade, subtract the last minute from the first and divide by the first. If minute one was 60 WPM and minute five was 54 WPM, you faded by 10%. A small drop is normal, a large drop means you started too fast or you are getting tired. A rise means you were slow to warm up.

For context, a study of 168,000 typists recorded an average speed of about 51.6 WPM with 1.17% uncorrected errors, and the fastest 10% typed above roughly 78 WPM. Those figures come from short tests, so a five-minute score slightly below your one-minute best is not a bad sign.

Why a 5 minute test shows more than a 1 minute test

A one-minute test is mostly a sprint. A five-minute test mixes a warm-up, a long steady stretch and the moment when attention drifts. It averages out lucky streaks and bad words, so it is a fairer picture of the speed you can use for an ordinary piece of writing, a long email or a data entry session. It also shows your consistency, because two people with the same one-minute score can finish a long test very differently. One keeps a flat line, and the other starts fast and fades. Typing jobs and courses that ask for a sustained rate care about the flat line.

How to pace a five minute test

  • Start a little below your best. A 5% easier pace in the first minute usually saves more time than it costs.
  • Keep a metronome rhythm. An even beat is easier on your hands and gives fewer errors than bursts.
  • Do not stop for old mistakes. Fix a slip if you spot it at once, otherwise carry on.
  • Read ahead. Keep your eyes a couple of words in front of your fingers. This is the single biggest habit of fast typists.
  • Use the middle for speed. Once you are warm, you can lift your pace slightly. Do not make the last minute a sprint unless your accuracy holds.

Fatigue, breaks and comfort

Five minutes of fast typing is a small effort, but it shows where your technique is tense. If your shoulders rise, your wrists bend upward or you press the keys hard, speed and accuracy fall in the later minutes. Keep your wrists straight and relaxed, your elbows near a right angle and your fingers curved, as workplace safety guidance such as OSHA's computer workstation advice suggests. Rest your hands for a minute between runs, and stop if you feel pain, tingling or numbness. Do not repeat long tests back to back, because tired hands give a lower score that does not reflect your skill.

How to build typing stamina

  • Build accuracy first. Mistakes cost time to notice and fix, and they pile up when you are tired.
  • Practise in blocks. Do two or three minutes of focused typing a few times a day instead of one long session.
  • Extend gradually. Move from 1 minute to 2, then 3, then 5 as your fade shrinks.
  • Use real text. Paragraph mode includes capitals and punctuation, so your fingers get used to Shift and the symbol keys.
  • Work on rhythm. A steady speed that you can hold is better than a high speed you cannot.
  • Look after your setup. A comfortable chair, a keyboard at the right height and regular breaks make long tests easier.

When a 5 minute test is useful

A five-minute test suits anyone who types for long stretches: writers, students, transcribers, support agents and people preparing for a typing assessment that uses a longer timed passage. It helps you set a sustained target, such as 45 WPM held for five minutes at 97% accuracy, instead of chasing a one-minute peak. It also helps you check a new keyboard or a change in technique. If the later minutes get better after a change, the change is working. Employers and courses set their own tests and standards, so use this as practice, not as a replacement for the real assessment.

Frequently asked questions

How does the 5 minute typing test work?

You type the text shown for 300 seconds. The timer starts with your first key. When time is up you see your net WPM, accuracy, a speed chart and a minute-by-minute table.

How is WPM calculated over five minutes?

Net WPM is your correct characters divided by five, then divided by five minutes. So 1,250 correct characters in five minutes is 250 words, which is 50 WPM.

What is a good score on a 5 minute typing test?

About 40 to 60 WPM with accuracy above 95% is good for most people. Because five minutes is a long test, a score close to your one-minute best means good stamina.

Why is my 5 minute score lower than my 1 minute score?

Fatigue, small pauses and loss of focus build up over longer tests, so sustained speed is usually below a short burst. A small drop is normal.

What does the minute-by-minute table show?

It shows your net WPM and accuracy for each minute of the test, so you can see whether you started fast and faded, stayed steady or warmed up slowly.

How do I calculate my fade?

Subtract your last-minute WPM from your first-minute WPM and divide by the first. For example, 60 down to 54 is a fade of 10%.

How many words is 40 WPM in five minutes?

At 40 WPM you type about 200 standard words of five characters, or 1,000 characters, in five minutes.

How many words is 60 WPM in five minutes?

At 60 WPM you type about 300 standard words, or 1,500 characters, in five minutes. That is five key presses a second held for 300 seconds.

Should I fix mistakes during a 5 minute test?

Fix a mistake if you spot it straight away, but do not go back to old errors. Stopping costs time. All wrong key presses count against your accuracy.

How should I pace myself?

Start a little below your best speed, hold an even rhythm and lift your pace slightly in the middle if you feel warm and accurate. Avoid a sprint at the start.

How can I build typing stamina?

Practise in short blocks several times a day, extend test lengths gradually, build accuracy first, keep your wrists relaxed and take regular breaks.

Is a 5 minute test better than a 1 minute test?

They answer different questions. One minute shows peak speed, and five minutes shows sustained speed and consistency. Use both, the one-minute test for speed and this one for stamina.

Which text type should I use?

Paragraphs are the default and are close to real writing. Use common words for the cleanest speed score, punctuation to practise Shift and symbols, and numbers for data entry practice.

What is the difference between gross and net WPM?

Gross WPM counts all characters typed. Net WPM counts only correct characters, so mistakes lower it. Net WPM is the main result.

How is accuracy calculated?

Accuracy is the share of key presses that were correct. Every wrong key press counts, even one you fixed with Backspace, so it can be lower than in tests that only look at the final text.

Can typing for five minutes hurt my hands?

Short tests are fine for most people, but poor posture, tense wrists or a bad keyboard height can cause strain. Rest between runs and stop if you feel pain, tingling or numbness.

Can I paste text into the box?

No. Pasting, cutting and dropping text are turned off so your result reflects real typing.

Can I get a typing certificate from this test?

No. This is a practice tool, not an official exam. Courses and employers use their own tests and rules, so check what they accept.

Does this work on a phone?

It works with the on-screen keyboard, but speeds are lower on a touch screen and five minutes is tiring on a phone. Compare phone scores only with other phone scores.

Do you store what I type?

No. The test runs in your browser. Nothing you type is uploaded or stored, and your results disappear when you press Reset or close the page.
