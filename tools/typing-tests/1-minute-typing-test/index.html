What this free 1 minute typing test does

This 1 minute typing test gives you exactly 60 seconds to type as much of the text as you can. The clock starts with your first key, so you can read the first words and settle your hands before you begin. When the minute ends you see your net words per minute (WPM), your gross speed, your accuracy, how many words you got right, and a chart of your speed in five-second steps.

You can choose the text: common words, paragraphs, sentences with punctuation, or words mixed with numbers. You can copy the results, download them as a CSV file or print a summary. The test runs in your browser and nothing you type is uploaded. If you want longer tests, use the main typing speed test or the 5 minute typing test.

How to take the test

  1. Choose a text type. Common words give the cleanest speed score. Paragraphs are closer to real writing.
  2. Click the box. Read the first line while your hands rest on the home row.
  3. Start typing. The 60 seconds begin with your first key. Type the text exactly, including capitals and punctuation.
  4. Keep moving. If you notice a slip straight away, fix it. Otherwise carry on, because stopping costs more time than it saves in a short test.
  5. Check the result. Press Reset or Esc for new text. Run it three times and read the middle result.

What your 60-second score means

Your net WPM is the number of correct characters divided by five. In a one-minute test that is simply the correct characters typed, divided by five, because the time is already one minute. If you type 250 correct characters, your net speed is 50 WPM. The gross WPM uses all characters, right or wrong, and accuracy shows the share of key presses that were correct. A large study of 168,000 typists found an average speed of about 51.6 WPM with 1.17% uncorrected errors, and the fastest 10% above roughly 78 WPM. If you are near 50 WPM with 95% accuracy or more, you are around the typical level.

Why one minute is the standard test

A one-minute test is long enough to include a warm-up, a steady stretch and a finish, and short enough to repeat several times in a sitting. It is also the unit of the measurement itself, since WPM means words per minute, so the score is easy to read without any conversion. The downside is variation. In only 60 seconds, a few slips, a stuck finger or a hard word can change your score by several WPM. That is why one run is a snapshot, not a verdict. Longer tests such as 3 or 5 minutes smooth the noise but are tiring, and your score is usually a bit lower than your best minute.

A simple way to run the test

For a fair result, do a short warm-up first. Type a few lines of anything for about 30 seconds so your fingers are loose. Then take three one-minute runs with a short rest between them, and write down the middle score. The middle score is less likely to be a fluke than the best or the worst. Test at about the same time of day, on the same keyboard, with the same text type. If you change the keyboard, the text type or the position of your hands, your score changes for reasons that have nothing to do with your skill.

How to raise your one-minute score

  • Do not start with a sprint. A frantic first ten seconds produces mistakes, and fixing them costs the time you gained.
  • Read ahead. Keep your eyes two or three words in front of your fingers.
  • Hold an even rhythm. Aim for equal gaps between keys, not fast bursts and pauses.
  • Keep your wrists relaxed. Tense hands slow down and tire quickly.
  • Raise speed in small steps. Once your accuracy is above 95%, push your pace by about 5%. If accuracy drops, go back one step.
  • Let your fingers overlap. The large typing study found that fast typists often press the next key before they release the previous one.

Common mistakes in a short test

  • Chasing every error. Going back to fix an old mistake in the middle of a line usually loses more than it gains.
  • Pressing Shift too late or too early. This is a common slip with capitals and punctuation, so the sentence and paragraph modes are good practice.
  • Reading the word and typing from memory. Glance at the text, do not try to memorise it, and look at the next word before you finish the current one.
  • Judging yourself on one run. A single minute is noisy. Use the median of three.
  • Testing when tired. Poor sleep and cold hands both lower scores.

60-second practice drills

Use the same one-minute window for short drills. Spend a minute on accuracy only, typing slowly with no mistakes. Spend a minute on rhythm, using a steady beat in your head. Spend a minute on weak keys, repeating words that contain them. Spend a minute on numbers if your digits are slow. Finish with a normal test and see whether the score has moved. Ten minutes of drills a day, with one test at the end, is a simple routine that works better than one long weekly session.

Frequently asked questions

How does the 1 minute typing test work?

You type the text shown for 60 seconds. The timer starts with your first key. When time is up you see your net WPM, accuracy, gross speed and a chart of your speed.

How do I calculate my WPM in one minute?

Divide your correct characters by five. For example, 250 correct characters in 60 seconds is 50 WPM. The test does this for you and also shows your gross speed and accuracy.

What is a good score on a 1 minute typing test?

About 40 to 60 WPM with accuracy above 95% is good for most people. A large study found an average of roughly 51.6 WPM, and the fastest 10% typed above about 78 WPM.

Is 60 WPM good?

Yes. 60 WPM is above the typical speed found in large studies and enough for most office and study work, provided your accuracy is high. It means five key presses every second.

Is 40 WPM good?

40 WPM is a little below the average found in large studies but fine for everyday use. It also means you can improve quickly with a few weeks of practice.

How many words is 50 WPM in one minute?

At 50 WPM you type 50 standard words of five characters, which is 250 characters, in one minute. In real text with longer or shorter words the count of actual words varies.

How many characters per minute is 60 WPM?

60 WPM equals 300 characters per minute, which is about 5 keys every second. Multiply your WPM by five to get characters per minute.

Why does my score change from run to run?

One minute is short, so a few slips or a hard word can change the score by several WPM. Take three runs and use the middle score to see your real level.

Should I fix mistakes during the test?

Fix a mistake if you spot it straight away. Do not go back to old errors, because stopping costs time. Both corrected and uncorrected mistakes affect your accuracy and net speed.

What is the difference between gross and net WPM?

Gross WPM counts all characters typed. Net WPM counts only correct characters, so mistakes lower it. Net WPM is the more useful number and is the main result.

How is accuracy calculated?

Accuracy is the share of key presses that were correct. Every wrong key press counts, even if you fixed it with Backspace afterwards, so it can be lower than in tests that only compare the final text.

Why does the timer wait for my first key?

So you can read the first words and settle your hands without losing time. The 60 seconds start when you press the first key.

Which text type gives the highest score?

Common words usually give the highest WPM, because they are short, lower case and have no symbols. Paragraphs, punctuation and numbers are slower for most people.

How can I type faster in one minute?

Do not sprint at the start, read ahead, keep an even rhythm, relax your wrists and raise your pace in small steps once accuracy is above 95%. Short daily practice helps most.

How often should I take the test?

Once a day is enough. Take three runs, write down the middle score, and compare weekly. Testing too often when tired can make you think you are not improving.

Can I use this test on my phone?

It works with the on-screen keyboard on a phone or tablet. Phone speeds are lower than keyboard speeds, so compare phone scores only with other phone scores.

Can I paste text into the box?

No. Pasting, cutting and dropping text are turned off so your result reflects real typing.

Is this 1 minute typing test free?

Yes. It is free to use, needs no sign-up and nothing is uploaded or stored. You can copy, download or print your results.

Can I get a typing certificate from this test?

No. This is a practice tool, not an official exam. If you need a certificate, check what the course or employer accepts, because they set their own tests and rules.

Why is my score different from another typing test?

Tests differ in their text, in how they count words and errors, and in how they time you. Compare scores only within one tool.
