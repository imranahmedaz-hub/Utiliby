The formula explained

For an open belt drive, the belt length is:

L = 2C + 1.57(D + d) + (D − d)² / (4C)

  • L — belt length (pitch length)
  • C — center distance between pulley axes
  • D — large pulley pitch diameter
  • d — small pulley pitch diameter
  • 1.57 ≈ π/2

The formula has three parts: the two straight belt runs (2C), the arc length around both pulleys (1.57 × (D + d)), and a correction for the size difference ((D − d)² / 4C).

Wrap angle

The wrap angle is how much of the small pulley's circumference the belt contacts. It should be at least 120° for the belt to grip without slipping. Below that, increase center distance or reduce the pulley size difference.

Pitch diameter vs outer diameter

For V-belts, always use pitch diameter — the circle where the belt sits inside the pulley groove. Using outer diameter gives a belt that is too long.

Worked example

Metric: d = 100 mm, D = 250 mm, C = 500 mm.

L = 2(500) + 1.57(250 + 100) + (250 − 100)² / (4 × 500)
L = 1000 + 549.5 + 11.25 = 1560.75 mm

With 3% stretch allowance: ~1607 mm.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for V-belt length?

L = 2C + 1.57(D + d) + (D − d)² / (4C), where C is center distance and D, d are pulley pitch diameters.

What is a good wrap angle?

At least 120° on the small pulley. Below that, the belt may slip under load.

Should I use pitch or outer diameter?

Always use pitch diameter for V-belts. Outer diameter produces an incorrect belt length.

How much should I add for stretch?

Add 2–5% for belt stretch and tensioning adjustment.

Is anything I enter stored?

No. All calculations run in your browser.
