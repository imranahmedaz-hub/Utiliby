Risers, Treads, Going and Nosing

A riser is the vertical increase between adjacent finished walking surfaces. A tread is the horizontal walking component. Going is the horizontal distance from one tread nosing reference to the next. Nosing is the projection beyond the riser face; it does not add the same amount to every going when the projection is uniform.

For a conventional flight where the upper floor is the final walking surface, N risers normally mean N−1 separate tread pieces. Fifteen risers therefore usually mean fourteen installed stair treads. The optional top-level tread arrangement adds another horizontal tread footprint. It does not remove a riser or establish a standard flush-mount connection.

A tread board's physical front-to-back depth is not necessarily the same as the going. Going plus nosing is shown as a nominal surface-depth estimate. Riser-board thickness, housings, brackets, gaps and the connection detail can change the actual cut dimension. Enter going for the geometry; do not substitute a board label without checking how the stair is assembled.

The Stair Rise and Run Formulas

Equal rise R = finished total rise H ÷ riser count N.
Conventional tread count M = N−1.
Total horizontal footprint = M × going G.
Pitch angle θ = arctan(R/G).

The automatic option chooses a whole count near your preferred rise while ensuring the selected maximum riser is not exceeded. It does not impose a universal four-inch minimum riser. A manual count lets you examine a different layout, including one outside the entered reference maximum. Such results remain visible with an outside-limit status instead of being silently changed.

Solving from run divides that run by the selected tread count. Solving from pitch uses G = R/tan(θ). A shorter available run can force a small going or a steep pitch; it does not make those dimensions acceptable. The alternative-count table lets you compare exact rise, going, footprint and pitch under the same input method.

Worked 108-Inch Stair Example

Suppose finished rise is 108 inches, preferred rise is 7.5 inches, and the entered maximum is 7.75 inches. Fourteen risers give 108/14 = approximately 7.714286 inches. With a 10.5-inch going and the upper floor as final tread, there are thirteen separate treads and a 136.5-inch footprint. The pitch is arctan(7.714286/10.5), approximately 36.3 degrees.

If you choose fifteen risers manually, each rise becomes 7.2 inches, there are fourteen treads and the footprint is 147 inches at the same going. Pitch becomes approximately 34.4 degrees. Neither configuration is approved simply because its rise and going meet selected dimensions. Headroom, landings, handrails, guards and structural details remain part of the design.

Stringer Length Is More Than One Diagonal

The diagonal of the entire rise-and-footprint envelope is √(H² + run²). For a conventional N−1 tread layout, it includes an extra vertical riser compared with the sequence of equal rise/going modules. Its angle is therefore not the stair pitch. The pitch follows each equal R/G module and the line joining the successive nosing references.

Module diagonal = √(R² + G²).
Repeated-module layout length = M × √(R² + G²).
Planning blank = module layout length + entered total end allowance.

These are layout and purchasing estimates rather than an exact end-to-end stringer cut. End bearings, top hangers, lower support, tread overhangs and the chosen mounting detail determine the actual blank and final cuts. Additional top-level tread mode includes a conservative extra module in the planning length; the attachment geometry still needs its own detail.

Do not buy a board solely from an overall envelope diagonal rounded to a whole foot. Compare the selected stock length with the blank estimate, then verify the full-size layout and both ends. An allowance is a user-defined margin, not a structural design requirement.

Stringer Throat Depth and Notch Geometry

The remaining throat is measured perpendicular to the uncut board edges, not vertically below a tread. For this ideal rectangular notch pattern, perpendicular notch depth is R×G/√(R²+G²). Geometric remaining throat is actual stock depth minus that notch depth. Simply subtracting the vertical riser from board depth mixes measurement directions and gives a different result.

For R = 7.2 inches and G = 10.5 inches, the perpendicular notch depth is approximately 5.94 inches. In an actual 11.25-inch-deep board, the remaining geometric throat is approximately 5.31 inches. That number describes geometry; it does not prove the member can carry the design load or span.

The throat criterion is editable and is explicitly a user comparison. Structural capacity also depends on species, grade, treatment, moisture, span, supports, loading, damage and the cut quality. Engineered products require their manufacturer's permitted details. Do not assume a generic LVL depth or permission to notch it because the calculator can display a geometric throat.

Finished Floors, Tread Thickness and the Bottom Cut

Finished-rise mode takes your entered total rise as the finished walking-surface difference. Raw-floor mode adds the upper finish and subtracts the lower finish to find the finished rise. Those adjustments are applied to the total before equal risers are calculated. They do not automatically define every stringer end cut.

The bottom rough tread-seat result assumes that its support is at a raw level below the finished lower floor. Under that convention, seat rise above raw support is R − tread thickness + lower-floor finish above support. If the lower support is already the finished surface, enter zero for that separate support finish. For example, R = 7.2 inches and a 1.5-inch tread gives a 5.7-inch seat rise above a finished lower support.

Keep the lower finish used in raw total-rise conversion consistent with the actual lower support convention. The fields are separate because an indoor subfloor, a concrete pad, a deck connection and finished grade need not share the same datum. A nonpositive bottom seat is flagged for correction rather than described as a usable cut.

Cumulative Marking Coordinates and Fraction Rounding

The coordinate table gives the horizontal reference and finished elevation for every tread. In the standard arrangement, the first tread reference is X = 0, elevation R; the next is X = G, elevation 2R. Rough seat elevation subtracts the entered tread thickness. Nosing projection and connection offsets must be incorporated in your physical layout detail.

The table is not a full-scale cutting template. Printed diagrams are resized to fit the report, so measuring a line on the PDF does not give the real stair dimension. Decimal calculations retain precision; fractional values are rounded to your chosen 1/16, 1/32 or 1/64 inch. Fraction display correctly carries values near a whole inch.

Repeatedly marking a rounded riser can accumulate error. For a rise that is not an exact fraction at your chosen precision, establish cumulative elevations from the total instead of multiplying a rounded display increment. Uniformity must be checked between the completed walking surfaces after treads and finishes are installed.

Headroom and the Required Floor Opening

The Advanced tab uses opening length measured horizontally back from the upper-floor connection, actual total floor thickness, and an optional ceiling clearance above the upper floor. It assumes a standard top tread one rise down, a straight flight and a flat floor underside. Beams, trim, sloping ceilings and separate landing conditions are excluded.

At the opening edge, the nosing pitch line lies H − opening×R/G above the lower datum. The floor underside lies H − floor thickness above that datum. The modeled vertical clearance is therefore opening×R/G − floor thickness. The opening solving an entered clearance target is (target + floor thickness)×G/R.

For fourteen risers over 108 inches, G = 10.5 inches, floor thickness = 12 inches and an 80-inch target, the pitched-line opening target is approximately 125.22 inches. This is a geometric model result, not a complete stairwell specification. Increasing floor thickness or flattening the stair can require a longer opening.

If the entered opening covers the whole flight, the tool does not invent an under-floor clearance over a tread that is no longer covered. If the solved opening extends beyond the first tread, verify the lower approach and landing separately. An omitted upper-ceiling input leaves that overhead condition unverified. The profile depicts the opening edge and its modeled vertical clearance when applicable.

Selected Residential Reference Dimensions

The starting comparison inputs reflect selected 2021 IRC residential dimensions: maximum riser 7¾ inches, minimum going 10 inches, width above handrails 36 inches, headroom 80 inches and riser variation ⅜ inch. Solid-riser nosing inputs start at ¾ to 1¼ inches, with an 11-inch going threshold for the no-nosing minimum exception. The handrail trigger starts at four risers and height inputs at 34 to 38 inches.

These values identify a reference subset, not the locally adopted code or a complete interpretation. For example, official local deck details can use amended rise and going values. Edit the inputs to match verified project requirements, and review relevant exceptions, measurement locations and jurisdictional changes. The tool never awards an “IRC compliant” certificate.

Width below handrails differs from width above them. The Advanced tab includes an additional standard-reference comparison of 31½ inches with one handrail and 27 inches with two, using your entered intrusions. Those fixed subset values still require local verification. It does not check allowable projection, grip shape, returns, continuity, wall clearance or anchorage.

Landings, guards, doors, lighting, surface slope, opening limits, fire/egress conditions and structural loads are explicitly unevaluated. The entered maximum single-flight rise can prompt a landing review; it does not create a landing design or split the flight automatically. A passing mathematical row means only that its value meets the shown comparison.

Deck Stair Stringers, Stock and Materials

The Deck tab estimates a separate standard N−1 tread scenario. Transfer Layout to copy its finished rise, going, width, nosing and tread thickness; the tool explains when an additional top-level tread is excluded. Select an actual stock depth and thickness and the length of board you intend to buy.

With two outer stringer centre lines inset from the tread edges, supported width is stair width minus twice the inset. Actual equal support spacing is supported width divided by stringer count minus one. The suggested count is ceiling(supported width ÷ user-approved maximum spacing) plus one. This evaluates a spacing assumption; it does not size stringers or establish an allowable span.

The tread-board layout uses boards per tread, actual board width and gaps. Their assembled surface depth is compared with going plus nosing as an approximate coverage check. Composite products can have specific support spacing, fastening and overhang requirements. Use the manufacturer's current installation specification.

Tread pieces equal separate treads times boards per tread. Fasteners equal tread pieces times installed stringers times fasteners per connection. Purchase allowance rounds the selected stringer boards, tread pieces and fasteners upward. It does not increase the installed stringer count or recalculate structural support.

Board Feet Versus Linear Feet

Linear footage is total length. Board feet are a volume measure: thickness in inches × depth in inches × length in feet ÷ 12. This tool reports actual-volume board feet for the installed count of selected stock boards; the report also states linear footage. Lumber sellers may use nominal board-foot conventions, so confirm their billing method.

Use pressure-treatment, exposure rating, compatible corrosion-resistant hardware and suitable foundations as specified for the project. Choosing “concrete pad,” “gravel” or “soil” records an assumption; it does not verify that the support resists frost, movement or the imposed load.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a free stair stringer calculator online?

It calculates preliminary straight-flight rise, going, tread count, pitch, marking coordinates and material estimates from your inputs. Selected comparison rows are not full code approval.

What maximum riser height is used?

The starting maximum is 7¾ inches from selected 2021 IRC residential provisions. Local codes and amendments can differ, so verify the requirement and edit the comparison input.

What is the difference between tread depth and going?

Going is the horizontal distance between successive nosing references. Physical tread-board depth also depends on nosing, riser thickness and the assembly detail.

How do I calculate stair pitch?

Pitch is arctan(equal rise divided by unit going). The overall rise/footprint envelope angle can differ because the upper floor normally serves as the final tread.

How many treads are needed for fifteen risers?

Normally fourteen separate treads when the upper floor is the final walking surface. Additional top-level tread mode includes fifteen tread footprints and needs a separate connection detail.

How is stair headroom calculated?

For the stated flat-floor model, edge clearance is opening length × rise/going minus total floor thickness. Actual obstructions, ceilings, lower approaches and landings require independent checking.

Does the calculator verify handrails?

It compares entered rail presence, height and clear width in a limited scenario. Grip, continuity, returns, anchorage, projections and local rules remain unverified.

What is the stringer throat formula?

For the ideal notch geometry, remaining perpendicular throat is actual stock depth minus rise × going divided by the module diagonal. This geometric value is not a structural capacity check.

Can I use rounded fractions to repeat every riser?

Avoid accumulating rounded increments. Use the exact equal rise and cumulative elevations, then check the actual finished walking surfaces.

Can I notch an LVL stair stringer?

Only if the manufacturer permits the exact exposure, notch and attachment detail. This tool provides no generic approval for engineered-product cuts.

Does printing include the article?

No. Print and Save PDF export a separate calculation report. CSV, TXT and Copy report also exclude the article and website navigation.

Is this stair stringer calculator free?

Yes. This free online tool requires no sign-up and performs its arithmetic in your browser. Sitewide privacy practices are governed by the website policies.
