Use it as a planning worksheet for a deck foundation estimate, a concrete pier quantity check or a discussion with your contractor. A footing-size calculation is one part of a foundation design. Footing, pier, concrete tube and excavation: four different dimensions

A deck footing spreads load onto the supporting ground. A pier carries load upward from that bearing base to the post connection. A cylindrical concrete foundation may perform both functions; a narrower pier over a wider pad separates them. A concrete forming tube, often described as a Sonotube, establishes the shaft shape but does not automatically establish an adequate bearing base.

When a materials list calls for a 12-inch tube over a 24-inch spread footing, the bearing calculation uses the base area, while the concrete calculation includes both the pad and the pier. It would be incorrect to calculate all the concrete as a 24-inch cylinder or to use only the 12-inch shaft area as the pad's bearing area. Likewise, an excavation depth including a base layer is not the same as the depth to the underside of concrete.

Deck footing size calculation: load divided by allowable bearing pressure

For a centered downward load, the basic bearing-area relationship is A = W / qa. Here A is required bearing area, W is the total load included in the bearing check, and qa is the compatible allowable soil pressure. With W in pounds and qa in psf, A is in square feet. With force in kN and pressure in kPa, the resulting area is in square metres.

For a round footing, diameter in feet is √(4A / π); multiply by 12 for inches. For a square base, side in feet is √A. For a rectangular base with length-to-width ratio r, the unrounded width is √(A / r), and length is r times that width. This tool rounds the width upward to your increment and rounds the rectangular length upward separately. It also respects your entered minimum width or diameter.

The candidate-size control lets you evaluate an available tube or proposed pad. Candidate 0 uses the calculated rounded dimension. A positive candidate replaces that diameter or width and recalculates actual area and average pressure. A proposed base can therefore fail the entered bearing-pressure comparison even when the tool previously displayed a larger calculated size.

Worked bearing example: a confirmed 64 ft² equivalent load area at 50 psf produces 3,200 lb before additional allowances. At an allowable 1,500 psf, required area is 3,200 ÷ 1,500 = 2.133 ft². The round diameter is approximately 19.78 inches; rounding up in 2-inch increments gives 20 inches. A 20-inch round base has approximately 2.182 ft² of area and an average pressure of 1,467 psf. The square side for the same unrounded area is approximately 17.53 inches. These are bearing arithmetic examples, not approved footing dimensions or thicknesses.

The utilization percentage is calculated average pressure divided by entered allowable pressure. Below 100% means that this centered bearing comparison is within the supplied pressure. It says nothing about settlement limits, bending or punching of the pad, off-center posts, overturning, uplift, lateral resistance or the reliability of the input soil value.

Tributary area, deck post spacing and cantilever reactions

Tributary area describes the deck load assigned to a particular support. Interior posts, end posts, corners and posts receiving stair or landing loads can carry different reactions. Halfway-to-adjacent-support geometry is useful for appropriate regular framing, but it is not a universal reaction analysis. Unequal spans, beam continuity, joist cantilevers and concentrated loads can change the result.

Use the known tributary area mode when you have established the area appropriate to the post and load case. Use the known post reaction mode when a designer or a suitable structural analysis provides a downward service-level reaction directly. The latter avoids converting a nonuniform load back into a fictitious deck area. Extra downward reaction can be added only when it is a load at this specific post, not merely the weight of an object somewhere on the deck.

The optional simple two-post beam model

This model assumes joists uniformly loaded over a support span J from a ledger to a beam, with a joist cantilever c beyond that beam. Static equilibrium gives a beam reaction per foot of beam length equal to surface load multiplied by (J + c)² / (2J). With no cantilever, this reduces to half the joist support span. With a cantilever, it includes the additional cantilever contribution and its effect on the support reactions.

The resulting uniform beam line load is then applied to one beam supported by exactly two posts separated by span S. Let a be the loaded beam overhang to the left and b the loaded overhang to the right. The right reaction is w[(S + b)² − a²] / (2S), and the left reaction is w(S + a + b) − right reaction. The worksheet displays both reactions and lets you size for either post or the larger reaction.

These equations describe the stated static model, not an arbitrary freestanding deck or a continuous beam over three or more posts. The displayed equivalent area is reaction divided by uniform surface load. If the model produces an upward reaction, the bearing result is withheld because anchorage and uplift need a separate design. Joist and beam strength, deflection and allowable overhangs remain outside the calculator.

Live load, dead load, snow load and heavy deck features

Live load represents the appropriate occupancy loading; dead load represents the permanent supported construction. The surface-load selector offers dead plus live, dead plus snow, dead plus the larger of live or snow, or a user-selected sum. These are transparent worksheet scenarios. They are not a substitute for the governing load combinations in the project design, and a climate-map ground snow number is not automatically the correct deck snow load.

The familiar 40 psf live and 10 psf dead values appear as examples. They may be unsuitable for unusually heavy finishes, public occupancy, roofs, snow drift, masonry, planters, storage, hot tubs or other special features. A hot tub introduces substantial and often concentrated loads. Do not spread its weight uniformly over the whole deck or assume the simple beam mode determines its post reactions.

Allowable-bearing checks must use a compatible load basis. Do not combine a factored ultimate reaction with an allowable service pressure without the necessary design interpretation. Foundation self-weight and soil over a footing may also matter. The additional bearing-load allowance provides a place for an independently established value; it does not calculate gross-to-net bearing adjustments or decide whether those weights should be included twice.

What soil bearing capacity should I use for deck footings?

Use a value permitted for the actual bearing stratum and project by your local authority or geotechnical/design documents. Soil appearance alone cannot establish allowable bearing pressure. A dropdown that labels a generic pressure as “bedrock” or “sand” is not a field investigation, and increasing the pressure merely to obtain a smaller footing is not a defensible design choice.

Soft soils, organic material, uncontrolled fill, groundwater, expansive ground and a footing near a slope or another foundation can require site-specific assessment. The worksheet accepts a positive numerical value so that it can support different verified scenarios, but it cannot determine whether a presumptive value is permitted or whether deeper competent bearing material is necessary.

Settlement can govern even when average pressure is below a supplied bearing limit. An off-center load also changes pressure distribution; this calculator assumes a centered resultant and uniform average pressure. If the post is close to a pad edge, a retaining wall, the house foundation or a property constraint, obtain a design that accounts for the actual geometry and eccentricity.

Deck footing depth and the local frost line

The Frost Depth tab is a record of your local requirement, its source and your proposed concrete-bottom depth. It intentionally avoids assigning a construction depth from a state name. County, municipal, elevation and project conditions can vary, and a broad frost-depth map does not establish the adopted requirement for a particular footing.

The calculation is required concrete-bottom depth = max(entered local minimum, entered frost depth + entered allowance). The comparison indicates whether your proposed underside of concrete reaches that scenario. If an approved gravel or other base layer is placed beneath it, excavation goes deeper by that layer thickness. A deeper hole alone does not compensate for concrete terminating above the required bearing elevation.

American Wood Council DCA 6, based on the 2015 IRC, provides one prescriptive residential deck reference. Its footing guidance uses undisturbed bearing soil, a minimum depth and the locally specified frost line. Its scope and details must be checked as a whole; this worksheet does not reproduce its footing tables or claim that an area formula alone satisfies them. The adopted local code, amendments, site conditions and approved project documents govern.

Freestanding-deck exceptions, bearing on rock and approved alternative foundation systems can have different requirements. A frost-protected shallow foundation relies on a specific design and applicable conditions; it is not achieved by choosing an arbitrary shallow depth. Sloping grade also requires a clear datum for each footing. Record the applicable grade and depth separately where supports differ.

Concrete volume for a spread footing and pier

For a solid cylindrical section, volume is π × diameter² / 4 × height. For a square or rectangular solid section, use base area multiplied by height. Height includes concrete below grade plus its projection above grade. When dimensions are in inches, divide cubic inches by 1,728 for cubic feet; divide cubic feet by 27 for cubic yards.

For the spread-pad geometry, the tool adds pad area × pad thickness to round pier area × (below-grade depth + projection − pad thickness). The subtraction is essential: counting a full-height pier and then adding the whole pad would count the pier portion inside the pad twice. The tool requires the pier to fit inside the base and the pad to fit below grade.

Worked volume example: a 24 × 24 inch square pad, 8 inches thick, with a 12-inch round pier, concrete bottom 36 inches below grade and 6 inches above grade has 2.667 ft³ of pad concrete. The pier above the pad is 34 inches tall and contains approximately 2.225 ft³. Total concrete is approximately 4.892 ft³ per footing. Six identical footings need approximately 29.352 ft³ net; 10% waste raises that to approximately 32.287 ft³, or 1.196 yd³.

The excavation estimate uses a vertical envelope around the bearing-base footprint plus your entered clearance on every side. It is useful for a preliminary spoil or approved base-material quantity, but actual excavations may need battering, shoring, access or different shapes. It does not provide an excavation method or verify stability. Irregular holes and concrete overbreak also make field quantities larger than ideal geometric volume.

How many concrete bags do I need for deck footings?

Divide waste-adjusted concrete volume by the product's yield and round upward. Common planning yields for the referenced QUIKRETE Concrete Mix are approximately 0.30 ft³ for 40 lb, 0.375 ft³ for 50 lb, 0.45 ft³ for 60 lb, 0.60 ft³ for 80 lb and 0.675 ft³ for 90 lb. Other formulations and package sizes may differ. Use the custom yield control when the selected product label specifies another value.

A 12-inch-diameter cylinder with 36 inches of concrete and no projection contains approximately 2.356 ft³. At 0.60 ft³ per 80 lb bag, it needs 4 bags without waste, or 5 bags with 10% waste. Bag count depends on the actual volume and allowance, not the tube diameter alone.

Pooled versus separate batches: rounding after combining all footing volumes can buy fewer bags than rounding each footing separately. Use pooled rounding when the group is mixed and placed as one coordinated batch. Separate rounding is useful when each footing must have its own allocated bags. The report shows the chosen purchase total and an alternative bag-size comparison using the same batching assumption.

Waste allowance is an estimate, not a fixed standard. Account for uneven holes, placement losses and your supplier's practical order requirements. Follow the current product instructions for mixing, water, consolidation, curing and load-bearing applications. A fence-post method or short initial set time does not establish that a structural deck footing is ready to load.

Ready-mix quantity and deck footing material cost

The ready-mix order is waste-adjusted group volume converted to cubic yards, raised to any entered supplier minimum and rounded upward to the order increment. The cost comparison adds the quoted concrete price and your delivery or minimum-charge allowance. The bagged option uses whole purchased bags and bag price. Both alternatives then add the entered reinforcement quantity cost and other project costs.

Obtain local quotes for delivery, short loads, pumping, taxes, access, truck waiting and disposal. The calculator only includes charges you enter. Comparing a low ready-mix price per yard with retail bag price can be misleading if a small order has a large minimum charge. Conversely, a larger pour may make ready-mix attractive even when handling equipment adds cost.

If your deck uses several footing sizes, run each group separately. When combining groups into one delivery, add their waste-adjusted volumes first, then apply the supplier's increment and minimum once. Do not simply add separately rounded ready-mix orders unless you intend separate deliveries.

Rebar quantities without an invented cage design

Reinforcement requirements depend on the design, footing geometry, concrete strength, loads, anchorage and applicable provisions. A generic “four bars are adequate” statement cannot establish those requirements. The rebar controls here take a supplied schedule: number of straight pieces per footing, cut length including specified hooks or laps, available stock length, and any scheduled ties.

The stock calculation finds how many complete equal-length pieces fit into one stock bar and rounds the required stock-bar count upward. It reports purchased length and offcut. A cut longer than the stock length is rejected; the calculator does not assume you can create a structural splice from scraps. Cutting kerf, bend deductions, fabrication tolerances, multiple mixed cut lengths and reusable offcuts need a separate fabrication plan.

Tie cut lengths and completed-tie price are entered independently. The tool reports tie quantity and total tie cut length but does not design their shape or estimate tie stock-bar purchases. Selecting no bar schedule means that reinforcement has not been included in this takeoff; it does not mean reinforcement is unnecessary. Maintain the cover, placement, anchorage and inspection requirements specified for the actual foundation.

Using the result responsibly

A useful deck foundation worksheet preserves its assumptions. Before acting on a result, check the post's load path, the soil-value source, footing bearing elevation, pad thickness and connection details. Coordinate footing locations with utilities, drainage, property constraints and nearby foundations. Obtain the required utility-location service before excavation and follow your local inspection process before concrete placement.

Consult a qualified design professional for unusual loads, hot tubs, roofs, elevated or irregular decks, eccentric supports, uncertain soils, slopes, foundation interactions, uplift or any configuration outside an applicable prescriptive method. Keep the exported report with the corresponding approved drawings so that its quantities cannot be mistaken for a construction plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is a free deck footing calculator online?

It estimates centered bearing size, concrete and supplied reinforcement quantities, costs and entered depth comparisons. It is a planning worksheet, not an approved deck foundation design.

How do I calculate deck footing diameter?

Divide the compatible downward bearing load by allowable soil pressure to obtain area. Round diameter is the square root of four times that area divided by pi. Round upward and check all separate structural and local requirements.

How deep should deck footings be?

Use the adopted local requirements and approved project design. This worksheet compares the proposed concrete bottom with the larger of your entered minimum or frost depth plus an optional allowance. It cannot establish local requirements or exceptions.

Can a state frost-depth map determine my footing depth?

No. State averages do not establish a site-specific adopted requirement. Record the local building-department or design source and its specified depth in the Frost Depth tab.

What is tributary area?

It is the deck load area assigned to a particular support. Its relationship to reaction depends on framing, continuity, cantilevers and load distribution. Use an appropriate analysis for the actual post rather than dividing total deck area equally by post count.

Can I use a known post reaction instead of tributary area?

Yes. Enter a compatible service-level downward post reaction and a verified allowable bearing pressure. Add only loads that are not already included in that reaction or its bearing basis.

How many 80 lb bags for a 12-inch by 36-inch concrete footing?

A solid 12-inch-diameter cylinder with 36 inches of concrete and no projection contains about 2.356 cubic feet. At 0.60 cubic feet per bag, it requires 4 bags without waste or 5 bags with 10% waste.

How does a spread footing and pier volume calculation work?

Add the full pad volume to the pier volume above the pad top. Subtract pad thickness from the total concrete height used for the pier so the overlapping concrete is not counted twice.

What soil bearing capacity should I use?

Use a value authorized for the actual site and bearing stratum by the local authority or geotechnical/design documents. The calculator cannot identify soil, verify presumptive values or assess settlement.

Does the calculator design rebar for deck footings?

No. It estimates pieces, stock bars, offcuts, ties and cost from your supplied reinforcement schedule. Bar size, spacing, cover, hooks, laps and structural adequacy require a separate suitable design.

Does gravel below a footing count toward concrete depth?

No. An approved layer below concrete increases excavation depth. The depth to the underside of concrete remains a separate dimension and must satisfy the applicable bearing and frost requirements.

Can I calculate footings for a hot tub or roof?

You may enter an independently established compatible post reaction for a bearing estimate. The calculator does not determine special-load reactions or design the deck framing, foundation, uplift or connections for those features.
