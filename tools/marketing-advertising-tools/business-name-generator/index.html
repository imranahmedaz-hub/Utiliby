What this free online business name generator does

Choosing a name is one of the first and most lasting decisions for a new company, shop, blog or product. The right name is easy to say, easy to remember and easy to find, and it is available as a domain and a social handle. What makes a business name work

  • Easy to say. If people cannot say it after hearing it once, they cannot pass it on. A balanced mix of vowels and consonants helps.
  • Easy to spell. Avoid odd spellings, silent letters and doubled letters unless they add something.
  • Short. Names under ten letters are easier to type, remember and fit on a sign or an app icon.
  • Distinct. Look at your top competitors. A name that sounds like theirs causes confusion and legal risk.
  • Flexible. A name that is too narrow, such as Joe's Mobile Phone Cases, limits growth.
  • Available. You need a domain, social handles and a name you can register and trademark.
  • Positive in other languages. Check meaning and sound in the markets you will serve.

Domain names, handles and availability

A matching domain is the most practical test of a name. A .com remains the ending customers try first, but if it is taken you can add a short word such as get, try or the, choose another name, or use a good alternative such as .io for technology, .co as a shorter option, .app for apps or .shop for stores. Avoid hyphens and numbers, which are hard to say aloud. The generator shows each name as a domain and as a social handle in lowercase letters and digits, and the Check domain button opens the free ICANN lookup for that exact domain.

Social handles matter too. Try to use the same handle on each network you plan to use, and if the exact handle is not free, add a short word instead of random numbers. Trademarks, registration and legal checks

Having a free domain does not mean you can legally use a name. Trademark law protects names that are used in trade, and a similar name in the same field can lead to a dispute or a forced rebrand. Search the free databases of your national or regional trademark office, then search the web, app stores and social networks for similar names. Check the rules for registering a company, a trading name or a sole trader name where you operate, and be careful with restricted words such as Bank, Insurance or University. If the business matters, a short consultation with a trademark attorney is a small cost compared with a rebrand. This page does not give legal advice.

From shortlist to launch

Narrow your list to three to five names. Say each aloud, write it on paper and ask a few people to spell it after hearing it. Check the domain, handles and trademarks. Write a one-line description to go with the name, and test it on a few people from your target market. Then build the first brand assets: a short tagline such as the one you can draft in the Elevator Pitch Generator, a logo, a professional email signature and a QR code for your first print. Common naming mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a name only because the domain is free.
  • Names that are hard to spell, such as unusual letter swaps.
  • Names that are too close to a competitor.
  • Names that limit growth into new products or places.
  • Skipping the trademark search.
  • Not saying the name aloud, so that it is easy to mishear on the phone or on a podcast.
  • Waiting for the perfect name. A good, available, easy name that you start using is better than a perfect name you never launch.

Accuracy, limits and responsible use

The generator uses fixed word lists and patterns, so it will not invent a name with a deep meaning or recognise every unfortunate word in every language. The brandability score is a simple guide, not a prediction of commercial success, and the tool cannot confirm that a domain, handle or trademark is available. Frequently asked questions

How do I use this free business name generator?

Type one or more keywords, choose your industry and the styles you like, then press Generate names. You get scored ideas with matching domain and social handle formats. Star your favourites and download them as CSV.

Is this business name generator really free?

Yes. It is free to use online with no sign-up, no limits on how many times you generate, and no email wall before you see the results.

Does the tool check if a domain name is available?

No. A browser tool cannot check live domain registers. Each name has a Check domain button that opens the ICANN lookup so you can see registration details for the exact domain.

How do I choose a good business name?

A good name is short, easy to say and spell, memorable, distinct from competitors and available as a domain and social handle. Say it aloud, ask a few people to spell it after hearing it, and check for unfortunate meanings in other languages.

How long should a business name be?

Shorter names are easier to remember and type. Many strong brand names have two or three syllables and about 6 to 10 letters. The longest-name slider lets you cap the length.

What is a brandable business name?

A brandable name is an invented or altered word that is easy to say and not tied to one product, such as a blend of two words. They are often easier to trademark and register as a domain, but need more marketing to explain.

What is the difference between a descriptive name and a brandable name?

A descriptive name says what you do, such as Coffee Roast Hub, which helps search and clarity. A brandable name stands for the company itself, which is more flexible if you expand. Many brands use a descriptive tagline with a brandable name.

Should I choose a name that includes my keyword?

A keyword in the name can help people understand the business and may help local search a little, but it is not required and can limit growth. A short distinctive name with a clear tagline often works better.

What does the brandability score mean?

It is a simple guide, from about 20 to 98, based on length, how pronounceable the letters are and a few penalties for hyphens, numbers and awkward letter runs. It is not a measure of legal or commercial value.

Which domain ending should I pick?

A .com remains the most recognised ending. If it is taken, .io suits technology, .co is a short alternative, and .shop or .app are clear for stores and apps. Avoid endings that make the name hard to remember.

Can I use these names for my company?

The names are suggestions made from common words and patterns, so some may already be used. Search the trademark register in your country and the business register where you will operate before you commit.

How do I check if a business name is trademarked?

Search the free trademark databases of your national or regional office, such as the USPTO in the United States, the EUIPO in the European Union or the UKIPO in the United Kingdom, and consider asking an attorney before launch.

Why does the tool show a social media handle?

Matching handles help customers find you. The tool shows the name in lowercase letters and numbers only, which is the form most networks allow. Check each network because rules and availability differ.

Can I generate names in other languages?

The generator uses English word lists, but your keywords can be in any language that uses Latin letters, and the blend and invented-name patterns will work with them.

How many names can I generate?

You can generate 20, 40 or 60 names each time and as many times as you like. Each run shuffles the patterns, so press the button again for new ideas.

Can I save or print my list?

Yes. Star your favourites, then download a CSV, copy the list or print or save a PDF sheet. Only the names sheet is printed, not the whole page.

Are my keywords stored?

No. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is sent or stored.
