What to put in a professional email signature


  • Name and job title. Make the name the largest element.
  • Company. Add a department only if it helps routing.
  • One or two ways to contact you. A phone number and email address with real tel: and mailto: links let readers call and write with one tap.
  • Website. Link to a page you want visitors to see.
  • Social links. Include only profiles you keep up to date.
  • One call to action. A meeting link, a new product or an event beats five banners.
  • A short legal notice only if required.
Leave out quotes, long mottos, animated images and many colours. They slow loading, distract from the message and look unprofessional in a serious business mail. Studies of email design consistently favour short, scannable signatures, and the best ones are less than eight lines tall.


Email client compatibility: why signatures break


Email programs are not web browsers. Outlook for Windows uses the Word rendering engine, Gmail strips parts of the code and some phones remove images or change colours. Because of this, a good signature avoids modern layout features such as flexbox, grid, external stylesheets, scripts and web fonts. This generator uses nested tables, inline styles and absolute pixel sizes, which have the widest support. Images carry explicit width and height, and links use full https addresses so that they are clickable everywhere. The tool also drops unsafe link types such as javascript: and escapes all text to keep the code valid.
Images are the most common cause of trouble. Embedded images are often shown as attachments or removed, so host your photo and logo on your own website or an image host with a stable https address. Use PNG or JPG, keep files small, and write alt text so the signature still makes sense when images are blocked.


Design tips for a signature that gets read


  • Stay narrow. About 500 to 600 pixels wide and under 150 pixels tall.
  • One accent colour. Use your brand colour for the divider and links, and keep text dark grey. Check contrast with the Color Contrast Checker, and choose brand colours with the Color Picker.
  • Use readable text. At least 12 pixels, with enough space between lines.
  • Test dark mode. Avoid white boxes around logos.
  • Keep photos small. 80 to 100 pixels square is enough.
  • Make it work on phones. Short lines wrap better than wide banners.

Legal notices, privacy and accessibility


Rules differ by country and industry. In many places companies must show registered name, registration number and registered office on business emails, and regulated professions such as law, finance and health may need extra notices. Check with your employer or adviser. Confidentiality disclaimers rarely have legal force on their own, so keep them short. For accessibility, use sufficient contrast, real text rather than text inside images, meaningful alt text and descriptive link text.

Troubleshooting common signature problems


  • Logo missing. The image is blocked or not hosted. Use an https address.
  • Layout broken in Outlook. Use Copy HTML and paste into the signature editor's HTML mode, or choose the Classic layout.
  • Fonts changed. Choose a web-safe font and set the same font in your compose settings.
  • Signature too long. Remove the banner or the address.
  • Links not clickable. Include https:// in every link.
  • Colours look wrong in dark mode. Use a darker accent that still works on dark backgrounds.

Open Settings, choose See all settings, scroll to Signature, create a new signature, paste the copied signature into the box and save. The How to install tab lists the steps for Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail, Thunderbird and Yahoo.
How do I add a signature in Outlook?
In new Outlook and Outlook on the web go to Settings, Accounts, Signatures. In classic Outlook use File, Options, Mail, Signatures. Create a new signature and paste. If the layout breaks in classic Outlook, use the Copy HTML option.
Why does my photo or logo not show in the email?
Pasted images are usually stored on your computer or embedded in the message, and many programs remove them. Host the image on your website or an image service and enter its https web address so every recipient can load it.
Can I add a logo or banner to the signature?
Yes. Enter the web address of the logo as the photo, and a banner address in the Design tab. Keep banners about 800 pixels wide, because the tool shows them at 400 pixels for sharp screens.
What size should an email signature be?
Keep it about 500 to 600 pixels wide and under 150 pixels tall, with a photo of 80 to 100 pixels and text of 12 to 14 pixels. Smaller is better on phones.
Which fonts are safe in email signatures?
Web-safe fonts such as Arial, Verdana, Tahoma, Trebuchet MS, Georgia and Times New Roman, because custom fonts are not loaded by most email programs. The tool offers only fonts that display reliably.
Does the signature work in dark mode?
The preview lets you check both light and dark backgrounds. Use dark text on a transparent background logo and avoid white backgrounds on images, so the signature stays readable when a program inverts colours.
Is the signature mobile friendly?
Yes. The layouts are built from simple tables that scale down, and the Phone preview shows how the signature looks on a narrow screen. Prefer the Compact or Minimal layouts if most readers use phones.

Should I add a disclaimer or legal notice?
Add one only if your employer, industry or country requires it, such as a confidentiality notice or company registration details. Keep it short, because long notices hide your contact details.
