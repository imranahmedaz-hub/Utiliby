What this free online voice recorder does

An online voice recorder lets you capture sound from a microphone without installing an app. People use one to record a voice memo, an audition, a voice-over, an interview, a lecture, a message to send, or a quick test of a new microphone. Many web recorders save your audio to their servers or hide the download behind a sign-up. This free tool keeps the recording on your own device. It captures sound from your microphone in the browser, shows a live level meter, lets you trim the result, and saves it as MP3 or WAV.

It also works as a microphone test: turn the mic on, speak, and watch the meter without recording anything. There is no account, no watermark and no cloud copy.

How to record your voice in four steps

  1. Check the microphone. Choose your microphone from the list, click Test microphone and speak. The bar should rise to about two thirds of its width on normal speech.
  2. Record. Click Start recording and allow microphone access when the browser asks. Use Pause if you need a break.
  3. Stop and trim. Click Stop. Drag on the waveform to choose the part you want, or type start and end times.
  4. Save. Pick MP3 or WAV, and click Save recording.

Setting up for the best sound

  • Choose a quiet room. Close windows, switch off fans and put your phone on silent. Soft furnishings such as curtains and sofas cut echo.
  • Keep the microphone close. About a hand's width, or four to six inches, from your mouth is a good start for most microphones. Closer makes the voice fuller but exaggerates breath sounds.
  • Reduce plosives. Speak slightly across the microphone rather than straight into it, and use a pop filter if you have one.
  • Use headphones. They stop speaker sound from feeding back into the microphone.
  • Watch the meter. Peaks around 60 to 80 percent leave room before distortion.

Echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain

These three switches ask the browser to process your microphone signal before it reaches the page. Echo cancellation removes sound that comes out of your speakers and back in through the microphone, which matters if you record without headphones. Noise suppression lowers steady noise such as computer fans. Auto gain raises or lowers the volume to keep it even.

They are designed for calls, and they can make recordings sound a little processed, with a pumping volume or a thin tone. For speech in a noisy room they help. For a podcast, an instrument or a singer in a quiet room, switch them off and control the level yourself with Input gain. The settings take effect when the microphone opens, so change them before you click Start or Test.

Understanding levels and clipping

Digital audio has a hard ceiling. If the signal goes above it, the tops of the waves are cut flat, which is called clipping and sounds harsh. The level meter shows the peak of your voice. A healthy recording peaks between about minus 12 and minus 3 dB. After you stop, the summary shows the peak in decibels and warns you if it clipped or if the signal is very quiet.

If the recording is quiet but not clipped, tick Normalize, which raises the peak to minus 1 dB. If it clipped, normalizing cannot repair it. Lower Input gain or move back and record again.

MP3 or WAV?

FormatSize per minuteBest for
MP3 128 kbpsabout 1 MBVoice memos, sharing, uploads
MP3 192 kbpsabout 1.4 MBHigher quality speech and music
WAV 16-bit monoabout 5 MB at 44.1 kHzEditing, mastering, archiving

MP3 is compressed and small, which suits messages and uploads. WAV is uncompressed and is the better choice if you plan to edit the recording in an audio program, since you avoid compressing it twice.

Troubleshooting

  • The browser does not ask for permission: it may have been blocked earlier. Click the lock icon in the address bar and allow the microphone.
  • The meter does not move: choose another microphone from the list, check the system input volume and that the microphone is not muted.
  • The sound has an echo: use headphones, or turn on Echo cancellation.
  • The sound is thin or watery: turn off Noise suppression and Auto gain.
  • The page stops recording on a phone: keep the screen on and the browser in the foreground.
  • Save as MP3 fails: the encoder could not be downloaded. Choose WAV or check your connection.

Privacy and how it works

When you click Start, the page asks the browser for access to your microphone and receives a stream of audio samples. It keeps those samples in memory, draws the level meter from them, and encodes them locally when you save. Nothing is sent to Utiliby or to any other server. Stopping the recording turns the microphone off, and the browser's microphone indicator disappears. The MP3 encoder library loads from a public CDN the first time you save as MP3, but your audio is not part of that request.

Recording other people

Recording yourself is simple. Recording other people is regulated. In some places only one party in a conversation needs to know it is being recorded, and in others all parties must agree. Rules also differ for public speeches, workplaces and calls with people in other countries. Ask for consent, say clearly that you are recording, and check local law before you publish or share a recording of someone else.

Frequently asked questions

How do I record my voice online for free?

Click Start recording and allow microphone access when your browser asks. Speak, click Stop, trim the recording if you like, and save it as MP3 or WAV. Everything happens in your browser, with no account and no upload.

Is my recording uploaded or stored anywhere?

No. The audio is captured and kept in your browser's memory, then saved to your device when you choose. It is not sent to any server. If you close the tab before saving, the recording is gone.

Why does my browser ask for microphone permission?

Browsers always ask before a web page can use your microphone. You can allow it for this visit or always, and you can remove the permission at any time from the lock icon in the address bar.

The tool says no microphone was found. What should I do?

Check that a microphone is plugged in and enabled in your system sound settings, then choose it from the Microphone list. Close any other app that might be using it, and click Start recording again.

What does the microphone test do?

Test microphone turns the mic on and shows the level meter without saving anything, so you can check your setup. Aim for a meter that reaches about two thirds of the bar when you speak normally.

What do echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain do?

Echo cancellation reduces sound from your speakers that your mic picks up. Noise suppression lowers steady background noise such as fans. Auto gain adjusts volume automatically. These are done by your browser. For music or the most natural voice, turn them off.

How long can I record?

Up to one hour per recording. Recordings are held in memory, and one hour of mono audio uses roughly 640 MB, so close other tabs for long recordings.

Can I pause and resume?

Yes. Pause stops adding audio without ending the session, and Resume continues. The timer counts only the time that is actually recorded.

What formats can I save?

MP3 for small files that play everywhere, and WAV for uncompressed 16-bit audio that is best for editing. Recordings are mono, which is normal for a single microphone.

Why is my recording too quiet or distorted?

Too quiet: move closer, raise Input gain, or tick Normalize before you save. Distorted: lower Input gain or move back, because the signal is clipping. The recording summary warns you when the peak is too low or too high.

Can I trim the start and end?

Yes. Drag on the waveform to select the part you want, or type exact start and end times. Only the selected part is saved.

Can it record the sound of my computer or a video call?

No. This tool records microphone input only. Capturing the audio that plays on your computer needs screen or system audio capture, which browsers handle differently.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes, in current Safari, Chrome and Firefox. Allow the microphone when asked, and keep the screen on and the page open while recording, because mobile browsers can pause pages that go to the background.

Can I use it for podcasts, voice-overs or interviews?

Yes, for quick recording. Use a headset or external microphone in a quiet room, turn off echo cancellation if you wear headphones, and save as WAV if you will edit the audio afterwards.

Does it work offline?

After the page has loaded, recording and saving as WAV work without an internet connection. Saving as MP3 needs a one-time download of the open-source encoder.

Is it legal to record someone's voice?

Laws on recording conversations differ by country and region, and many require the consent of everyone involved. Always tell people they are being recorded and follow the rules where you live.

What can I print or save from the page?

Print / save summary prints the recording details and the waveform, and Copy summary copies them as text. The audio file is the main download.
