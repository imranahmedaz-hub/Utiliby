What this free online color picker does

A color picker turns the vague idea of “a blue like that” into an exact code you can paste into a stylesheet, a design file or a slide. This free online color picker combines a classic saturation and brightness square, a hue slider and an opacity slider with a screen eyedropper, so you can choose a colour by eye, by number or by sampling something that is already on your screen.

Every change is shown immediately as HEX, RGB, HSL, HSV, HWB, CMYK, CIE Lab, LCH, OKLab and OKLCH values with one-click copy buttons, together with the nearest CSS colour name. Extra tabs show matching colour harmonies, a set of tints and shades, and a built-in WCAG contrast checker, so one visit answers “what is the code?”, “what goes with it?” and “is it readable?”. Nothing is uploaded, and there is nothing to install.

How to use the HEX color picker

  1. Choose a hue. Drag the rainbow slider to the base colour you want, such as blue or orange.
  2. Set saturation and brightness. Drag the dot in the square. Left is washed out, right is vivid, top is bright and bottom is dark. You can also use the arrow keys when the square is focused.
  3. Fine tune with numbers. Type exact RGB or HSL values, or paste any colour code into the main box.
  4. Add transparency. Move the opacity slider to get rgba(), hsla() and eight-digit HEX values.
  5. Copy the code. Press Copy beside the format you need.
  6. Explore. Open Harmonies for matching colours and Tints, shades and contrast for a scale and an accessibility check.

To match a colour you can already see, use Pick from screen and click anywhere. The picker then shows its code and everything else about it.

How the picker works: hue, saturation and brightness

The square uses the HSV model. Hue is the position on the colour wheel, measured as an angle from 0 to 360 degrees, with red at 0, green at 120 and blue at 240. Saturation is how far the colour is from grey, and value or brightness is how far it is from black. The slider sets the hue, and the square fills in every saturation and brightness for that hue, which is why it is so quick to find a lighter, darker, duller or more vivid version of the same colour.

HSL looks similar but treats lightness differently: 0 percent is black, 100 percent is white, and the purest colour sits at 50 percent. Designers often prefer HSL for building systems of colours because changing only the lightness gives a clean tint or shade. Both models are just different coordinates for the same screen colours, and the tool converts between them for you.

Colour harmony: complementary, analogous, triadic and more

Colour harmonies are combinations that tend to look balanced because of where they sit on the wheel. The Harmonies tab calculates them from your colour by rotating the hue and keeping saturation and lightness.

  • Complementary: two opposite colours, strong contrast, good for a call to action.
  • Split-complementary: a colour and the two neighbours of its opposite, with more variety and less tension.
  • Analogous: three neighbouring colours, calm and cohesive.
  • Triadic: three colours spaced evenly, lively and balanced when one leads.
  • Tetradic and square: four colours that give a rich palette, best with one dominant colour.
  • Monochromatic: one hue in several lightness levels, safe and elegant.

A reliable rule is the 60-30-10 split: a dominant colour for about 60 percent of the design, a secondary for 30 percent and an accent for 10 percent. To go from one colour to a whole set, try the Color Palette Generator or the Color Wheel.

Contrast and accessibility

A colour is only useful if people can read what sits on it. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) measure this with a contrast ratio from 1:1 (no difference) to 21:1 (black on white). Level AA asks for at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and for user-interface parts such as button borders and icons. Level AAA asks for 7:1 and 4.5:1.

The Tints, shades and contrast tab shows the ratio between your colour and any background and marks each requirement as pass or fail. The tool also tells you whether black or white text reads better on your colour. For a deeper audit of pairs, use the free Color Contrast Checker. Do not rely on colour alone to carry meaning, because many people have colour vision differences. Pair colour with text, shape or position, and try the Color Blind Test to understand how others see.

Choosing colour codes for websites and CSS

All modern CSS accepts these notations, and the picker shows each one ready to paste:

  • color: #0369a1; is compact and universal.
  • color: rgb(3 105 161 / 80%); adds transparency with the modern space-separated syntax.
  • color: hsl(201 96% 32%); lets you create hover states by changing lightness.
  • color: oklch(50% 0.12 243); keeps lightness consistent across hues, which helps accessible theming.

Store brand colours as CSS custom properties, such as --brand: #0369a1;, so you change them in one place. The Download CSS file button writes the current colour, your chosen harmony, tints, shades and saved colours as a ready-made :root block. Use the nearest CSS name only when it is close, as names such as steelblue are convenient but rarely match a brand exactly.

Screen colour versus print colour

Screens mix light (RGB) and printers mix ink (CMYK), and the range of colours each can show is different. Bright blues, greens and oranges on a monitor often look duller on paper. The CMYK value shown here is a straightforward mathematical conversion, which is a good starting point but not what a press will produce. For packaging, brand guides or large print jobs, ask the printer for their colour profile and a printed proof, and consider spot colours when exact matching matters.

The screen eyedropper and your privacy

The Pick from screen button uses the browser EyeDropper API, which opens a magnifier and lets you click any pixel in any window on a computer. The browser handles the sampling and gives the page only the colour you chose, not a picture of your screen. It is available in Chromium-based browsers such as Chrome, Edge and Opera. If your browser lacks it, the button is disabled and the rest of the tool works as normal. For photos, the Image Color Picker is more precise because you can zoom and sample many points.

Common colour mistakes and how to avoid them

  • Pure black on pure white everywhere. It has maximum contrast but can feel harsh. A very dark grey on off-white often reads better.
  • Light grey text. It looks elegant and fails contrast checks. Test it.
  • Too many saturated colours. Pick one main colour and use neutrals for the rest.
  • Ignoring dark mode. A colour that works on white may not work on black, so check both.
  • Trusting one screen. Preview on a phone and a laptop before you finalise.
  • Mixing formats by hand. Copy codes from the picker instead of retyping them.

To pick a colour from a photo or screenshot instead, use the Image Color Picker, and to blend two chosen colours use the Color Mixer.

Accuracy, limits and responsible use

Calculations use standard published formulas and run in your browser. Values are rounded for display. Colours outside the sRGB range are not offered, so OKLCH and Lab values come from sRGB colours. Colour perception varies between people and displays, and the contrast ratio is a guide rather than a certificate of compliance. Treat this free online tool as an aid for design decisions, and confirm anything legally or commercially important with your own testing.

Frequently asked questions

What is a color picker?

A color picker is a tool that lets you choose a colour visually and gives you its code, such as HEX, RGB or HSL, so you can use the exact same colour in a website, app, document or design.

How do I get the HEX code of a colour?

Drag in the colour square and move the hue slider until you like the colour. The HEX code appears in the box and in the list of values, and the Copy button puts it on your clipboard.

How do I convert HEX to RGB with this tool?

Paste the HEX code into the colour box. The RGB fields and the rgb() value update at once. For example #0369A1 becomes rgb(3, 105, 161).

What is the difference between HEX, RGB and HSL?

They describe the same sRGB colour in different ways. HEX and RGB give the amount of red, green and blue. HSL gives hue, saturation and lightness, which is easier for people to adjust by hand.

How do I pick a colour from anywhere on my screen?

Press Pick from screen, then click any pixel in any window. This uses the browser EyeDropper feature, which works in Chrome, Edge and Opera on a computer. In other browsers use the picker or the Image Color Picker tool.

Can I pick a colour from a photo?

Yes. Use the free Image Color Picker on this site to click a pixel in an uploaded photo, or paste the code you find here into the colour box.

What are HSV and HSB, and how do they differ from HSL?

HSV and HSB are the same model: hue, saturation and value or brightness. The square in this picker works in HSV. HSL puts pure white at 100 percent lightness, while HSV puts the brightest, most saturated colour at 100 percent value.

How do I make a colour transparent?

Move the Opacity slider. The tool then shows rgba(), hsla() and an eight-digit HEX code such as #0369A180, where the last two digits are the opacity.

What is OKLCH and why is it shown?

OKLCH is a modern CSS colour format based on how people see lightness, chroma and hue. It makes colours easier to adjust evenly. Current versions of major browsers support the oklch() function.

Why does my CMYK colour look different when printed?

CMYK here is a simple mathematical conversion. A real printer uses ink, paper and a colour profile, so the result changes. For print work, ask your printer for a proof or use their profile.

How do I find complementary colours?

Open the Harmonies tab. The complementary colour sits opposite your colour on the wheel, 180 degrees away. The same tab shows split-complementary, analogous, triadic, tetradic, square and monochromatic schemes.

How do I check whether text is readable on my colour?

Open the Tints, shades and contrast tab, enter the background colour and read the contrast ratio. WCAG asks for at least 4.5 to 1 for normal text and 3 to 1 for large text.

What does the nearest colour name mean?

The tool compares your colour with the 148 CSS colour names and shows the closest one by perceptual distance. If your colour is not an exact match it says closest.

Are my saved colours stored?

Saved colours stay on the page while it is open and disappear when you close or reload it. Use Download CSS file to keep them.

Why does the same colour look different on another screen?

Screens differ in brightness, colour gamut and calibration, and ambient light changes what you see. The code is the same, but the display of it varies.

Can I type a colour name or a function like rgb() into the box?

Yes. The box accepts HEX with 3, 4, 6 or 8 digits, rgb(), rgba(), hsl(), hsla(), hwb(), cmyk(), lab(), lch(), oklab(), oklch(), CSS names such as steelblue, and plain numbers like 255, 128, 0.

Is this colour picker free and private?

Yes. It is a free online tool with no sign-up and no limits. Everything runs in your browser and no colour or image is sent to a server.
