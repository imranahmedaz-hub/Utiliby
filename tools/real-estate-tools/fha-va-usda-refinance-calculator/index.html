What this free online streamline refinance calculator does

If you have a loan backed by the Federal Housing Administration, the Department of Veterans Affairs or the US Department of Agriculture, you can often refinance with a streamline program that asks for less than a standard refinance. The paperwork is lighter, many lenders do not require an appraisal, and the loan only needs to give you a clear benefit. That benefit has to be real, so these programs come with tests, and they come with their own fees and insurance.

This free online FHA, VA and USDA streamline refinance calculator puts it together. It adds the program's upfront fee and annual mortgage insurance or fee to the payment, works out your true saving and break-even, and checks the commonly applied net tangible benefit tests. A rate what-if tab shows how the result changes with the rate you are offered, and a third tab compares refinancing an FHA loan into a conventional loan, where removing mortgage insurance can matter more than the rate. Everything runs in your browser, so your numbers stay private.

How the calculator works

The tool starts from the same refinance maths as a conventional loan: the payment on the new balance, the closing costs and fees and the month your monthly savings repay them. It then adds the annual mortgage insurance or fee, divided by twelve, to the payment of both loans, so the saving reflects what you actually pay. If you roll the fees into the loan, the loan is grown so that the percentage costs are a share of the final loan. The break-even is the first month in which the total saved reaches the costs. When the costs are rolled in, the savings must also cover the extra interest on the larger balance.

Worked example. An FHA borrower owes 250,000 at 7.0% with 27 years left and pays 0.55% a year in mortgage insurance. A new 30-year loan at 6.0% with a 1.75% upfront premium and 1.0% other costs is rolled into the balance. The new loan is about 257,000, the new principal and interest is about 1,541 against about 1,720 now, and with insurance the monthly total falls by about 175. The combined rate falls by a full point. The 7,069 of costs are repaid in about 44 months, a little under four years, because they were added to the loan and the savings have to cover that extra balance.

Net tangible benefit tests

A streamline refinance must leave you better off, so each program has a test. The tool shows the commonly applied ones as a checklist: a minimum reduction in the rate or combined rate, a lower payment, a minimum monthly payment drop for USDA and recouping fees within a set time for VA. These tests change, can differ by lender and by whether the loan is fixed or adjustable, so treat a pass as a good sign and a fail as a reason to ask your lender, not as a final answer.

Mortgage insurance and why it matters

Government-backed loans replace a large down payment with insurance or a fee. FHA loans carry an upfront premium and an annual premium, and on many loans the annual premium lasts for the life of the loan. USDA loans carry an upfront and an annual fee. VA loans have a funding fee and no monthly insurance. Because the annual charge is a percentage of the balance, it is a large part of the payment and a lower rate does not always lower it much. That is why the calculator includes it, and why the FHA to conventional tab can show a bigger saving than a streamline.

FHA to conventional: when it can win

If your home has gained value or you have paid the loan down, your loan-to-value may now be 80% or lower. A conventional loan at that level generally needs no private mortgage insurance, so refinancing from FHA can end the insurance for good. The conventional route needs a standard application and usually an appraisal and higher closing costs, and the rate may be higher or lower than the FHA streamline. The FHA to conventional tab compares the two with your home value, rate, costs and the PMI you would pay if your loan-to-value is above 80%.

Tips for a streamline refinance

  • Ask your current servicer and at least two other lenders for quotes. Streamline rates and fees differ.
  • Ask for the net tangible benefit test in writing.
  • Check any seasoning period, so you do not apply too early.
  • Compare rolling in fees with paying them, using the break-even.
  • Do not skip payments before closing. Streamlines usually need a clean recent payment history.
  • Be careful with offers that promise "no cost". Check the rate and the balance.

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring the annual insurance. It changes the saving and the test.
  • Using the typical fee figures instead of your quote.
  • Expecting cash out.
  • Extending the term and assuming the lower payment is all savings.
  • Refinancing again too soon, before the break-even.

Frequently asked questions

What is a streamline refinance?

A streamline refinance is a simplified refinance of an existing government-backed loan: FHA, VA or USDA. It usually needs less paperwork than a standard refinance, often no appraisal, and it must give you a clear benefit such as a lower payment or rate.

What is the FHA streamline refinance?

It lets a borrower with an FHA loan refinance into a new FHA loan with limited documentation. It is meant to lower your payment, and it generally cannot be used to take cash out.

What is a VA IRRRL?

The Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan, often called a VA streamline, lets a borrower with a VA loan refinance into a lower rate with limited paperwork. It carries a funding fee that is lower than on a purchase loan.

What is the USDA streamlined-assist refinance?

It is a refinance for borrowers with a USDA Single Family Housing Guaranteed loan. It usually needs a lower payment to qualify, and it does not require an appraisal in many cases.

What is the net tangible benefit?

It is a requirement that the refinance must clearly benefit the borrower. The tests differ by program, for example a minimum rate reduction, a lower payment, or recouping fees within a set number of months. The tool shows the tests that are commonly applied.

What is the typical net tangible benefit test for VA IRRRL?

Commonly cited tests are a rate at least half a point lower for a fixed-to-fixed refinance and recouping the fees through lower payments within about three years. Confirm the current rules with a VA lender.

What is the FHA streamline benefit test?

It is commonly applied as a reduction in the combined rate, meaning the interest rate plus the annual mortgage insurance, of at least half a point for a fixed-to-fixed refinance. Check the current HUD guidance with your lender.

What is the USDA streamlined test?

The new payment is commonly required to be at least 50 a month lower than the current one. The tool checks this using principal, interest and the annual fee.

What are the upfront fees?

FHA charges an upfront mortgage insurance premium, VA charges a funding fee and USDA charges an upfront guarantee fee. The defaults here are typical illustrations. Replace them with the amounts on your Loan Estimate.

Can I take cash out in a streamline refinance?

Generally no. Streamline programs are designed for rate and payment reductions, and any cash back is limited to a small amount. For cash, see a cash-out refinance.

Can I roll the fees into the loan?

Often yes for the upfront program fee, and sometimes for other costs, which keeps cash at closing low. Rolling in raises the balance, so choose Roll into the new loan to see the effect.

How soon can I use a streamline?

Programs usually require that your current loan is a certain age and that you have made a number of on-time payments. Rules differ by program and lender, so ask your lender.

Why does the tool include mortgage insurance?

FHA and USDA loans carry annual mortgage insurance or fees that change the true payment. The tool adds the annual rate to each monthly payment so the saving is realistic. It uses a flat amount on the starting balance, which is a simplification.

What does the FHA to conventional tab do?

It compares refinancing out of an FHA loan into a conventional loan. If your loan-to-value is 80% or less, conventional mortgage insurance may not apply, which can remove FHA insurance for good. You enter the conventional rate, costs and PMI.

Can I remove FHA mortgage insurance?

On many FHA loans the insurance lasts for the life of the loan, depending on the original loan-to-value and date. Refinancing into a conventional loan with enough equity is one way to end it.

Is my information private?

Yes. The calculator runs in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server, saved or shared, and reloading the page resets it.
