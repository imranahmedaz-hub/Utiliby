What this free bionic reading converter does

This free online bionic reading converter turns ordinary text into text where the first letters of each word are bold. Paste an article, an email, a school reading or a long document, and you instantly get a version that gives your eyes a series of bold anchor points to follow. You can choose how much of each word is bold, how often to bold words, and the font, size, spacing, line width and colours, so you can build a layout that suits you.

Does bionic reading work? What the evidence says

The idea behind bionic reading is that the eye does not need to read every letter of every word. Skilled readers recognise words from their shape and first letters, so bold fixation points may help the eye jump through the text. Supporters report that it makes dense text feel lighter and easier to start. Some people with attention difficulties say it helps them keep their place.

The research so far is limited and not encouraging for broad claims. Small experiments that compared plain text with bionic-style text found no clear improvement in speed or comprehension for the average reader, and some readers were slower. That does not mean it is useless for you. Reading comfort is personal, and a formatting change that you like and that keeps you reading is worth having. Treat bionic formatting as one option in a toolbox of reading aids, not as a cure or a guaranteed speed boost.

Settings explained

  • Fixation (bold share). The share of each word that is bold, from 10 to 90%. A one-letter word is bold completely; a ten-letter word at 50% has five bold letters.
  • Bold every N words. A larger number gives more plain words between bold anchors, which some people call a faster rhythm.
  • Short words. Skip one-letter or one-to-two-letter words such as a, I, of and to if the bold on tiny words feels busy.
  • Font and size. The dyslexia-friendly option uses OpenDyslexic or Lexend when installed, then a clear fallback. Verdana and Tahoma are wide and open, and are often recommended.
  • Line height and spacing. A line height of 1.5 to 2 and a small increase in letter or word spacing can help. Do not overdo it.
  • Line width. About 60 to 70 characters per line is common advice.
  • Theme. Light, cream paper, soft blue, dark and high contrast yellow on black.

The reading ruler and line focus

Line focus tools highlight the line you are reading and soften the rest. Many readers use a card or a finger on paper; the reading ruler does the same on screen. Tick the option, then move your mouse or drag your finger over the text. The band is exactly one line high at your chosen size and line spacing. It is useful for dense text, tables of data and for readers who lose their place when lines wrap.

Speed reading with the word-by-word reader

The Speed reader tab uses rapid serial visual presentation: one word (or a small group) appears at a time in the same place, so your eyes do not move across the line. The red letter marks the optimal recognition point, roughly one third of the way into a word, where readers are fastest at recognising it. Pauses are added after commas and sentence endings.

Readability scores explained

The Readability tab calculates the Flesch Reading Ease, Flesch-Kincaid grade level, average sentence length, long word share and reading time. The Flesch Reading Ease formula is 206.835 − 1.015 × (words per sentence) − 84.6 × (syllables per word). A score of 60 to 70 is considered plain English that most adults can follow easily; scores under 30 are very difficult.

The syllable count is a quick English heuristic and can be off by a few percent. Use the scores to compare drafts, not as exact values. Shorter sentences and everyday words help every reader more than any font trick.

Tips for more comfortable reading

  • Compare the same paragraph in plain and bionic form for a minute each and see which feels easier.
  • Use a lower fixation (30 to 40%) if the bold is distracting.
  • Increase line height before you increase letter spacing.
  • Take breaks and use the dark or cream theme in the evening.
  • Break long paragraphs into shorter ones before converting.
  • Keep links plain so they are easy to spot and click.

Frequently asked questions

What is bionic reading?

Bionic reading is a text formatting style that makes the first letters of each word bold, so the eyes land on the bold part and the brain completes the rest of the word. It is meant to guide the eye and keep attention moving through the text.

How do I convert text to bionic reading?

Paste your text into the box. The converted text appears straight away with the first part of every word in bold. Use the fixation slider to bold more or fewer letters, and the other controls to change font, size, spacing and theme.

Does bionic reading actually make you read faster?

The evidence is mixed. Small studies and tests published so far have not found a reliable improvement in reading speed or comprehension for most readers. Some people, including some with ADHD or dyslexia, say it helps them stay focused. Try it and compare, because it is a personal preference.

Is bionic reading good for dyslexia or ADHD?

There is no proof that it treats either condition. Better-supported adjustments for dyslexia include larger text, wider spacing, shorter lines and a clear font, and this tool provides all of those. Use whatever feels comfortable, and talk to a specialist about support.

What does the fixation slider do?

It sets what share of each word is bold. At 50% a six-letter word has three bold letters. Lower values give a subtler look, and higher values make most of the word bold.

What is the Bold every setting?

It lets you bold only every second or third word, which gives your eyes a skipping rhythm, often called the saccade setting. Every word is the standard look.

What are the best settings?

Many people start with 40 to 50% fixation, bold on every word, 18 px type, line height 1.6 to 1.8, a line width around 60 to 70 characters and a sans-serif font. Then adjust one setting at a time.

Can I copy the result into Word, Docs or an email?

Use Copy HTML and paste into an editor that accepts HTML, or Copy Markdown for tools that support double-asterisk bold. You can also download a ready-made HTML page and open it in any browser.

What does the reading ruler do?

It highlights a band the height of one line that follows your mouse or finger, so you can keep your place and block out the lines above and below. Many readers find a line focus helpful.

What is the speed reader tab?

It shows words one at a time (or two or three at a time) at a speed you choose, with a red letter at the optimal recognition point. This method is called rapid serial visual presentation. It is useful for skimming, but comprehension usually drops at high speeds.

What is the Flesch Reading Ease score?

It estimates how easy English text is to read from the average sentence length and the average number of syllables per word. Scores run from 0 (very hard) to 100 (very easy); 60 to 70 is plain English. The tool also shows the Flesch-Kincaid grade level.

Does it work in other languages?

Yes for bolding: it works on any language that uses letters, including accented letters. The readability scores and syllable counts are designed for English and will be less accurate for other languages.

Can I download the converted text?

Yes. Download HTML page saves a self-contained page with your font, size, spacing and theme. You can also copy HTML or Markdown, or print the converted text to a PDF.

Why does my text look different when pasted elsewhere?

Plain text has no bold. The bold marks are HTML or Markdown, so paste into a place that understands one of them. If you paste plain text into a basic text field, you will lose the formatting.
