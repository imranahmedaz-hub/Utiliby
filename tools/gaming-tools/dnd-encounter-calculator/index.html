What a D&D encounter difficulty calculator does

Building a fair fight is one of the hardest parts of running a game. A D&D 5e encounter difficulty calculator turns the guidelines into a quick check: add your players and the monsters you want to use, and it tells you whether the fight is easy, medium, hard or deadly, how much XP each player earns and how many monsters of a given challenge rating the party can handle.

This free online tool follows both the 2014 encounter-building rules (XP thresholds per character, challenge rating XP and the monster-number multiplier) and the 2024 XP budget approach. It supports parties of mixed levels, up to six groups of monsters, a table of how many monsters of each CR reach each difficulty and reference tables for thresholds and CR to XP. It runs in your browser and nothing is stored.

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose the rules. 2014 uses Easy to Deadly with a multiplier. 2024 uses Low, Moderate and High budgets.
  2. Enter your party. Players and level. Use extra groups for characters at different levels.
  3. Add monsters. Pick each group's challenge rating and number. Names are optional and appear in your report.
  4. Read the difficulty. See the monster XP, adjusted XP, thresholds and the XP each player earns.
  5. Open How many monsters to see counts for any CR, or Tables for thresholds and CR to XP.

The 2014 method, step by step

  1. Add the XP thresholds of all characters for each difficulty.
  2. Add up the XP of all monsters.
  3. Multiply by the number-of-monsters multiplier (shifted up for parties under three, down for six or more).
  4. Compare the adjusted XP with the party thresholds. The highest band reached is the difficulty.

Worked example. Four level 3 characters have thresholds of 300 Easy, 600 Medium, 900 Hard and 1,600 Deadly. Four CR 1/4 monsters (50 XP each) and one CR 1 monster (200 XP) total 400 XP. Five monsters use a ×2 multiplier, so the adjusted XP is 800: a Medium encounter, because it is above 600 and below 900. Each player earns 100 XP. Replace the CR 1 monster with a second one and the adjusted XP rises to 1,200, which is Hard.

The 2024 XP budget method

The 2024 rules simplify the process. Each character has an XP budget for Low, Moderate and High difficulty. You add the budgets of your players and compare the total XP of the monsters with them. There is no multiplier, so budgets are larger than the 2014 thresholds. A level 3 character has budgets of 150, 225 and 400. For four players that is 600, 900 and 1,600. The same 400 XP encounter from the example above is below the Low budget, so it rates as trivial under the 2024 numbers, and you would need to add monsters to make it a real challenge.

The calculator shows both so that you can see the difference and pick the method your table uses. Check the figures against your own book.

Challenge rating and XP

Challenge rating (CR) estimates how strong a monster is for a party of four characters of a similar level. Each CR has an XP value, from 10 XP for CR 0 and 25 for CR 1/8 up to 155,000 for CR 30. The values rise steeply: a CR 5 monster is worth 1,800 XP and a CR 10 monster 5,900 XP. As a rough guide, a single monster whose CR equals the party's level is usually a Medium fight for four characters, while a CR well above the party's level can be Deadly on its own: a CR 5 monster is Deadly for four level 3 characters.

Tuning the difficulty beyond the numbers

  • Action economy. More creatures take more actions. A boss with minions is safer for the players than a boss alone.
  • Terrain and cover. Choke points, hazards and ranged attackers change the balance quickly.
  • Resources. Fights later in the adventuring day are harder because spell slots and hit points are lower.
  • Party strength. Optimised parties and magic items push difficulty down; inexperienced parties push it up.
  • Number of encounters. The guidelines assume several encounters per day.

Tips for building better encounters

  • Start with the difficulty you want and use the How many monsters tab to choose counts.
  • Mix a few strong monsters with weaker ones for variety.
  • Prepare a way to scale up or down if the fight is going too easily or too hard.
  • Give players a reason to fight smart: terrain, objectives and retreat options.

Common encounter building mistakes

  • Forgetting the multiplier for large groups of monsters (2014 rules).
  • Comparing monster XP with the wrong party size.
  • Treating Deadly as impossible to survive.
  • Ignoring how many encounters the party faces in a day.
  • Relying only on CR for monsters with unusual abilities.

Solo bosses, minions and legendary actions

A single strong monster is a classic boss, but it gets one turn a round while the party gets four or more, so a solo creature at the same XP often loses the action economy. Legendary actions, lair actions, minions and phases help even this out. If you build a boss fight, treat the calculator's difficulty as a minimum and add minions, terrain effects or legendary actions to keep the fight tense. For groups of many weak monsters, the number-of-monsters multiplier in the 2014 rules is the main correction for the extra attacks they make; the 2024 budget approach leaves the judgement to you.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate D&D 5e encounter difficulty?

Add up the XP of all monsters, apply the number-of-monsters multiplier (2014 rules), then compare the adjusted XP with the sum of your players' XP thresholds for Easy, Medium, Hard and Deadly. This calculator does each step.

What are the XP thresholds by character level?

They are listed in the Tables tab. For example, a level 3 character has thresholds of 75 (Easy), 150 (Medium), 225 (Hard) and 400 (Deadly) under the 2014 rules.

What is the encounter multiplier?

Under the 2014 rules, more monsters are harder than their XP alone suggests, so total XP is multiplied: ×1 for one monster, ×1.5 for two, ×2 for three to six, ×2.5 for seven to ten, ×3 for eleven to fourteen and ×4 for fifteen or more.

How does party size change the multiplier?

With fewer than three players the multiplier moves up one step, and with six or more it moves down one step. The calculator applies this automatically.

What is an XP budget?

In the 2024 rules each character has an XP budget for Low, Moderate and High difficulty. You add up monster XP and compare the total with the party's budget. There is no multiplier.

What is the difference between the 2014 and 2024 encounter rules?

The 2014 rules use Easy, Medium, Hard and Deadly thresholds with a monster-number multiplier. The 2024 rules use Low, Moderate and High budgets with no multiplier, so the same encounter can be rated differently.

What is a deadly encounter?

Deadly under the 2014 rules means the encounter could kill one or more characters, particularly if the party is unlucky or makes mistakes. It is not a guarantee of death, and many groups use deadly encounters as set-piece fights.

How many monsters can I use for a level 3 party of four?

The How many monsters tab lists, for each CR, how many identical monsters reach each difficulty. For a party of four at level 3, about 3 CR 1/4 monsters reach Easy, 8 reach Hard and 11 reach Deadly under the 2014 rules.

How much XP does each player get?

Divide the total monster XP by the number of players. This calculator shows the unadjusted XP share, because the multiplier only affects difficulty.

Does the calculator include monster stat blocks?

No. It only needs each monster's challenge rating. For stat blocks use the books, the SRD or your own notes.

What is challenge rating (CR)?

CR is a number that rates how dangerous a monster is for a party of four characters of the same level. Each CR has an XP value, for example CR 1 is 200 XP and CR 5 is 1,800 XP.

Can I mix characters of different levels?

Yes. Add up to three groups of players at different levels. Thresholds are added together across all characters.

Why does a boss alone feel easy?

A single powerful monster gets only one set of actions per round, so it can be less dangerous than the same XP split among several creatures. The multiplier partly corrects this, but tactics and action economy matter.

Why do my players find my medium encounters too easy?

The guidelines are conservative for optimised parties and for multiple encounters per day. Use the tool as a starting point, then adjust.

Should I count minions or allies?

Count only the monsters that fight the party. If allied NPCs fight beside the players, add them as extra characters.

Does it work for the free Basic Rules and SRD monsters?

Yes, because it only uses challenge ratings. You can use monsters from any source.

Can I print or export an encounter?

Yes. Print / save as PDF prints only the result for the tab you are on, CSV downloads it and copy summary puts it on your clipboard.

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server or stored.
