What this free online random student picker does

Every teacher knows the pattern. A question goes up, the same three hands rise, and the rest of the room relaxes. Cold calling by random selection changes that. When any student might be called, more of them prepare an answer. A random student picker makes the choice for you, so it feels fair, takes no thought and avoids unconscious favouritism.

This free online picker is built for real lessons. It spins a wheel, but the winner is chosen first and the wheel is calculated to stop on that exact name, so the display and the result can never disagree. You can mark absent students, stop repeats until everyone has had a turn, pick several names in a row, start an answer timer, run the wheel full screen on a projector, and keep a record with a chart of how often each name has come up.

How to use the random student picker

  1. Add your class list. Paste names, one per line. Duplicates are removed.
  2. Mark absent students by clicking their chip. They are struck through and removed from the wheel.
  3. Choose options. Keep no-repeat on for fairness, choose how many names to pick, and set an answer timer if you want one.
  4. Press Spin or the space bar. The wheel turns and stops on a name.
  5. Use fullscreen to show the wheel on a projector.
  6. Start a new round when everyone has been picked, and review the history chart whenever you like.

How the picker keeps the draw fair

The random number comes from the Web Crypto API through an unbiased method, so no name is favoured by rounding. With no-repeat on, a student drops out of the pool after being picked and returns when you reset the round. That guarantees a cycle in which each present student answers once before anyone answers twice.

If you want repeats allowed, untick the option. Every present student then has an equal chance on every spin, so the same name can come up again, as with rolling a die. The history chart shows how that plays out, and you will see that true randomness is a little streaky.

Using random selection well in the classroom

Random cold calling works best when students understand why you use it and feel safe being wrong. Some practical habits help.

  • Ask the question first, then pick. Everyone must prepare an answer.
  • Give think time. Even ten seconds improves answers.
  • Use pair talk before the pick. Students rehearse with a partner so the chosen student speaks for the pair.
  • Allow a pass or a phone-a-friend. This keeps the activity supportive for anxious students.
  • Use the answer timer for quick-fire review, such as 30 seconds to define a term.
  • Vary the use. Pick for questions, for who presents, for who tidies up, for who goes first.

Equity, fairness and sensitivity

Randomness is fair in chance, but not always fair in effect. A student who is shy, new to the language or dealing with a hard day may need extra support. You can mark a student as absent for a round to skip them, give a choice of sitting out, or let them answer from their seat. Avoid using the picker for anything that could embarrass a student, and avoid picking on personal topics.

Do not rely on a picker alone for high-stakes choices such as awards or discipline. Those need clear criteria and a person to take responsibility.

Privacy and saving your list

Your names are saved only in your own browser storage so they are there next time you visit on the same device. They are not uploaded and the site has no account system. The share link works by placing the names in the web address after a hash sign. Anyone with the link can read them, so share it only with colleagues, and clear your list when the class finishes. Use first names or initials if you prefer.

Frequently asked questions

What is a random student picker?

A tool that chooses a name from your class list by chance so you can call on students fairly. This one shows a spinning wheel and keeps a record of who has been picked.

How do I use the random student picker?

Type or paste your class list, one name per line, and press Spin. The wheel stops on a student and shows the name in large type.

Is the pick really random?

Yes. The tool picks the name first with your browser's cryptographic random number generator, then turns the wheel to land on it. Every eligible name has an equal chance.

Why does the wheel always land on the name shown?

Some pickers draw a random angle and read the name afterwards, which can disagree with the display. This tool decides the name first and calculates the stopping angle, so the pointer and the winner always match.

How do I avoid picking the same student twice?

Keep the no-repeat option ticked. Each picked student leaves the wheel until everyone has had a turn, then press Reset round to start again.

How do I skip absent students?

Click a name in the chip list to mark it absent. It is struck through and removed from the wheel. Click again when they return.

Can I pick several students at once?

Yes. Set Names to pick per spin from 1 to 10. The wheel spins once for each name, and nobody is picked twice in the same run.

Can I give students time to answer?

Set an answer timer in seconds and a countdown starts after each pick.

Will my list be saved?

Yes, in your own browser, so it is there next time on the same device. It is never uploaded.

How do I share or reopen my list on another device?

Press Copy link to this list. The link carries your names in the web address, so open it anywhere to load the same list. Share it only with people who may see the names.

Can I show the wheel on a projector?

Press Fullscreen and the wheel and the winner's name fill the screen. Press the space bar to spin when you are not typing in a box.

Does it keep a record?

Yes. It records the order of picks and counts how often each name was picked, with a chart. You can copy the history, download a CSV file or print a record.

Can I turn off the animation?

Yes. Untick Animate the wheel to jump straight to the result. The wheel also skips animation if your device asks for reduced motion.

How many names can I add?

Up to 200. With more than 40 names the wheel hides the labels because they would be too small, but the winner is still shown clearly.

Is it fair to call on students randomly?

It removes the bias of favouring volunteers, and it encourages every student to stay ready. Many teachers combine it with think time and pair talk so the experience is supportive.

Can I use it for things other than students?

Yes. Use it for raffles at a class party, choosing who goes first, picking topics, or any list of names or words.

Is it suitable for official prize draws?

It is fine for classroom use. For prizes or legal selection, use an audited process.
