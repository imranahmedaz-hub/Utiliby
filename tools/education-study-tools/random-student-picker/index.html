What is a free random student picker online?

A free random student picker online is a tool that selects a student from your class list at random. Instead of scanning the room and unconsciously calling on the same eager hands, you paste your roster, spin the wheel, and one student is chosen. The pick is visibly fair, which removes the "you always pick her" complaint, and the spinning wheel adds a game-like anticipation that students actually enjoy.

This random student picker goes beyond a basic name selector. It includes a no-repeat mode that ensures every student is picked once before anyone is picked twice — essential for cold calling across a full class period. It supports picking one, two, three or four students at a time. It stores your class list in the URL so you can bookmark it and return to it without re-typing. It has a fullscreen mode for displaying on a projector or smartboard. And it produces an embed code so you can drop the picker into Google Slides, PowerPoint (web), Moodle, Canvas or Notion. Everything runs in your browser — no sign-up, no account, no upload, no tracking.

How to use this free classroom name picker

  1. Paste your class list into the text area, one name per line. You can paste directly from a spreadsheet.
  2. Choose your pick mode. "Allow repeats" is the standard random pick. "No repeats until everyone is picked" ensures fairness across the whole class.
  3. Select how many students to pick at a time — useful for group activities, presentations or pair work.
  4. Click Spin the wheel. The wheel spins, decelerates, and lands on a random student (or students).
  5. Use Fullscreen to display the wheel on a projector. Use Reset picked to clear the no-repeat history.

Why random selection matters in the classroom

Research on classroom questioning consistently shows that teachers unconsciously favour certain students — often those who sit in the front, raise their hands most, or share the teacher's background. A random picker removes that bias. It also raises the general level of attention, because every student knows they might be next. And it signals to the class that participation is expected from everyone, not just the volunteers.

The no-repeat mode is particularly useful for cold calling across a full lesson. Without it, a class of 25 students will see some students picked two or three times and others not at all. With it, every student is picked once before the cycle restarts.

Classroom use cases

  • Cold calling. Pick the next student to answer a question without any perception of bias.
  • Presentation order. Randomly decide who presents first, second and third, removing the "I don't want to go first" argument.
  • Group formation. Use the picker to select group leaders, then have them choose teammates.
  • Reading aloud. Pick who reads the next paragraph. Students follow along because they might be next.
  • Prize draws. Pick a winner fairly for a classroom reward.
  • Turn-taking. Use no-repeat mode to ensure every student has a turn at a classroom task.
