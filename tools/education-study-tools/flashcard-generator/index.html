What this free online flashcard generator does

Flashcards work because they force you to recall an answer rather than re-read it, and recall is what builds memory. The hard part is the admin: writing cards, deciding what to review today and keeping track of what you know. This free online flashcard generator takes care of that. Paste a list, build the deck in one click and study with a schedule that decides what to show next.

It goes beyond a simple card flipper. A spaced repetition scheduler tracks every card, three study modes let you flip, take a multiple choice quiz or type answers, and your deck and progress are saved in the browser. A progress tab shows how much of the deck is new, learning, young or mature, how many reviews are due over the next two weeks and how many cards you reviewed each day. You can import a CSV, pronounce terms aloud, download the deck and print a clean study sheet.

How to use this flashcard generator

  1. Name your deck and paste your list, one pair per line, such as bonjour = hello. Or import a CSV file.
  2. Press Build deck. The tool detects the separator, skips duplicates and tells you how many cards were created.
  3. Open the Study tab and choose a mode and direction. Set how many new cards to learn per session.
  4. Grade yourself honestly. Press Again, Hard, Good or Easy, or use the keys 1 to 4. The next review date is set for you.
  5. Come back daily. Due cards appear first, then new ones.
  6. Check the Progress tab to see mastery and upcoming reviews.

How spaced repetition works

Spaced repetition is built on the forgetting curve: memory fades quickly after learning and more slowly each time it is refreshed. If you review a card just before you would forget it, the memory is strengthened and the next gap can be longer. Done well, a few minutes a day is enough to hold hundreds of cards.

The scheduler here follows a simplified SM-2 method, the algorithm behind many popular apps. A new card is shown again after one day, then after about six days, and after that the gap is multiplied by the card's ease factor, which rises when you answer easily and falls when you struggle. Cards you forget return to the queue within the same session and their ease is reduced.

A good routine is to start new cards in multiple choice mode, then move to flip and type modes as they become familiar. Meaning to term direction is harder, and mixing the direction makes the test closer to real use.

Recognition and production

  • Term to meaning tests recognition, which is how you read and listen. It is the easier direction and the usual first step.
  • Meaning to term tests production, which is how you speak and write. It is harder and matters for exams that require spelling.
  • Mixed randomises the direction for each card and is the most demanding.

Building an effective deck

  • Keep it small. Twenty to fifty cards per topic are easier to finish than five hundred.
  • One fact per card. Split a long definition into several cards.
  • Add context. A short example, such as banca = bank (money), separates meanings that look alike.
  • Use your own words. Cards you wrote yourself are remembered better than copied ones.
  • Study daily. Ten minutes every day beats two hours once a week.
  • Say it aloud. For languages, pronunciation strengthens memory. Use the Hear term button to check it.

Importing from a spreadsheet or file

Select two columns in Excel or Google Sheets, copy them and paste into the text box. Spreadsheet rows arrive separated by tabs, and the tool detects that automatically. You can also import a CSV or text file with the file box. Quoted values are cleaned up, blank lines are ignored and duplicate pairs are skipped. A separator inside the meaning, such as a comma in a definition, is kept because only the first separator on each line is used.

Frequently asked questions

What is a flashcard generator?

A tool that turns a list of terms and meanings into a deck of study cards. Paste your list, build the deck and study it on screen, with quizzes, typing practice and a review schedule.

What is spaced repetition?

A study method that increases the time between reviews each time you recall a card correctly. Hard cards come back soon and easy cards come back after days or weeks, which makes revision efficient.

How does the scheduler work?

It uses a simplified version of the SM-2 method. Each card has an interval and an ease factor. Good answers grow the interval, hard answers grow it slowly and an Again answer resets it and returns the card to the queue.

What do Again, Hard, Good and Easy mean?

Again means you forgot. Hard means you remembered with effort. Good means a normal recall. Easy means you knew it instantly. Each button shows the next interval.

What formats can I paste?

Lines separated by equals, dash, tab, comma, semicolon, pipe or colon. The tool can detect the separator, so you can paste straight from a spreadsheet.

Can I import a file?

Yes. Use the file box to load a CSV or text file with two columns. Cards are added to the list and the deck is rebuilt.

Will my progress be saved?

Yes, in your own browser. Your deck, intervals and daily history are kept so you can return later. They are not synced between devices.

How do I move my deck to another device?

Use Copy deck or Download deck to save the list, then paste or import it on the other device. Progress for the new device starts fresh.

What study modes are there?

Flip and grade for spaced repetition, a multiple choice quiz that picks plausible wrong answers from your own deck, and a typing mode that ignores accents and capital letters.

Can I study in both directions?

Yes. Choose term to meaning, meaning to term, or mixed. Meaning to term is harder and is closer to speaking and writing.

Can the cards be read aloud?

Yes. Pick a language and press Hear term, or turn on automatic speech. It uses your browser's built-in voices, so quality varies by device.

How many cards should be in a deck?

Twenty to fifty cards is a good size for one topic. Large lists can be split into themed decks. The tool allows up to 2,000 cards.

How many new cards should I learn per day?

Ten to twenty new cards a day is sustainable for most people. The limit is adjustable, and reviews of old cards always come first.

Can I print the deck?

Yes. Print produces a clean table of terms and meanings, without the rest of the page.

Does it work for subjects other than languages?

Yes. Any term and definition pairs work, such as medical terms, dates, formulas, capitals or exam facts.

How is this different from Anki?

This tool needs no account or install and works instantly in the browser. Anki offers deeper options, sync and add-ons. For quick decks and light use, this tool is faster to start.

Is my data private?

Yes. Your list never leaves your browser and nothing is uploaded.
