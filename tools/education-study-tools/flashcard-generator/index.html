What is a free language flashcard generator online?

A free language flashcard generator online is a tool that turns a word list into a study deck. You paste your vocabulary — one term and its meaning per line — and the tool creates a set of flashcards you can flip through in study mode or test yourself on in quiz mode. Instead of writing out each card by hand, you build the whole deck in one paste.

This language flashcard generator goes beyond a simple card flipper. It includes a spaced repetition scheduler that tracks which items you know and which you need to review more often. It supports three delimiters (equals, dash and tab) so you can paste directly from a spreadsheet or a vocabulary list. It can flip the study direction — term to meaning, meaning to term, or a random mix of both. And it lets you print the deck as a PDF or copy the list back as plain text. Everything runs in your browser — no sign-up, no account, no upload, no tracking.

How to use this free flashcard maker

  1. Paste your vocabulary list, one pair per line, using "term = meaning" format. For a spreadsheet paste, select Tab as the delimiter.
  2. Choose the study direction. "Term → Meaning" is the standard vocabulary recall direction. "Meaning → Term" trains production, which is harder.
  3. Click Build deck. The cards appear one at a time in study mode.
  4. Click a card to flip it and reveal the answer.
  5. Mark whether you knew the card. The spaced repetition scheduler uses this to decide when to show it again.
  6. Continue until the deck is exhausted. Click Print to save a paper copy of the deck, or Copy as text to save the list.

How spaced repetition works

Spaced repetition is a study method in which review intervals increase each time you recall an item correctly. A new card appears frequently; once you have recalled it correctly a few times, the interval grows to days, then weeks. Items you find difficult stay in the frequent-review pile. Research consistently shows that spaced repetition produces better long-term retention than re-reading or massed practice.

The scheduler in this tool is a simplified version of the SM-2 algorithm used by Anki. It tracks each card's interval, its ease factor and the number of successful recalls. When you mark a card as "known", the interval increases. When you mark it as "again", the interval resets. If you leave the tool and come back later, the schedule is restored from your browser's local storage.

Study direction: recognition vs production

  • Recognition (Term → Meaning). You see the foreign word and recall what it means. This is the easier direction and is usually the first one to master.
  • Production (Meaning → Term). You see the meaning and recall the foreign word. This is harder and is the direction that matters for speaking and writing.
  • Mixed. Cards are shown in a random direction each time. This is the most challenging and the most realistic for exam conditions.

Building an effective vocabulary deck

  • Keep it small. A deck of 20–30 cards is easier to finish than a deck of 500. Split a large vocabulary list into themed sub-decks.
  • One word per card. Avoid putting multiple words or a full sentence on a single card. The card should test one fact.
  • Use context where it helps. For tricky words, add a short example sentence after the meaning: "banca = bank (financial institution)".
  • Review daily. Spaced repetition works by being consistent. Ten minutes a day beats two hours on a Sunday.
  • Say it out loud. For language learning, saying the word as you see the card strengthens the pronunciation memory as well as the meaning.

Importing from a spreadsheet

If your word list is in Excel, Google Sheets or a CSV file, you can paste the two columns directly into the text area. Select Tab as the delimiter. Each row becomes one card, with the first column as the term and the second as the meaning. The tool ignores blank lines automatically.

Printing flashcards

Click Print / Save as PDF to produce a printable deck. The print layout shows each card with the term and meaning on the same sheet, formatted as a grid. This is useful for offline study, for handing a deck to a student who does not have a device, or for cutting out physical cards.
