What this free online rubric generator does

Marking without a rubric is slow and uneven. Two similar essays can get different marks on different days, and students are left guessing what the grade means. A rubric solves this by naming the qualities that matter and describing what each level of performance looks like. It speeds marking, makes it fairer and turns a grade into feedback.

This free online rubric generator builds one in minutes. Choose from twelve templates, from essays to lab reports to programming, then edit criteria, weights and descriptors. Switch between analytic, single-point and holistic formats, choose three, four or five levels, and see a live preview with the maximum score. In the grade tab, pick a level for each criterion to get a score, percentage, letter grade and a chart of a student's profile. Copy the rubric into Word or Sheets, download a CSV or print only the rubric.

How to create a rubric step by step

  1. Pick a template close to your task, or start blank.
  2. Choose a type and number of levels. Analytic with four levels suits most tasks.
  3. Edit the criteria. Rename them, set weights and rewrite descriptors in your own words.
  4. Reorder or delete criteria so the rubric has a manageable number, usually four to six.
  5. Check the preview and the maximum score, then copy as a table or print.
  6. Grade with it. Open the grade tab, select a level for each criterion and add comments.

How to write strong criteria and descriptors

  • Link each criterion to a learning goal. If you did not teach it, do not mark it.
  • Keep criteria separate. Do not combine ideas such as organisation and spelling in a single row.
  • Describe quality, not quantity. Replace five examples with relevant, well-chosen examples.
  • Use parallel wording across levels. Change the degree rather than introducing new ideas.
  • Write positively. Describe what the work does, not only what it lacks.
  • Test it. Mark two or three real samples and revise any descriptor that was hard to apply.

Weighting and scoring

Without weights every criterion is worth the same. In an essay rubric, you might give argument and evidence a weight of 2 and conventions a weight of 1, so the marks reflect what you value most. The maximum score is the highest level points multiplied by each weight, summed over criteria. Percent is score divided by maximum. The letter grade then applies a common scale, but you can ignore it and use your own boundaries.

ExampleCalculation
4 criteria, weight 1, 4 levelsMaximum 16. Ratings 4, 3, 3, 2 give 12 of 16, or 75 percent

Using rubrics for feedback and self-assessment

Rubrics work best when they are shared before the task, discussed with examples and used by students to check their own work. The grade tab can be used by students for self-assessment: they rate their draft, you rate it, and the differences become the conversation. Single-point rubrics are especially useful for comments, because the blank columns invite specific notes.

Fairness and accessibility

A rubric reduces bias but does not remove it. Mark one criterion across all papers at a time where practical, anonymise work when possible and revisit descriptors if they disadvantage certain groups. Check that criteria do not penalise accommodations, for example by judging handwriting when a student types by agreement. Write descriptors in plain language and give students a printed or screen reader friendly copy.

Accuracy, limits and responsible use

Templates are general starting points. A rubric is only as valid as its criteria, and scores from a rubric should inform, not replace, professional judgement. Grades calculated here are for your information and do not change any official record.

Frequently asked questions

What is a rubric?

A scoring guide that lists the criteria for a task and describes what work looks like at each performance level. It makes marking consistent and tells students what is expected.

What types of rubric can I make?

Analytic rubrics with a grid of criteria and levels, single-point rubrics that describe only the proficient standard, and holistic rubrics that give one overall description for each level.

Which type should I choose?

Use analytic for detailed feedback on several skills, single-point for feedback-rich formative work, and holistic for fast overall judgements such as short responses.

How many performance levels should a rubric have?

Four is common because it avoids a neutral middle. Three is quicker, and five adds a No evidence level. Fewer well-described levels are better than many vague ones.

What are rubric criteria?

The qualities you will judge, such as thesis, evidence, organisation and conventions in an essay. Each should be separate, observable and tied to a learning goal.

How do I weight criteria?

Use the weight box. A weight of 2 counts the criterion twice, so it is worth twice as many points. The maximum score updates automatically.

How do I write good performance descriptors?

Use observable, specific language and describe quality rather than counting things. Avoid vague words such as good or poor, and make each level clearly different from the next.

Can I grade a student with the rubric?

Yes. Open the grade tab, pick a level for each criterion, and see the score, percentage, letter grade and a bar chart of strengths and gaps.

How is the letter grade decided?

It uses a common scale of 90, 80, 70 and 60 percent for A to D. Treat it as a guide and apply your own grading policy.

Can I edit the templates?

Yes. Every name, weight and descriptor is editable. You can add, delete and reorder criteria.

Can I paste the rubric into Word or Google Docs?

Use Copy as table and paste. You can also copy spreadsheet text for Excel or Google Sheets, or download a CSV file.

What does printing include?

Only the rubric table, plus the graded result and chart if you have graded a student. The rest of the page is hidden.

Is my rubric saved?

It is saved automatically in your browser so you can return to it. Nothing is uploaded.

Should I share the rubric with students?

Yes. Sharing it before the task helps students aim at the criteria, and research on assessment suggests it improves the quality of their work.

What is a single-point rubric?

A rubric with one column that states the proficient standard and blank columns beside it for areas to improve and evidence of exceeding the standard. It encourages personal feedback.

Can students use it for self-assessment?

Yes. Students can rate their own work with the grade tab before handing it in, then compare it with the teacher's rating.

Is it suitable for university and school?

Yes. The templates cover school and university tasks, and the criteria can be adapted to any level.
