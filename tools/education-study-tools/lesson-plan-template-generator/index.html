What makes a strong lesson plan

A good lesson plan is a working document, not a formality. It should be short enough to actually use during class and clear enough that a substitute teacher could follow it. Research on effective teaching consistently points to a few essentials:

  • Clear learning objectives — what students will know or do by the end.
  • A measurable assessment — how you'll know whether they got there.
  • A logical sequence — warm-up, instruction, practice, closure.
  • Student-centered activities — students should be doing, not just listening.
  • Timing guidance — rough minutes per section so the lesson stays on track.

Writing measurable objectives

Use action verbs tied to observable behavior. Vague verbs like understand, know or appreciate are hard to assess. Better choices, grouped by cognitive level:

  • Remember / Understand: define, list, describe, explain, summarize
  • Apply / Analyze: solve, calculate, compare, differentiate, categorize
  • Evaluate / Create: justify, critique, design, construct, formulate

A simple structure: "Students will be able to [verb] [content] with [accuracy or condition]." For example: "Students will be able to add fractions with unlike denominators with 80 percent accuracy on an exit ticket."

The 5E lesson model (alternative structure)

Many science and inquiry-based lessons use the 5E model. If you prefer it, simply map the sections of this template:

  • Engage → Warm-up
  • Explore → Guided practice
  • Explain → Direct instruction
  • Elaborate → Independent practice
  • Evaluate → Assessment / exit ticket

Frequently asked questions

What should a lesson plan include?

A strong lesson plan includes learning objectives, materials, a warm-up, direct instruction, guided practice, independent practice, assessment and closure. It should also state the subject, grade level, duration and any standards being addressed.

How long should a lesson plan be?

Most lesson plans are one to two pages. Shorter is fine for a single 45-minute class. Longer plans are useful for multi-day units. The goal is a working document you can actually use in the classroom.

How do I write good learning objectives?

Use measurable action verbs from Bloom's Taxonomy. Instead of "students will understand fractions", write "students will add fractions with unlike denominators and explain their steps with 80 percent accuracy on an exit ticket".

What is the difference between objectives and standards?

Standards are set by a governing body and describe what students should know by the end of a grade or course. Objectives describe what students will achieve in this specific lesson.
