What this free online exam timer does

Most students do not fail a paper because they cannot answer the questions. They run out of time. They spend too long on an early question they find hard, then rush the questions they could have answered well. A good exam timer fixes the problem by making time visible and by forcing a decision at a set moment: move on, flag the question and come back.

This free online exam timer works as a simple countdown for invigilators and as a pacing trainer for students. It can run a single total clock with warnings, or give each question its own limit and alarm. A live ring shows how much time is left and changes colour as it runs down. In per-question mode, a timeline of coloured blocks shows which questions are finished, which were over time and which is current. At the end you get a summary table and chart of time allowed versus time used, your minutes per mark and your biggest overrun.

How to use this exam timer

  1. Choose a mode. Single total timer for a normal countdown, or per-question pacing for practice.
  2. Enter the total time, or pick a preset such as a 2-hour paper or IELTS Writing.
  3. Add reading time and warnings if your exam has them. For example, enter 10,5,1 to be warned at ten, five and one minute left.
  4. In per-question mode, list your questions with marks and minutes. Use Split total evenly or Allocate by marks to fill the limits automatically.
  5. Press Start or the space bar. Use Pause, Next question, plus and minus one minute, or Fullscreen as needed.
  6. Review the summary when the session ends, then copy, download or print it.

How to budget time in an exam

A reliable starting point is minutes per mark = total minutes / total marks. A 60-minute paper worth 60 marks gives one minute per mark, so a 5-mark question gets about five minutes. Leave a buffer of five to ten percent for checking. In a 3-hour paper worth 100 marks, that is about 1.8 minutes per mark, or 160 minutes of working time and 20 minutes of review.

Not every question deserves the same rate. Reserve extra time for questions with heavy marks, and be strict with questions that give few marks for a lot of work. The Allocate by marks button applies this proportional split to your total.

Why per-question pacing works

Time pressure bends decision-making. Students tend to over-invest in questions they find hard, because stopping feels like giving up, and the hardest questions rarely pay the most marks. Setting a limit in advance turns stopping into a plan rather than a failure. When the alarm sounds, you move on, mark the question for review and return if there is spare time.

Practising this under a timer builds a feel for how long things take. After a few sessions, the summary reveals patterns: you may always run over on essays and under on calculations, or you may spend most of your time in the first section. Those patterns tell you exactly what to train.

Using the timer as an invigilator or teacher

  • Show the clock in fullscreen on a projector so candidates can see the time without asking.
  • Use reading time to count the quiet period before writing begins.
  • Set warnings at 15, 5 and 1 minute so announcements are consistent.
  • Add or remove a minute with one click to handle interruptions or accommodations.
  • Print the plan for your records.

Remember that this is a practice and classroom aid. For official examinations, use the equipment your exam board or school specifies.

Exam timing tips

  • Do a first pass. Answer the questions you can do quickly, flag the rest and return.
  • Read the marks. Spend time in proportion to what each question is worth.
  • Plan before you write. Two minutes of planning an essay often saves ten of rewriting.
  • Keep a check window. Reserve the last five minutes for reviewing answers and calculations.
  • Practise under realistic conditions. Same time limit, no phone, one sitting.
  • Review your summary. Aim to reduce your biggest overrun each week.

Accuracy and device tips

The timer calculates time from the device clock, so it stays correct even when the browser throttles a background tab. For the best experience, keep the page open, plug in a laptop or tablet, and turn off battery saving. The tool asks the browser to keep the screen awake while it runs, where supported. Sounds require a click on Start because browsers block automatic audio.

Frequently asked questions

What is an exam timer?

A countdown timer for tests, practice papers and revision. This one can run a single total countdown or give every question its own limit, with warnings and an alarm.

What is per-question pacing?

Setting a time limit for each question instead of watching only the total clock. It trains you to move on when a question is taking too long, which is a leading cause of unfinished papers.

How do I work out time per question?

Divide the total minutes by the total marks to get minutes per mark, then multiply by the marks for each question. The Allocate by marks button does this for you, and you should leave a few minutes to check your work.

Can the timer warn me before time runs out?

Yes. Enter minutes remaining, such as 10,5,1, and the timer sounds a short alert at each point. The clock also changes colour in the final minute.

What is reading time?

A quiet period before writing starts, used in some exams to read the paper. Enter the minutes in the reading time box and the timer counts it first, then switches to the exam clock.

Does the timer move on to the next question by itself?

In per-question mode it can. Tick the option and the next question starts when time is up. Untick it to keep going over time and move on yourself with the Next button.

Can I add or remove time during the exam?

Yes. Use the plus and minus buttons to change the current limit by one minute, for example to give extra time for an accommodation.

Will the timer keep working if I switch tabs?

Yes. It measures real elapsed time from the device clock rather than counting ticks, so it stays accurate even if the browser slows a background tab. Keep the tab open and the screen awake.

Can I show the timer on a projector?

Press the Fullscreen button, or the F key, and the clock fills the display with large digits that can be read from across a room.

What sounds are available?

A beep, a softer chime, or silent mode with visual warnings only. You can set the volume. Browsers only allow sound after you click Start.

What does the summary show?

After a per-question session it lists each question with its limit, the time you actually spent, the difference and minutes per mark, plus a chart. It highlights your biggest overrun.

Can I save or print the results?

Yes. Copy the summary, download a CSV file, or print. The print output contains only the report, not the whole page.

Is the timer suitable for official exams?

It is a study and invigilation aid, not certified timing equipment. For formal exams use the official clock.

Which presets are included?

A 60-minute test, a 2-hour paper, IELTS Writing, IELTS Reading, a timed essay and a 10-question quiz. All values can be edited.

How can I use it for revision?

Do past papers under timed conditions in per-question mode, then use the summary to see where you lose time and adjust your approach.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens, and fullscreen works where the browser supports it.

Is my data stored?

Your settings are kept in your own browser so they are there next time. Nothing is sent to a server.
