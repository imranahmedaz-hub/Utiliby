What is a free exam timer online?

A free exam timer online is a countdown timer designed for exam practice or invigilation. Most timers only track the total session. This exam timer goes further: it can be configured with per-question time limits, so each question on a practice paper gets its own countdown and alarm. That trains you to move on when a question is taking too long, which is the single biggest reason students run out of time in real exams.

The exam timer also works as a standard full-screen countdown for invigilators and study sessions. It includes a fullscreen mode for displaying on a projector or smartboard, a choice between an audible beep and a silent visual-only alarm, and a summary screen at the end of per-question sessions that shows how long each question actually took. Everything runs in your browser — no sign-up, no account, no upload, no tracking.

How to use this free exam timer

  1. Choose your mode. "Single total timer" for a standard countdown. "Per-question pacing" for a question-by-question practice session.
  2. For total mode, enter the total duration and click Start timer.
  3. For per-question mode, enter a name and a time limit for each question. Add more questions with the + button.
  4. Choose the sound — beep for an audible alarm, silent for a visual-only flash.
  5. Click Start timer. In per-question mode, the timer counts down each question in turn and sounds an alarm when time is up, then automatically moves to the next question.
  6. At the end, the summary shows each question, its time limit and how long you actually spent on it.

Why per-question pacing matters

In a timed exam, students rarely run out of time evenly. They tend to over-invest in early questions — often the ones they find hardest — and then rush the final section. Per-question pacing forces a decision at a set time: move on, flag the question and come back if there is spare time at the end. Over several practice sessions, this trains the pacing instinct that experienced test-takers have.

For invigilators, the total timer is the standard tool. Display it fullscreen on the room projector so candidates can see the remaining time without asking. The urgent mode turns the clock red in the final minutes.

Exam timing tips

  • Divide the time by the marks. If a paper is 60 minutes and worth 60 marks, you have roughly one minute per mark. Give harder questions a little more and easier ones less.
  • Do a first pass. Answer the questions you can do quickly first. Flag the hard ones and come back.
  • Set a per-question limit that is realistic. If a question is worth 5 marks and you have 60 minutes for 60 marks, the limit is about 5 minutes, plus a small buffer.
  • Practise with the timer. The point is to build instinct, not to check the clock on the day.
