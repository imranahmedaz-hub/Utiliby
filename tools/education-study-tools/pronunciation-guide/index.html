What this free online pronunciation guide does

English spelling is a poor guide to English sound. The same letters can sound different (through, though, tough, cough) and the same sound can be written many ways. Learners and native speakers alike meet words such as comfortable, February and colonel and wonder how to say them. A good pronunciation guide answers with more than audio: it shows the sounds, the stress and the reason a word is tricky.

This free online guide covers the words people most often look up. For each you get IPA in American or British English, a plain respelling with the stressed syllable in capitals, a visual stress diagram and a short note. You can hear the word at normal or slow speed, explore an interactive IPA chart, speak any phrase you type, and keep a personal list to print or download.

How to use the pronunciation guide

  1. Type a word in the search box. Suggestions appear as you type.
  2. Choose your accent, American or British, and a speaking speed.
  3. Read the entry: the IPA, the respelling and the note. The stress diagram shows the loud syllable as the tall block.
  4. Press Hear it and then Slowly, and say the word aloud after the voice.
  5. Add the word to My list to practise later, then copy, download or print the list.
  6. Explore: browse words by category, tap IPA symbols in the chart, or paste a sentence in the speak tab.

How to read the International Phonetic Alphabet

IPA uses one symbol per sound, so you can decode a word you have never heard. A mark such as ˈ before a syllable shows primary stress, and ː after a vowel shows a long vowel. Symbols such as θ (as in think), ð (as in this), ʃ (as in ship) and ŋ (as in sing) cover sounds that spelling hides. The IPA chart tab lets you hear an example for each of the sounds of English.

You do not need to learn every symbol at once. Start with the ones that differ from your first language and the stress mark, then add more as you meet them.

Why word stress matters

English is a stress-timed language. Stressed syllables are longer and louder, and unstressed ones shrink, often to a weak vowel called schwa (ə). That is why comfortable sounds like KUMF-ter-bul and not com-FORT-a-ble. Getting the stress right often helps a listener more than getting each sound perfect. The stress diagram makes the pattern visible: the tall block is the syllable to push.

Stress can also change meaning. REcord is a noun and reCORD is a verb. PREsent is a gift and preSENT is to give. When a word has two common patterns, the note says so.

Common types of tricky English words

  • Silent letters: debt, knife, island, hour, psychology, pneumonia.
  • Dropped syllables: comfortable, vegetable, chocolate, interesting.
  • Irregular spellings: through, thorough, enough, cough, though.
  • Borrowed words: croissant, genre, gnocchi, bourgeois, entrepreneur.
  • Place names: Worcestershire, Leicester, Edinburgh, Illinois, Arkansas.
  • Academic terms: hierarchy, hypothesis, mitochondria, photosynthesis.

The category filter in the browse list lets you practise one type at a time.

How to practise pronunciation well

  1. Listen to the word several times, slowly first.
  2. Say only the stressed syllable, then add the rest.
  3. Record yourself and compare it to the voice.
  4. Practise in a short sentence, because stress and rhythm work across words.
  5. Revisit your saved list every few days. Spaced practice helps memory, as covered in our flashcard generator.

Teachers can use the list for a lesson by saving a set of words, printing them and adding a pair-work speaking task.

Privacy and the online lookup

Your saved list stays in your browser. The guide works fully offline for the built-in words. The optional online lookup is off by default. When you turn it on and search for a word that is not in the list, that word is sent to dictionaryapi.dev, a third-party service, so it can find a transcription. No other information is sent, and the results are shown as unchecked.

Accuracy, limits and responsible use

Transcriptions are simplified, and real speech varies. This guide is not a substitute for a full dictionary, a teacher or a native speaker. The built-in speech voices belong to your browser and may mispronounce unusual words. For exams, medical or legal terms, always check an authoritative source.

Frequently asked questions

What is a pronunciation guide?

A reference that shows how a word is said. This one gives the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA), a plain-English respelling, the stressed syllable and a note for tricky words.

What is IPA?

The International Phonetic Alphabet is a system where each symbol stands for one speech sound. It is used in most dictionaries, so one symbol always means the same sound.

How do I read the respelling?

It writes the word as syllables in plain English letters, such as KUMF-ter-bul. Capital letters mark the syllable that gets the main stress.

What is word stress?

The syllable said louder, longer and with a higher pitch. Wrong stress is a common reason that a correctly pronounced word is still hard to understand.

What is the difference between US and UK pronunciation?

Differences include the pronunciation of 'r' after vowels, vowels in words like aunt and tomato, and stress in words like laboratory. Choose the accent you want and the guide shows it.

Can I hear the words spoken?

Yes. Press Hear it or Slowly. The tool uses your device's built-in text-to-speech voices, and you can pick a voice and speed.

Why does the voice sometimes sound wrong?

Built-in voices are computer generated and vary by device. Use them as a rough model and rely on the IPA and respelling for exact sounds.

How many words are in the guide?

More than a hundred of the words learners most often mispronounce, grouped as tricky spellings, silent letters, US and UK differences and academic vocabulary.

What if my word is not listed?

Use the phrase tab to hear any text spoken, or switch on the online lookup, which fetches a transcription from a free dictionary service. Online results are not checked by us.

Is the online lookup private?

It is off by default. If you switch it on, the word you type is sent to dictionaryapi.dev to find it. Nothing else is sent.

What are silent letters?

Letters written but not pronounced, such as the b in debt or the k in knife. They come from older pronunciations or from other languages.

What is the IPA chart tab for?

It lists the 44 sounds of English with an example word for each. Tap a symbol to hear its example slowly.

Can I save words to practise later?

Yes. Add a word to your list. The list is stored in your browser, so it is there next time, and you can copy, download or print it.

How do I practise pronunciation?

Listen, repeat out loud, record yourself and compare. Practise the stressed syllable first, then say the word at slow speed and build up to normal speed.

Is this suitable for English learners and teachers?

Yes. Learners can check hard words, and teachers can build and print word lists for a lesson.

What does printing include?

Only the current word card, its stress diagram and your saved list. The rest of the page is hidden.

Are the transcriptions official?

They are simplified guides. Dictionaries differ and speakers vary, so check an authoritative dictionary for formal or exam use.
