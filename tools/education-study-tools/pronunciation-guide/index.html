Understanding IPA

The International Phonetic Alphabet is a standardised system where each symbol represents exactly one sound. Unlike English spelling, which is famously inconsistent — through, though, tough and cough all end differently — IPA is regular. Once you know the symbols, you can pronounce any word that is transcribed.

/iː/Long "ee"see, tree, machine
/ɪ/Short "i"sit, bit, him
/e/Short "e"bed, said, head
/æ/Flat "a"cat, bat, hand
/ʌ/Short "u"cup, love, some
/ə/Schwa (unstressed)about, sofa, together

/ð/Voiced "th"this, mother, breathe
/ʃ/"sh"ship, nation, special

Why English spelling is not a pronunciation guide

English has around 44 sounds and only 26 letters. That mismatch, combined with centuries of borrowing from other languages, produces spellings that are famously unreliable. The letters "ough" are pronounced at least eight different ways: through, though, tough, cough, thought, bough, hiccough and slough. This is why IPA exists, and why a pronunciation guide that shows both IPA and a phonetic respelling is useful — it lets you check the sound and also see how the sound relates to familiar letters.

Syllables and stress

The tool breaks each word into syllables and marks the stressed syllable. Stress is one of the hardest parts of English pronunciation for learners, and it changes meaning in some pairs: REcord (noun) versus reCORD (verb); PREsent (noun) versus preSENT (verb). Listening to the audio while watching the syllable breakdown trains the ear and the eye together.

Accent differences

  • American English. Rhotic — the "r" is pronounced in car, hard, bird. The vowel in dance and can't is flatter.
  • British English (RP). Non-rhotic — the "r" is silent in car but reappears in car park. The vowel in dance and can't is a long "ah".
  • Australian English. Non-rhotic, with distinctive vowel shifts. The ay sound in day is raised, and better often sounds like bedder.

None of these accents is more correct than the others. The tool shows each so you can hear the difference and choose the accent you want to learn or teach.
