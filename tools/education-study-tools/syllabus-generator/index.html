What this free online syllabus generator does

A syllabus is the first document students read and the one they come back to most. It answers the practical questions: when and where does the class meet, how will I be assessed, what happens if I am late or ill, and what is due next week. A clear one reduces emails, prevents disputes and sets the tone of the course. A vague one creates confusion that lasts all term.

This free online syllabus generator helps you build a complete one quickly. Enter the course details, outcomes and materials, generate a dated weekly schedule, and set grading weights that are checked to total 100 percent with a visual breakdown. Pick policies for late work, attendance and generative AI, and include accessibility, integrity and support statements. Preview the document, then copy it, download a Word file, print it or export a calendar file with class sessions and due dates.

How to write a syllabus step by step

  1. Course tab. Add the title, code, term, instructor, office hours, meeting times and prerequisites.
  2. Describe the course in two or three sentences and write learning outcomes with action verbs.
  3. Schedule tab. Pick a start date and number of weeks, then add topics, readings and due dates. Tick No class for breaks.
  4. Grading tab. List components with weights until the check shows 100 percent.
  5. Choose policies and include the institutional statements you need.
  6. Preview the syllabus and then copy, download, print or export a calendar.

Writing learning outcomes

Outcomes describe what students will be able to do, not what you will cover. Explain how research methods test claims is stronger than Cover research methods. Use observable verbs such as explain, apply, analyse, evaluate and design, and aim for three to eight outcomes. Link each major assessment to at least one outcome so students can see the point of each task. Our lesson plan template generator includes a Bloom's taxonomy verb checker that works equally well for course-level outcomes.

Setting up grading and assessment

Weights should reflect what you value. If a course aims to build skills through practice, a final exam worth eighty percent sends the wrong message. Many instructors use several smaller assessments and one or two larger ones, so that no single day decides the grade. Whatever you choose, make the total exactly 100 percent, as the checker does, and show the grade scale so students can work out where they stand. A rubric for each major assignment makes the marking transparent.

Policies that prevent problems

  • Late work. State the penalty, the limit and how to ask for an extension.
  • Attendance. Say whether it is recorded and how absences are handled.
  • Generative AI. Say what is allowed, how to disclose it, and who is responsible for accuracy. Specific assignment sheets can override the course default.
  • Academic integrity. Define plagiarism and refer to the institution's procedure.
  • Accessibility. Welcome accommodation requests and point to the disability service.
  • Support. Name counselling, tutoring and basic needs services.

Using the dated schedule and calendar export

Once you enter a start date, every week shows its date. Weeks marked No class are labelled and skipped. The calendar file adds an event for each class session on the days you choose, at the times you set, and an all-day event for each due date. Import it into Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar, and share the file with students or a teaching assistant. Check the dates against your institution's academic calendar, because holidays and exam weeks vary.

Tips for a syllabus students will read

  • Put the most-used information, meeting times, contact details and grading, on the first page.
  • Use plain language and short paragraphs.
  • Keep policies fair and explain the reason for important ones.
  • Use a table for the schedule so it prints on one page.
  • Review it with students in the first class and invite questions.
  • Tell students how and when changes will be announced.

Frequently asked questions

What is a syllabus?

A document that tells students what a course covers, how it runs, how it is assessed and what the rules are. Many institutions treat it as a form of agreement between teacher and students.

What should a syllabus include?

Course details, instructor contact and office hours, description, learning outcomes, materials, a weekly schedule, assessment and grading, policies and support information.

How do I create a syllabus with this tool?

Fill in the course tab, set a start date and weeks in the schedule tab, enter grading components and policies, then check the preview and copy, download or print.

How are the weekly dates worked out?

Enter the first week's start date. Each week is seven days later, and the date shown on every row updates automatically.

Can I mark holidays or weeks with no class?

Yes. Tick No class on any week. It stays in the dates, is labelled in the schedule and is skipped in the calendar file.

How does the grading check work?

Enter each component and its weight, such as Midterm exam, 30. The tool totals the weights, shows a coloured breakdown bar and tells you if you are over or under 100 percent.

Can I set a grade scale?

Yes. Type letters and minimum percentages, such as A:90, B:80, C:70, D:60. The scale appears in the syllabus.

What late work options are available?

No late work, 10 percent per day, grace passes, or a flexible ask-first approach. Choose the one that suits your course and edit the text if needed.

What should a generative AI policy say?

It should say whether tools are allowed, for what, how use must be disclosed and who is responsible for accuracy. Choose a starting option and adapt it to each assignment.

Is the accessibility statement enough?

It is a general starting statement. Many institutions require specific wording and contact details, so replace or add to it with your institution's official text.

Can I export a calendar?

Yes. Download an ICS file with your class sessions and due dates, then import it into Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar.

Can I download the syllabus as a Word file?

Yes. Download a Word-compatible file that opens in Word and Google Docs, or copy the text.

What does printing include?

Only the syllabus and the grading chart. The rest of the page is hidden.

Is my syllabus saved?

It is saved automatically in your browser, so you can return to it. Nothing is uploaded.

How long should a syllabus be?

Long enough to be complete but short enough to be read. Many are two to five pages. Use clear headings and a one-page schedule so students can find things.

Can I use it for school as well as university?

Yes. Rename the fields to suit, for example unit or course outline, and use the policies that match your school.

Is the syllabus legally binding?

This tool makes no legal claim. Your institution's rules decide how a syllabus is used, so check them and keep your own copy.
