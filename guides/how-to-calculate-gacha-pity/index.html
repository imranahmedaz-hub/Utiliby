What pity is and the numbers you need

Most gacha games hide a safety net behind their random pulls. Pity is a counter of how many pulls you have made since your last top-rarity item (usually a 5-star). The counter changes the odds in two stages, and one extra rule decides whether the item you get is the one you wanted.

  • Base rate: the chance of a 5-star on each early pull. A common value is 0.6%.
  • Soft pity: the pull where the chance per pull starts to climb. On a typical 90-pity character banner this is around pull 74.
  • Hard pity: the pull where a 5-star is guaranteed, which is pull 90 on that same banner.
  • The 50/50 and the guarantee: when you get a 5-star on a limited banner, there is a fixed chance (often 50%) that it is the featured one. If you lose, the next 5-star is guaranteed to be the featured one.

The values above are the defaults of the Gacha Pity Calculator. They match the basic structure used by several popular games, but rates and rules change, so always confirm the real numbers on your game's in-game drop-rate page and enter them into the calculator if they differ.

Two things stand out. Tip: Look at your pity counter before you decide how many pulls to make. Pulls made from pity 0 to 73 each have the same low chance, so what matters is how many pulls you can afford once you reach the soft-pity range.

The 50/50 and the guarantee

Getting a 5-star is only half the story on a limited banner. If the featured chance is 50%, then about half of your first 5-stars will be the wrong one, and you will need a second 5-star (guaranteed to be featured) to finish. The guarantee is why the featured character costs far more than the first 5-star.

The worst case with no guarantee is 180 pulls: 90 pulls to reach hard pity and lose the 50/50, then 90 more. Even so, the 99% line is 161 pulls, not 180, because reaching the worst case needs both an unlucky first 5-star and a late second one.

How to calculate your odds, step by step

Step 1: Count your total pulls

Add your tickets, your currency divided by the cost per pull, and any free pulls you expect before the banner ends. For example, 16,000 currency at 160 per pull is 100 pulls.

Step 2: Note your current pity

Check your pull history for the number of pulls since your last 5-star on this banner type. If your pity is already 50, you are 24 pulls away from soft pity.

Step 3: Check whether you have the guarantee

If your last 5-star on this banner type was not the featured one, your next 5-star is guaranteed to be featured. This single fact changes your odds more than almost anything else.

Step 4: Enter the banner rates

Choose a preset in the Gacha Pity Calculator or type the base rate, soft pity start, increase per pull, hard pity and featured chance from your game's rate page.

Step 5: Read the chance and the thresholds

The calculator shows your chance of the featured item within your pulls and the number of pulls needed for 50%, 90% and 99%. Compare your total from Step 1 with those numbers.

Step 6: Decide how much risk you accept

If your chance is already above 90%, you are in a safe position. If it is near 50%, you are close to a coin flip. If it is low, consider whether to save for a later banner instead of spending now.

Worked examples

Example 1: starting from nothing You have 60 pulls, zero pity and no guarantee. Your chance of the featured character is about 17.7%. That is low because 60 pulls is still before soft pity, and even a lucky 5-star is only featured half the time.
Example 2: a full 160 pulls You have 160 pulls (25,600 currency), zero pity and no guarantee. Your chance is about 98.5%. This is close to the 99% line of 161 pulls.
Example 3: pity 60, no guarantee, 40 pulls Your pity counter is 60, so soft pity is only 14 pulls away. With 40 pulls your chance of the featured character is about 56.5%. You are likely to see a 5-star, but you could lose the 50/50.
Example 4: pity 50 with the guarantee Your pity counter is 50 and the next 5-star is guaranteed to be featured. With 30 pulls your chance is about 89%. With 35 pulls it is about 99.9%, and with 40 pulls it is 100%, because pull 90 on your counter is hard pity.

How banners differ between games

Not every banner follows the 90-pity character pattern. Weapon banners, light cone or engine banners and some other games use shorter pity and different featured chances. Some games add rules the calculator does not model: a stronger guarantee after several lost 50/50s, shared pity between banners, a separate counter for secondary items, or bonus items at milestone pulls. If your game has a rule like that, your real odds will differ from the calculated ones, so read the game's rate page first.

Common probability mistakes

  • Treating the average as a promise. The average cost is 93.4 pulls, but you might need 160 or more. Plan for a high-confidence number, not the average.
  • Confusing "any 5-star" with the featured one. A 5-star by pull 90 is certain. The featured character is not.
  • Thinking you are "due" before soft pity. In this model the rate stays at 0.6% until pull 74. Pulls 20 and 50 are not more likely to hit than pull 2.
  • Forgetting the guarantee. Check whether your last 5-star was the featured one before you do any sums.
  • Mixing banner types. Pity on a character banner may not apply to a weapon banner. Check each banner on its own.
  • Assuming the rates never change. Games adjust rates and rules. Use the numbers from the current banner.

Spending responsibly

Pull odds are probabilities, not promises, and the cost can be well above the average. Set a budget before you start, avoid chasing losses and remember that you only reach the guaranteed outcome by spending the full amount shown. If you are worried about your spending, use spending limits on your account or device and talk to someone you trust. This guide and the calculator are for planning and entertainment only.

Frequently asked questions

What is pity in a gacha game?

Pity is a counter of pulls since your last top-rarity item. As the counter climbs, the game raises your chance (soft pity) and eventually guarantees the item (hard pity). Because the counter protects you from very long dry streaks, a gacha pity calculator uses it to work out your real odds.

What is the difference between soft pity and hard pity?

Soft pity is the point where the chance per pull starts to rise. Hard pity is the pull where the item is guaranteed. On a typical 90-pity character banner, soft pity begins around pull 74 and hard pity is pull 90. These are the values used by the calculator's default preset, so confirm the numbers for your game on its in-game rate page.

How many pulls do I need to be sure of the featured 5-star?

Under the standard 50/50 model, the worst case is 180 pulls: 90 to hit pity and lose the 50/50, then 90 more with the guarantee. For practical planning, about 155 pulls gives a 90% chance and about 161 pulls gives a 99% chance from zero pity with no guarantee. If the guarantee is already active, about 80 pulls gives a 90% chance.

Does a 50% chance mean 50 pulls?

No. From zero pity with no guarantee, the chance of the featured character reaches 50% at about 80 pulls, not 50. The chance of any top-rarity item reaches 50% at about 76 pulls. The two numbers differ because the first top-rarity item you get is only the featured one about half the time.

How do I calculate my chance of getting the featured character?

Add up your pulls (tickets plus currency divided by the cost per pull plus any free pulls), note your current pity and whether your next 5-star is guaranteed, then enter those with the banner's rates into a gacha pity calculator. It returns your chance within those pulls and the pulls needed for 50%, 90% and 99%.

Is a 0.6% rate really one in 167 pulls?

On the base rate alone, the average wait would be about 167 pulls. Soft pity changes that: under the 90-pity model the average wait for any 5-star is about 62 pulls, because most 5-stars arrive between pulls 74 and 90.

Does pity carry over between banners?

It depends on the game and the banner type. Many games carry pity across banners of the same type, and some reset it or keep a separate counter for weapons or other banners. The calculator does not assume anything about your account, so enter your current pity and guarantee status yourself and check the rules in the game.

How many pulls should I save for a character banner?

A common plan is to save enough for the number of pulls that gives you a 90% chance. On a 90-pity banner with 160 currency per pull, that is about 155 pulls (24,800 currency) from zero pity with no guarantee, or about 80 pulls (12,800 currency) if the guarantee is active.

Are the calculator results exact?

They are exact for the rates and rules you enter, but they are only as accurate as those inputs. Games change rates and add extra rules, so treat the results as planning estimates and check the game's own drop-rate page.

Can I use the calculator for games other than Genshin-style banners?

Yes. The base rate, soft pity, hard pity and featured chance are all editable, so any game with a soft pity and hard pity structure can be modelled. Mechanics that the calculator does not model, such as shared pity, bonus rules after several lost 50/50s or milestone rewards, will make real results differ.
